## SOL.2. A weak local cumulant estimate

The next estimate is deliberately much weaker than (S1). It is needed
only for normalized frequencies \(|y|\le4\).

### Lemma SOL.2

For \(m\ge700\), \(4/m<\lambda\le0.89\), and \(|y|\le4\),
\[
\log\Phi(y/\sigma)
 =-\frac{y^2}{2}+i\alpha y^3+R(y),                       \tag{SOL.7}
\]
where
\[
|\alpha|\le 0.0298,
\qquad
|R(y)|\le0.0021\,y^4.                                   \tag{SOL.8}
\]

### Proof

For one tilted uniform variable of length \(j\), its variance and third
cumulant are
\[
v_j(\lambda)
 =\frac14\left(
 \operatorname{csch}^2\frac\lambda2
 -j^2\operatorname{csch}^2\frac{j\lambda}{2}\right),     \tag{SOL.9}
\]
and
\[
\kappa_{3,j}(\lambda)
 =\frac14\left(
 \operatorname{csch}^2\frac\lambda2\coth\frac\lambda2
 -j^3\operatorname{csch}^2\frac{j\lambda}{2}
                \coth\frac{j\lambda}{2}\right).          \tag{SOL.10}
\]

Differentiation of
\[
\log\frac{1-e^{-j\lambda}}{1-e^{-\lambda}}
\]
gives these identities directly. Summing (SOL.9)–(SOL.10), and using
the elementary hyperbolic inequalities
\[
0<\frac{x}{\sinh x}\le1,\qquad
0<x\coth x-1\le\frac{x^2}{3}\frac1{1-x^2/15}
\quad(0\le x\le0.445),                                   \tag{SOL.11}
\]
together with the geometric-series representation for the terms with
\(j\lambda/2>0.445\), gives
\[
\lambda|\kappa_3|\le\frac52s_2.                          \tag{SOL.12}
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{|\kappa_3|}{6\sigma^3}
 \le\frac{5}{12\lambda\sigma}
 =\frac5{12\sqrt A}
 \le\frac5{168}
 <0.0298.                                                \tag{SOL.13}
\]

For completeness, the same termwise calculation applied from order four
onwards gives, whenever
\[
|t|/\lambda\le\frac27,
\]
the Taylor remainder bound
\[
\left|
 \log\Phi(t)+\frac{s_2t^2}{2}
             -\frac{i\kappa_3t^3}{6}
\right|
 \le 0.0021\,\sigma^4t^4.                               \tag{SOL.14}
\]
Indeed, after inserting the exact derivatives of
\(\log(1-e^{-x})\), the left side divided by \(\sigma^4t^4\) is bounded by
\[
\frac{1}{24A}
 \left(
  6.72+\frac{0.72\,|t|/\lambda}{1-|t|/\lambda}
 \right).                                                \tag{SOL.15}
\]
Here \(A\ge196\) and \(|t|/\lambda=|y|/\sqrt A\le4/14=2/7\), so
\[
\frac{1}{24A}
 \left(6.72+\frac{0.72(2/7)}{1-2/7}\right)
 =
 \frac{7.008}{4704}
 <0.00149<0.0021.                                       \tag{SOL.16}
\]
The stated \(0.0021\) leaves more than \(40\%\) slack for the endpoint
rounding in (SOL.11).

Taking \(t=y/\sigma\) proves (SOL.7)–(SOL.8). ∎

## SOL.4. A \(C^2\) local limit estimate

Define the real Fourier interpolation

## SOL.5. Curvature of the Fourier interpolation

### Lemma SOL.5

For every \(|z|\le\varepsilon\), \(f(z)>0\), and
\[
0.607<
-\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\log f(z)
<1.535.                                                   \tag{SOL.37}
\]

### Proof

From (SOL.3), \(|z|\le0.06358\). Therefore
\[
g(z)>0.39813,\qquad
|g'(z)|<0.02532,                                         \tag{SOL.38}
\]
and
\[
0.39652<-g''(z)<0.39895.                                 \tag{SOL.39}
\]

Lemmas SOL.4 and (SOL.38)–(SOL.39) imply
\[
0.35813<f(z)<0.43895,                                    \tag{SOL.40}
\]
\[
|f'(z)|<0.08532,                                         \tag{SOL.41}
\]
and
\[
0.26652<-f''(z)<0.52895.                                 \tag{SOL.42}
\]
In particular \(f(z)>0\).

Now
\[
-\bigl(\log f\bigr)''
 =-\frac{f''}{f}+\left(\frac{f'}f\right)^2.               \tag{SOL.43}
\]
Hence
\[
-\bigl(\log f\bigr)''
 >
 \frac{0.26652}{0.43895}
 >0.607,                                                  \tag{SOL.44}
\]
while
\[
-\bigl(\log f\bigr)''
 <
 \frac{0.52895}{0.35813}
 +\left(\frac{0.08532}{0.35813}\right)^2
 <1.535.                                                  \tag{SOL.45}
\]
This proves (SOL.37). ∎

## SOL.6. Passage from continuous to lattice curvature

Put \(\ell(z)=\log f(z)\). By (SOL.20) and tilt invariance (SOL.0),
\[
\log r_m(k)
 =2\ell(0)-\ell(-\varepsilon)-\ell(\varepsilon).          \tag{SOL.46}
\]
The exact symmetric-difference identity is
\[
2\ell(0)-\ell(-\varepsilon)-\ell(\varepsilon)
 =
 \int_0^\varepsilon(\varepsilon-t)
 \bigl[-\ell''(t)-\ell''(-t)\bigr]\,dt.                  \tag{SOL.47}
\]
Since
\[
2\int_0^\varepsilon(\varepsilon-t)\,dt=\varepsilon^2,
\]
Lemma SOL.5 gives
\[
\frac{0.607}{\sigma^2}
 <\log r_m(k)
 <\frac{1.535}{\sigma^2}.                                \tag{SOL.48}
\]

Set
\[
L=\sigma^2\log r_m(k).
\]
Then
\[
0.607<L<1.535.                                           \tag{SOL.49}
\]

Since \(r_m(k)=e^{L/\sigma^2}\),
\[
\sigma^2(r_m(k)-1)
 =\sigma^2\left(e^{L/\sigma^2}-1\right).                 \tag{SOL.50}
\]
For \(x\ge0\),
\[
x\le e^x-1\le xe^x.
\]
Thus, using \(\sigma^2>247.44\),
\[
\sigma^2(r_m(k)-1)>L>0.607,                              \tag{SOL.51}
\]
and
\[
\sigma^2(r_m(k)-1)
 <1.535\exp\!\left(\frac{1.535}{247.44}\right)
 <1.545.                                                  \tag{SOL.52}
\]

We have proved:
