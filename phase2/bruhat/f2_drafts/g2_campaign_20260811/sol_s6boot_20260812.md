## SOL.4. Branchwise convex two-endpoint closure

Let \(0<a<b\), and let \(G_1,\ldots,G_N:[a,b]\to\mathbb R\) be convex. Set

\[
G(t):=\max_{1\le j\le N}G_j(t).
\]

Assume, branch by branch,

\[
G_j(a)<a,\qquad G_j(b)<b.
\]

Then

\[
G(t)<t\qquad(a\le t\le b).
\]

Consequently, if \(x\le b\) and

\[
x\in[a,b]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\le G(x),
\]

then \(x\le a\).

### Proof

A finite maximum of convex functions is convex. For

\[
t=(1-\theta)a+\theta b,\qquad 0\le\theta\le1,
\]

convexity gives

\[
G(t)
 \le(1-\theta)G(a)+\theta G(b)
 <(1-\theta)a+\theta b
 =t.
\]

If \(x>a\), then \(x\in(a,b]\), and hence

\[
x\le G(x)<x,
\]

a contradiction. ∎

This proves that checking every exact branch at both endpoints is sufficient. Monotonicity of \(G\) is not needed.

## SOL.6. A useful analytic convexity criterion for inflation branches

Suppose an exact branch has the form

\[
G(t)=C(t)+F_{A,B}(Q(t)),
\]

where \(C\) is convex,

\[
F_{A,B}(q)
 :=(1+q)\left(A+\frac{B}{1-q}\right),
\qquad A,B\ge0,
\]

and \(Q\) is convex and nondecreasing with \(Q(t)<1\).

Then \(G\) is convex.

### Proof

For \(q<1\),

\[
F_{A,B}'(q)
 =A+\frac{2B}{(1-q)^2}\ge0,
\]

and

\[
F_{A,B}''(q)
 =\frac{4B}{(1-q)^3}\ge0.
\]

Thus \(F_{A,B}\) is convex and nondecreasing. The composition of a convex nondecreasing function with a convex function is convex. Adding the convex function \(C\) preserves convexity. ∎
