## SOL.1. Exact cumulant representation

For \(\lambda>0\), the tilted Mahonian law is the convolution of the independent laws

\[
\Pr_\lambda(X_j=a)
=\frac{e^{-\lambda a}}{1+e^{-\lambda}+\cdots+e^{-(j-1)\lambda}},
\qquad 0\le a\le j-1,\quad 1\le j\le m.
\]

Put

\[
g_n(x)=\sum_{\nu\ge1}\nu^{n-1}e^{-\nu x},\qquad x>0.
\]

Explicitly,

\[
g_2(x)=\frac{e^{-x}}{(1-e^{-x})^2},
\]

\[
g_3(x)=\frac{e^{-x}(1+e^{-x})}{(1-e^{-x})^3},
\]

\[
g_4(x)=
\frac{e^{-x}(1+4e^{-x}+e^{-2x})}{(1-e^{-x})^4}.
\]

Define the regularized functions

\[
h_n(x)=x^n g_n(x),
\]

with removable values

\[
h_2(0)=1,\qquad h_3(0)=2,\qquad h_4(0)=6.
\]

Then

\[
s_2=\kappa _2
=m g_2(\lambda)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}j^2g_2(j\lambda),
\tag{1}
\]

\[
\kappa _3
=m g_3(\lambda)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}j^3g_3(j\lambda),
\tag{2}
\]

\[
\kappa _4
=m g_4(\lambda)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}j^4g_4(j\lambda).
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently, setting \(w=m\lambda\),

\[
D_n(m,\lambda)
:=w h_n(\lambda)-\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_n(j\lambda),
\tag{4}
\]

we have

\[
D_2=\lambda^3s_2,\qquad
D_3=\lambda^4\kappa _3,\qquad
D_4=\lambda^5\kappa _4.
\tag{5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{\lambda|\kappa _3|}{s_2}
=\frac{|D_3|}{D_2},
\qquad
\frac{\lambda^2\kappa _4}{s_2}
=\frac{D_4}{D_2}.
\tag{6}
\]

### Proof

The tilted partition function of \(X_j\) is

\[
Z_j(\lambda)=\frac{1-e^{-j\lambda}}{1-e^{-\lambda}}.
\]

The \(n\)-th cumulant is

\[
(-1)^n\frac{d^n}{d\lambda^n}\log Z_j(\lambda)
=g_n(\lambda)-j^ng_n(j\lambda).
\]

Summing over \(j\) gives (1)–(3), and multiplication by the indicated powers of \(\lambda\) gives (4)–(6). ∎

## SOL.2. Positivity of the third cumulant

For every \(x>0\),

\[
h_2'(x)<0,\qquad h_3'(x)<0.
\tag{7}
\]

Hence for every \(j\ge2\),

\[
h_2(\lambda)-h_2(j\lambda)>0,
\qquad
h_3(\lambda)-h_3(j\lambda)>0.
\]

In particular,

\[
D_2>0,\qquad D_3\ge0,
\tag{8}
\]

so the absolute value in the third-cumulant ratio may be removed for \(\lambda>0\).

### Proof

First,

\[
h_2(x)=\left(\frac{x}{2\sinh(x/2)}\right)^2,
\]

which is strictly decreasing because \(2\sinh(x/2)>x\) and
\(x\mapsto \sinh(x/2)/(x/2)\) is strictly increasing.

Also

\[
h_3(x)=\frac{x^3\cosh(x/2)}{4\sinh^3(x/2)}.
\]

Its logarithmic derivative is

\[
\frac{h_3'(x)}{h_3(x)}
=\frac3x-\coth(x/2)-\frac1{\sinh x}.
\]

Thus \(h_3'(x)<0\) is equivalent to

\[
x(\cosh x+2)>3\sinh x.
\]

Let

\[
F(x)=x\cosh x+2x-3\sinh x.
\]

Then \(F(0)=F'(0)=0\), while

\[
F''(x)=x\cosh x-\sinh x>0
\]

because its derivative is \(x\sinh x>0\). Hence \(F(x)>0\) for \(x>0\). This proves (7), and (8) follows termwise from (4). ∎

## SOL.3. Geometric-envelope inequalities

Define

\[
a(x)=\frac{h_3(x)}{h_2(x)}
=x\coth(x/2),
\tag{9}
\]

and

\[
b(x)=\frac{h_4(x)}{h_2(x)}
=x^2\left(1+\frac{3}{2\sinh^2(x/2)}\right)
=x^2+6h_2(x).
\tag{10}
\]

Both \(a\) and \(b\) are strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\). Consequently,

\[
0\le \frac{D_3}{D_2}\le a(\lambda),
\qquad
\frac{D_4}{D_2}\le b(\lambda).
\tag{11}
\]

### Proof

For \(a\),

\[
a'(x)=\coth(x/2)-\frac{x}{2}\operatorname{csch}^2(x/2)>0
\]

is equivalent to \(\sinh x>x\).

For \(b\), writing \(y=x/2\),

\[
b'(x)
=x\operatorname{csch}^2y
\left(2\sinh^2y+3-3y\coth y\right).
\]

The bracket is positive for \(y>0\); after multiplication by \(\sinh y\cosh y\), two differentiations reduce the assertion to \(\sinh(2y)>2y\).

Since \(a(j\lambda)\ge a(\lambda)\),

\[
h_3(\lambda)-h_3(j\lambda)
\le
a(\lambda)\bigl(h_2(\lambda)-h_2(j\lambda)\bigr).
\]

Summing over \(j\) proves the first inequality in (11). The same argument with \(b\) proves the second:

\[
h_4(\lambda)-h_4(j\lambda)
\le
b(\lambda)\bigl(h_2(\lambda)-h_2(j\lambda)\bigr).
\]

No positivity assumption on \(h_4(\lambda)-h_4(j\lambda)\) is required. ∎

## SOL.4. Exact integral formula for the continuum terms

For \(n=2,3,4\), define

\[
G_n(w)=(n-1)!\,w-\int_0^w h_n(x)\,dx.
\tag{12}
\]

Then

\[
G_n(w)
=(n-1)!\,w-n!\zeta(2)
+n!\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}
\frac{e^{-\nu w}}{\nu^2}
E_n(\nu w),
\tag{13}
\]

where

\[
E_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{x^k}{k!}.
\]

### Proof

Since

\[
h_n(x)=x^n\sum_{\nu\ge1}\nu^{n-1}e^{-\nu x},
\]

termwise integration gives

\[
\int_0^w h_n(x)\,dx
=n!\sum_{\nu\ge1}\frac{1}{\nu^2}
\left(1-e^{-\nu w}E_n(\nu w)\right).
\]

Using \(\sum_{\nu\ge1}\nu^{-2}=\zeta(2)\) proves (13). ∎
