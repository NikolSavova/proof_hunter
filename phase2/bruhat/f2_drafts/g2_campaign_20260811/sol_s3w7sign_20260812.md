## Lemma SOL.1 — the global hyperbolic inequality

For every \(y>0\),
\[
3\left(\coth y-\frac1y\right)-\tanh y>0.
\]

### Proof

Define
\[
F(t):=t\cosh t+2t-3\sinh t.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
F(0)=F'(0)=F''(0)=0,\qquad F'''(t)=t\sinh t.
\]
Hence, for \(t>0\),
\[
F(t)=\frac12\int_0^t(t-u)^2u\sinh u\,du>0.
\]

On the other hand,
\[
3\coth y-\tanh y
 =\frac{3\cosh^2y-\sinh^2y}{\sinh y\cosh y}
 =\frac{2(\cosh 2y+2)}{\sinh 2y}.
\]
Consequently
\[
3\left(\coth y-\frac1y\right)-\tanh y
 =\frac{F(2y)}{y\sinh(2y)}>0.
\]
All denominators are positive for \(y>0\). ∎

\[

When \(m\ge2\), at least the \(j=2\) inequality is strict. ∎

## Lemma SOL.5 — disappearance of the \(B_8\) endpoint term on the half-line

For \(n=2,3,4\),
\[
h_n^{(7)}(0)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{x\to\infty}h_n^{(7)}(x)=0.
\]
Hence the \(B_8\) endpoint term in the half-line application is exactly
zero:
\[
\boxed{
\frac{B_8}{8!}\lambda^8
\bigl(h_n^{(7)}(\infty)-h_n^{(7)}(0)\bigr)=0.
}
\tag{SOL.13$'$}
\]

### Proof

Let
\[
S(x):=\frac{\sinh(x/2)}{x/2},\qquad S(0):=1.
\]
The hyperbolic forms are
\[
h_2(x)=S(x)^{-2},
\]
\[
h_3(x)=2\cosh(x/2)\,S(x)^{-3},
\]
\[
h_4(x)=2(\cosh x+2)\,S(x)^{-4}.
\]
Each right side is an even real-analytic function near \(x=0\).
Therefore every odd derivative at zero vanishes, in particular
\[
h_n^{(7)}(0)=0.
\]

For the endpoint at infinity, termwise differentiation is justified
uniformly on every interval \([a,\infty)\), \(a>0\). For \(p=7,8\) and
\(x\ge1\), Leibniz's rule gives
\[
|h_n^{(p)}(x)|
 \le C_{n,p}\,x^n e^{-x},
\]
where the explicit constant
\[
C_{n,p}:=
\sum_{r=0}^{n}
 \binom pr\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}
 \frac{(n+p-1-r)!}
 {(1-e^{-1})^{\,n+p-r}}
\]
is finite. Indeed,
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}k^s e^{-(k-1)}
 \le\frac{s!}{(1-e^{-1})^{s+1}}.
\]
Since \(x^ne^{-x}\to0\), this proves \(h_n^{(7)}(x)\to0\).

More precisely, the \(k=1\) term gives
\[
\lim_{x\to\infty}
 \frac{e^x h_n^{(7)}(x)}{x^n}=-1.
\]
Thus \(h_n^{(7)}(w)\) is not identically zero at finite \(w\); indeed it
is negative for all sufficiently large finite \(w\). The half-line
endpoint cancellation therefore does not justify use of the old
finite-\(w\) remainder constant. ∎
