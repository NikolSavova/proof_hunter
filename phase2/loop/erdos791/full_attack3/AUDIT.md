## P1 issues and exact repairs

### P1.1: the displayed amplifier proof needs bounded-gap interpolation

`automaton/AUTOMATON_RESULT.md` says that letting `t` tend to infinity proves

```text
liminf R(k)/k^2 >= m/L^2.
```

A construction on a subsequence alone proves only a limsup statement.  Here
the liminf conclusion is nevertheless correct because the admissible scales
contain a bounded-gap progression.  Insert the following argument.

For the general fixed-`r` family take sufficiently large `t congruent 1
(mod M)`; for the optimized family take sufficiently large `t` coprime to
`30`.  These sets have gaps bounded in terms of fixed `r`.  Given arbitrary
large `k`, choose the largest such `t` with

```text
L(t+2C_r) <= k.
```

Then `t=k/L+O_{r,L}(1)`.  The amplifier has at most `k` marks and range at
least `mt^2-1`, so

```text
R(k)/k^2 >= (mt^2-1)/k^2 -> m/L^2.
```

The definition in `FULL_ATTACK3_RESULT.md` uses `|A|<=k`, so padding is not
needed; under an exactly-`k` convention, add unused marks.  This proves the
claimed liminf rather than merely the subsequential bound.

### P1.2: the synthesis compresses away the kernel-index step

`FULL_ATTACK3_RESULT.md` lists two kernel vectors of determinant `B=t^2` and
then calls them a kernel basis.  Determinant `B` is not by itself sufficient:
one must first know that the homomorphism is onto, hence that its kernel has
index `B`.  The complete argument is present in
`automaton/AUTOMATON_RESULT.md`, but the synthesis should retain this sentence:

> Since `t congruent 1 (mod M)`, `gcd(t,a)=1` for every nonzero slope.  Thus
> at least one of `t+a,t+b` is a unit modulo `t^2` (if `a=0`, use `t+b`), so
> `phi` is onto and its kernel has index `B`.  The sublattice generated by
> `p,q` lies in the kernel and also has index `|det(p,q)|=B`; hence it is the
> full kernel.

This is a proof-completeness issue in the short synthesis, not a failure of
the detailed theorem.

## Theorem-by-theorem audit

### 1. General unbounded `K_r` family: PASS after the recorded repairs

For

```text
M=lcm(1,...,r-1),  D_r={0,M,...,(r-1)M},
```

write `a=iM<b=jM`.  Then `(b-a)=(j-i)M` divides `ab=ijM^2`, because
`j-i` divides `M`.  Consequently `u=a^2/(b-a)`, `v=b^2/(b-a)`, and
`h=ab/(b-a)` are integers.

For the map

```text
phi(x,y)=x(t+a)+y(t+b) mod t^2,
```

direct expansion independently gives

```text
p=(t-h,u), q=(-v,t-b+v),
phi(p)=phi(q)=0 mod t^2, det(p,q)=t^2.
```

The onto/index argument in P1.2 makes these a full kernel basis.  If
`t>max h`, both relevant parallelogram directions have the signs used in the
coordinate bounds.  A half-open fundamental representative satisfies

```text
-v <= x < t-h,
0 <= y < t-b+u+v.
```

For integer `y`, the upper endpoint is
`y<=t-b+u+v-1`; therefore the exact phase radius is

```text
C_r=max_{a<b} max(v,u+v-b),
```

with no extra `+1`.  The current `unbounded_cliques.py` and regenerated JSON
use precisely this bound and require `t>max h`.  The older vulnerable form
(`t>max slope` and a radius off by one) is no longer present.

For fixed `r`, sufficiently large `t congruent 1 (mod M)` also gives
`gcd(t+a,t^2)=1` for nonzero `a` and `t+2C_r<t^2`.  Hence every nonzero tile
has exactly `t+2C_r` residues, while `R_0` has `t`; all are `t+O_r(1)`.
The crude stated estimate `C_r<=2(r-1)^2M` is safe.  The result is for every
fixed `r`; it does not justify taking `r=r(t)` without controlling `C_r`.

Independent rerun: symbolic audits pass for `2<=r<=8`, and literal modular
coverage passes for `2<=r<=5`.

### 3. Carry transition and macro amplifier: PASS, with strict scope

Modular coverage gives only

```text

```

and therefore the self-loop `e->e`.  It does not give `e->f` for arbitrary
pair states.  The macro predicate correctly requires both `s-1` and `s` in
the *same* role-pair sumset.  Shifting `U_e` from macro sum `s-1` and `L_e`
from macro sum `s` literally covers block `s`; the `V+R_0=[0,t^2]` bootstrap
is also exact.  The size bound follows by a union bound over role placements.

This distinction is now stated accurately.  At `t=251` I independently read

### 4. Projective three-tile carry family: PASS

The identities `X+Y=Y+Z=[0,B-1]` and `X+Z mod B=Z/BZ` check exactly.  For
`q>=4` the unphased transition graph is

```text
XY -> XY,YZ;  YZ -> XY,YZ;  XZ -> XY,YZ,XZ.
```

The lower `XZ` footprint omits `2`, so a direct state cannot enter `XZ`.
The phase search proves only its finite scopes (`q=4` can realize all nine
transitions, while `q=5,6` attain at most seven), as the text says.

### 5. Analytic compactness/Fourier theorem: PASS

The pair-accounting identity is correct and converts near-minimal pair budget
plus near coverage into total-variation convergence to a translated uniform
interval law.  The three pair-sum centers can be simultaneously removed.
Tightness of every marginal follows from
`2X_1=S_12+S_13-S_23` and its analogues.  Subsequence limits have all three
pair convolutions equal to `Unif[-1/2,1/2]`; the same identity makes the
limits compactly supported.  Their characteristic functions are entire and,
near zero, division forces all three to agree.  The resulting entire identity

```text
f(z)^2=2 sin(z/2)/z
```

is impossible because the right side has simple nonzero zeros.  No bounded
diameter or moment assumption is hidden here.

The adversarial projective family is not a counterexample: its lower raw
`X+Z` window has `binom(q-2,2)` holes and the best translated `B`-window has
`floor((q-2)^2/4)` holes, both a positive asymptotic fraction.  Its escape is
carry splitting, outside the theorem's one-raw-pair hypothesis.  The finite
checker passes all identities through `q=100`.

The rank-one absorber and its conditional closure theorem are also correct;
the unproved content is explicitly the existence of an `o(k)`-cost additive
rectangle containing the global residual set.
