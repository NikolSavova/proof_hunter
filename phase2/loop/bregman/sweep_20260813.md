## 1. Assessment of the candidate construction

Your example appears to meet the survey’s hypotheses other than full domain:

- \(f(x)=\sum_i(x_i\log x_i-x_i)\) has  
  \(\operatorname{dom}f=\mathbb R_+^2\neq\mathbb R^2\),
  \(U=\mathbb R_{++}^2\), and
  \(U^*=\mathbb R^2\).
- \(C\) is compact in \(U\).
- \(C^*=\{(t,-t^2):1\leq t\leq2\}\) is compact, so \(\overline{C^*}\subset U^*\).
- For every \(x\in U\), minimization over \(C\) reduces to a strictly convex scalar function on \([1,2]\).
- \(C^*\) is nonconvex: for example, the midpoint of \((1,-1)\) and \((2,-4)\) is \((3/2,-5/2)\), not \((3/2,-9/4)\).

Thus it appears to be an explicit negative answer to the full-domain part of Problem 2.

The survey itself only says that its Example 3.3 shows that the conclusion \(C^*\) convex **can** hold without full domain; it then asks whether full domain is necessary and separately asks for a characterization of right-Chebyshev sets for negative entropy. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.3127))

### Novelty should be stated narrowly

Do **not** claim:

- the first nonconvex right KL-Chebyshev set—the survey already has such sets;
- the first nonconvex entropy-related Bregman projection example—Laude–Ochs–Cremers have a local one.

The defensible claim is:

> An explicit globally right \(D_f\)-Chebyshev set for negative entropy whose gradient image is nonconvex, showing that the full-domain assumption in Fact 3.2 cannot be dropped even when \(\overline{C^*}\subset U^*\).
