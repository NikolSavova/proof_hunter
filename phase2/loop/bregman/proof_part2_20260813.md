## Lemma SOL.1 — Dual formulation and relative closedness

**Hypotheses.** Let \(f:X\to]-\infty,+\infty]\) be Legendre with \(\operatorname{dom}f=X\), and let \(C\subset X\) be closed and nonempty.

**Conclusion.**

1. \(U=X\), and \(\nabla f:X\to U^*\) and \(\nabla f^*:U^*\to X\) are mutually inverse homeomorphisms.
2. \(S=C^*\) is closed relative to \(U^*\).
3. For \(x\in X\) and \(y\in C\), with \(p=\nabla f(y)\),
   \[
   D_f(x,y)=f(x)+f^*(p)-\langle x,p\rangle.
   \]
4. Consequently, \(C\) is right \(D\)-Chebyshev if and only if, for every \(x\in X\),
   \[
   \operatorname*{argmin}_{p\in S}
   \big(f^*(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\big)
   \]
   exists and is a singleton.

**Proof.**

Legendre duality gives the homeomorphisms in item 1. If \(p_k\in S\) and \(p_k\to p\in U^*\), then
\[
y_k:=\nabla f^*(p_k)\in C
\quad\text{and}\quad
y_k\to \nabla f^*(p).
\]
Since \(C\) is closed,
\[
\nabla f^*(p)\in C,
\]
and hence \(p\in S\). This proves relative closedness.

If \(p=\nabla f(y)\), Fenchel equality gives
\[
f^*(p)=\langle p,y\rangle-f(y).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_f(x,y)
 &=f(x)-f(y)-\langle p,x-y\rangle\\
 &=f(x)+f^*(p)-\langle x,p\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(y\mapsto \nabla f(y)\) is bijective from \(C\) onto \(S\), item 4 follows. ∎

## Lemma SOL.2 — Quantitative coercivity supplied by \(\operatorname{dom}f=X\)

**Hypotheses.** Let \(f\) be proper, lower semicontinuous and convex with \(\operatorname{dom}f=X\). Put \(g=f^*\).

**Conclusion.** For every \(R\ge 0\), define
\[
A_R:=\max_{|z|=R+1} f(z)<+\infty.
\]
Then, for every \(x,p\in X\) with \(|x|\le R\),
\[
g(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\ge |p|-A_R.
\]
In particular, if
\[
g(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\le M,
\]
then
\[
|p|\le M+A_R.
\]

**Proof.**

For \(p\neq0\), choose
\[
z=(R+1)\frac{p}{|p|}.
\]
By the definition of the conjugate,
\[
g(p)\ge \langle z,p\rangle-f(z)
       \ge (R+1)|p|-A_R.
\]
Since \(|x|\le R\),
\[
\langle x,p\rangle\le R|p|.
\]
Hence
\[
g(p)-\langle x,p\rangle
\ge (R+1)|p|-A_R-R|p|
=|p|-A_R.
\]
For \(p=0\), the same inequality follows from \(g(0)\ge -A_R\). ∎

Thus \(f^*\), as an extended-real-valued function on \(X\), is supercoercive. Escape to Euclidean infinity is impossible for bounded families of tilts. The only missing compactness is escape to a finite point of \(\operatorname{bd}U^*\).

## Lemma SOL.3 — A lower-semicontinuous tilt-uniqueness theorem

**Hypotheses.** Let \(\Omega\subset X\) be nonempty, open and convex. Let \(g:X\to]-\infty,+\infty]\) be proper, lower semicontinuous and supercoercive, continuous and strictly convex on \(\Omega\). Let \(S\subset\Omega\) be nonempty, and define
\[
h:=g+\iota_S.
\]
Assume:

1. \(h\) is lower semicontinuous on \(X\);
2. for every \(x\in X\),
   \[
   \operatorname*{argmin}_{p\in X}
   \big(h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\big)
   \]
   is a singleton;
3. for every \(p\in S\), \(g\) is differentiable at \(p\).

**Conclusion.** \(S\) is convex.

**Proof.**

Let
\[
H:=h^{**},
\]
the lower semicontinuous convex envelope of \(h\). Fix \(x\in X\), and denote the unique minimizer of
\[
h_x(p):=h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle
\]
by \(p_x\), with minimum \(m_x\).

The affine function
\[
p\mapsto m_x+\langle x,p\rangle
\]
is bounded above by \(h\), and therefore by the maximal lower semicontinuous convex minorant \(H\). Hence
\[
H(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\ge m_x
\quad\text{for all }p.
\]
At \(p_x\),
\[
H(p_x)\le h(p_x),
\]
so equality holds:
\[
H(p_x)-\langle x,p_x\rangle=m_x.
\]

We next prove that \(p_x\) is the unique minimizer of \(H-\langle x,\cdot\rangle\). Define
\[
a_x(p):=h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle-m_x.
\]
Then \(a_x\ge0\), with unique zero \(p_x\).

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). Supercoercivity permits \(R_\varepsilon>|p_x|+\varepsilon\) such that
\[
a_x(p)\ge 1+|p-p_x|
\qquad\text{when }|p|\ge R_\varepsilon.
\]
On the compact set
\[
K_\varepsilon
 :=
 \{p:|p|\le R_\varepsilon,\ |p-p_x|\ge\varepsilon\},
\]
lower semicontinuity and uniqueness give
\[
\delta_\varepsilon
 :=
 \inf_{p\in K_\varepsilon}a_x(p)>0
\]
whenever \(K_\varepsilon\cap\operatorname{dom}h\neq\varnothing\). Put
\[
\kappa_\varepsilon
 :=
 \min\left\{
 1,\,
 \frac{\delta_\varepsilon}{1+R_\varepsilon+|p_x|}
 \right\}>0,
\]
with the second entry omitted when the compact set contains no finite point of \(h\). Then
\[
|p-p_x|\le \frac{a_x(p)}{\kappa_\varepsilon}
\quad\text{whenever }|p-p_x|\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{SOL.3.1}
\]

Suppose \(q\) minimizes \(H-\langle x,\cdot\rangle\). Since
\[
\operatorname{epi}H
 =\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(\operatorname{epi}h),
\]
there are finite convex combinations, indexed by \(k\),
\[
q_k=\sum_i\lambda_{ki}p_{ki}\to q,
\qquad
\sum_i\lambda_{ki}h(p_{ki})-\langle x,q_k\rangle\to m_x.
\]
Thus
\[
\sum_i\lambda_{ki}a_x(p_{ki})\to0.
\]
Using (SOL.3.1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|q_k-p_x|
&\le \sum_i\lambda_{ki}|p_{ki}-p_x|\\
&\le \varepsilon+
 \frac{1}{\kappa_\varepsilon}
 \sum_i\lambda_{ki}a_x(p_{ki}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Letting \(k\to\infty\) gives
\[
|q-p_x|\le\varepsilon.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon>0\) was arbitrary, \(q=p_x\).

We now identify \(S\). If \(p\in S\), choose
\[
x=\nabla g(p).
\]
Strict convexity gives, for every \(q\in\Omega\setminus\{p\}\),
\[
g(q)>g(p)+\langle \nabla g(p),q-p\rangle.
\]
Hence \(p\) is the unique minimizer of \(h-\langle x,\cdot\rangle\), and therefore also of \(H-\langle x,\cdot\rangle\). Thus
\[
x\in\partial H(p),
\]
so
\[
S\subset\operatorname{dom}\partial H.
\]
Conversely, if \(q\in\operatorname{dom}\partial H\), choose \(x\in\partial H(q)\). Then \(q\) minimizes \(H-\langle x,\cdot\rangle\). The preceding uniqueness result gives \(q=p_x\in S\). Therefore
\[
S=\operatorname{dom}\partial H.
\tag{SOL.3.2}
\]

Let \(A=\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{dom}H)\), and choose
\[
a\in\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}H).
\]
Finite-dimensional separation of the epigraph gives
\[
\partial H(a)\neq\varnothing,
\]
hence \(a\in S\) by (SOL.3.2).

Take \(p_0,p_1\in S\), \(t\in(0,1)\), and put
\[
r=(1-t)p_0+tp_1.
\]
Then \(r\in\operatorname{dom}H\). For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), put
\[
r_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)r+\varepsilon a.
\]
If
\[
B_A(a,\rho)\subset\operatorname{dom}H,
\]
then
\[
B_A(r_\varepsilon,\varepsilon\rho)
 \subset\operatorname{dom}H,
\]
because
\[
r_\varepsilon+\varepsilon u
 =(1-\varepsilon)r+\varepsilon(a+u).
\]
Thus \(r_\varepsilon\in\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}H)\), so
\[
r_\varepsilon\in S.
\]

Since \(r\in\Omega\), choose
\[
d=\tfrac12\operatorname{dist}(r,X\setminus\Omega)>0
\]
and
\[
L=\max_{|z-r|\le d}|\nabla g(z)|<+\infty.
\]
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\),
\[
|g(r_\varepsilon)-g(r)|
 \le L|r_\varepsilon-r|.
\]
Hence \(g(r_\varepsilon)\to g(r)<+\infty\). Since \(h\) is lower semicontinuous and \(h(r_\varepsilon)=g(r_\varepsilon)\),
\[
h(r)\le\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}h(r_\varepsilon)=g(r)<+\infty.
\]
Therefore \(r\in S\). Thus \(S\) is convex. ∎

## Lemma SOL.4 — A strict weakening of hypothesis (b)

**Hypotheses.** Assume (a) and (c), and let \(S=C^*\). Suppose
\[
h:=f^*+\iota_S
\]
is lower semicontinuous on \(X\).

**Conclusion.** \(C^*=S\) is convex.

**Proof.**

By Lemma SOL.2, \(f^*\) is supercoercive. By Legendre duality, \(f^*\) is continuous and strictly convex on \(U^*\). Lemma SOL.1 converts (c) into unique minimization of every linear tilt of \(h\). Lemma SOL.3 applies. ∎

A convenient sufficient condition for the lower semicontinuity in SOL.4 is:

\[
\boxed{
p_k\in C^*,\quad p_k\to\bar p\notin U^*
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f^*(p_k)\to+\infty.
}
\tag{SOL.4.1}
\]

Indeed, relative closedness handles limits in \(U^*\), while (SOL.4.1) handles limits outside \(U^*\).

This is strictly weaker than
\[
\overline{C^*}\subset U^*.
\]

For example, let
\[
g(p)=-\log(1-|p|^2),\qquad |p|<1,
\]
and \(g=+\infty\) otherwise. Put \(f=g^*\) and \(C=X\). Then:

- \(f\) is Legendre with \(\operatorname{dom}f=X\);
- \(C^*=U^*=B(0,1)\);
- \(\overline{C^*}\not\subset U^*\);
- \(g+\iota_{C^*}=g\) is lower semicontinuous because \(g(p)\to+\infty\) as \(|p|\uparrow1\);
- \(C\) is right \(D\)-Chebyshev;
- \(C^*\) is convex.

Thus the closure-inside condition is not needed in the barrier-boundary case.

## Lemma SOL.5 — Complete removal of (b) in dimension one

**Hypotheses.** Let \(n=1\). Assume (a), \(C\subset X\) is closed and nonempty, and \(C\) is right \(D\)-Chebyshev.

**Conclusion.** Both \(C\) and \(C^*\) are intervals, hence \(C^*\) is convex. No assumption on \(\overline{C^*}\) is required.

**Proof.**

Suppose \(C\) is not an interval. There exist \(a<b\) in \(C\) and a point between them not in \(C\). Since \(C\) is closed, there are \(\alpha<\beta\) in \(C\) such that
\[
(\alpha,\beta)\cap C=\varnothing.
\]
Put
\[
p_\alpha=f'(\alpha),\qquad p_\beta=f'(\beta).
\]
Strict convexity gives
\[
p_\alpha<p_\beta.
\]

Define
\[
x_0
 :=
 \frac{f^*(p_\beta)-f^*(p_\alpha)}
      {p_\beta-p_\alpha}.
\]
Strict convexity of \(f^*\) gives
\[
f^*(p_\beta)>
f^*(p_\alpha)+\alpha(p_\beta-p_\alpha),
\]
so \(x_0>\alpha\). Similarly,
\[
f^*(p_\alpha)>
f^*(p_\beta)+\beta(p_\alpha-p_\beta),
\]
so \(x_0<\beta\). Hence
\[
x_0\in(\alpha,\beta).
\]

By construction,
\[
f^*(p_\alpha)-x_0p_\alpha
=
f^*(p_\beta)-x_0p_\beta.
\tag{SOL.5.1}
\]

The function
\[
p\mapsto f^*(p)-x_0p
\]
has derivative
\[
(f^*)'(p)-x_0.
\]
It is strictly decreasing for \(p<f'(x_0)\) and strictly increasing for \(p>f'(x_0)\). Since there is no point of \(C\) between \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), the best point on the left side is \(\alpha\), and the best point on the right side is \(\beta\). Equation (SOL.5.1) shows that both are global minimizers. This contradicts right \(D\)-Chebyshevness.

Therefore \(C\) is an interval. Since \(f'\) is strictly increasing and continuous,
\[
C^*=f'(C)
\]
is also an interval. ∎

Thus the unresolved part starts only in dimension \(n\ge2\).

## Lemma SOL.6 — The finite-height ghost boundary is the exact remaining obstruction

**Hypotheses.** Assume (a) and (c). Let
\[
h=f^*+\iota_S,\qquad S=C^*,
\]
and let \(\bar h\) be the lower semicontinuous hull of \(h\). For \(x\in X\), let \(p_x\in S\) be the unique minimizer of
\[
h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle.
\]

**Conclusion.**

1. \(p_x\) also minimizes
   \[
   \bar h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle.
   \]
2. Every additional minimizer of the latter problem lies in
   \[
   \overline S\setminus U^*\subset\operatorname{bd}U^*.
   \]
3. If there are no such additional boundary minimizers for any \(x\), then \(S\) is convex.

**Proof.**

Let
\[
m_x=h(p_x)-\langle x,p_x\rangle.
\]
Since
\[
h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\ge m_x
\]
for all \(p\), taking lower limits gives
\[
\bar h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\ge m_x.
\]
At \(p_x\), relative closedness of \(S\) and continuity of \(f^*\) on \(U^*\) give
\[
\bar h(p_x)=h(p_x),
\]
so \(p_x\) is a minimizer.

If \(q\in U^*\) and \(\bar h(q)<+\infty\), then some sequence in \(S\) converges to \(q\). Lemma SOL.1 gives \(q\in S\). If \(q\) is a minimizer, uniqueness over \(S\) gives \(q=p_x\). Thus every additional minimizer is outside \(U^*\).

If no additional minimizers exist, apply the convexification argument from Lemma SOL.3 to \(\bar h\). Every tilt then has the unique minimizer \(p_x\in S\), and the same identification
\[
S=\operatorname{dom}\partial(\bar h^{**})
\]
followed by relative closedness inside \(U^*\) yields convexity. ∎

Define the ghost set
\[
G_x:=
\operatorname*{argmin}_{p\in X}
\big(\bar h(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\big)
\cap\operatorname{bd}U^*.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname*{argmin}
\big(\bar h-\langle x,\cdot\rangle\big)
=
\{p_x\}\cup G_x.
\]

The unresolved case is precisely \(G_x\neq\varnothing\) for some \(x\).

# The kernel \(f(x)=\sqrt{1+|x|^2}\)

## Lemma SOL.7 — Exact spherical reformulation

**Hypotheses.** Let
\[
f(x)=\sqrt{1+|x|^2}.
\]

**Conclusion.**

1. \(f\) is Legendre with \(\operatorname{dom}f=X\).
2.
   \[
   \nabla f(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+|x|^2}},
   \qquad
   U^*=B(0,1).
   \]
3.
   \[
   f^*(p)=
   \begin{cases}
   -\sqrt{1-|p|^2},& |p|\le1,\\
   +\infty,&|p|>1.
   \end{cases}
   \]
4. Define
   \[
   \Psi(y):=\frac{(1,y)}{\sqrt{1+|y|^2}}\in\mathbb S^n,
   \qquad \Psi(y)_0>0.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   D_f(x,y)
   =f(x)\big(1-\langle\Psi(x),\Psi(y)\rangle\big)
   =\frac{f(x)}2|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)|^2.
   \tag{SOL.7.1}
   \]
5. If \(y_k/|y_k|\to d\) and \(|y_k|\to\infty\), then
   \[
   D_f(x,y_k)\to f(x)-\langle x,d\rangle
   \ge f(x)-|x|
   =\frac1{f(x)+|x|}>0.
   \tag{SOL.7.2}
   \]

**Proof.**

The formulas for the gradient and conjugate follow by direct differentiation and one-dimensional radial optimization. For (SOL.7.1),
\[
D_f(x,y)
=f(x)-\frac{1+\langle x,y\rangle}{f(y)}
=f(x)\left(
1-\frac{1+\langle x,y\rangle}{f(x)f(y)}
\right).
\]
Both \(\Psi(x)\) and \(\Psi(y)\) are unit vectors, so
\[
1-\langle\Psi(x),\Psi(y)\rangle
=\tfrac12|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)|^2.
\]
Finally,
\[
f(x)-|x|
=\frac{f(x)^2-|x|^2}{f(x)+|x|}
=\frac1{f(x)+|x|}.
\]
∎

Thus the hinted kernel is exactly nearest-point projection on the open upper hemisphere, with the equator omitted.

## Lemma SOL.8 — An affine line gives all desired failures except existence

Let \(n=2\), let \(f\) be as in SOL.7, and put
\[
C=\{(t,1):t\in\mathbb R\}.
\]

**Conclusion.**

1. \(C\) is closed and nonempty.
2. \(\overline{C^*}\not\subset U^*\).
3. \(C^*\) is nonconvex.
4. \(C\) is not right \(D\)-Chebyshev because a right projection fails to exist.

**Proof.**

Here
\[
C^*
=
\left\{
\frac{(t,1)}{\sqrt{t^2+2}}:t\in\mathbb R
\right\}.
\]
As \(t\to\pm\infty\),
\[
\frac{(t,1)}{\sqrt{t^2+2}}\to(\pm1,0)\in\operatorname{bd}U^*.
\]

At \(t=\pm1\), the midpoint of the two dual points is
\[
\frac12\left(
\frac{(1,1)}{\sqrt3}
+
\frac{(-1,1)}{\sqrt3}
\right)
=
\left(0,\frac1{\sqrt3}\right).
\]
Any point of \(C^*\) with first coordinate zero must correspond to \(t=0\), where the second coordinate is \(1/\sqrt2\). Since
\[
\frac1{\sqrt3}\neq\frac1{\sqrt2},
\]
\(C^*\) is nonconvex.

Take \(x=(1,-1)\). Maximizing the score in SOL.7 is equivalent to maximizing
\[
\frac{1+\langle x,(t,1)\rangle}{\sqrt{t^2+2}}
=
\frac{t}{\sqrt{t^2+2}}.
\]
For finite \(t\),
\[
1-\frac{t^2}{t^2+2}
=\frac2{t^2+2}>0,
\]
so the score is strictly less than \(1\). Its limit as \(t\to+\infty\) is \(1\). Hence the supremum is not attained. ∎

## Lemma SOL.9 — An offset halfspace has nonconvex \(C^*\), but existence fails

Let
\[
C=\{(y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb R^2:y_2\ge1\}.
\]

**Conclusion.**

1. \(\overline{C^*}\not\subset U^*\).
2. \(C^*\) is nonconvex.
3. \(P^\to_C(0,-2)\) is empty.

**Proof.**

The points
\[
p^\pm=\frac{(\pm1,1)}{\sqrt3}
\]
belong to \(C^*\). Their midpoint is
\[
m=\left(0,\frac1{\sqrt3}\right).
\]
The inverse gradient is
\[
(\nabla f)^{-1}(p)=\frac{p}{\sqrt{1-|p|^2}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
(\nabla f)^{-1}(m)
=
\left(0,\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)\notin C.
\]
Hence \(m\notin C^*\).

For \(x=(0,-2)\), the score at \(y\in C\) is
\[
\frac{1-2y_2}{\sqrt{1+y_1^2+y_2^2}}<0,
\]
because \(y_2\ge1\). Along \(y=(t,1)\),
\[
\frac{1-2}{\sqrt{t^2+2}}
=-\frac1{\sqrt{t^2+2}}\to0.
\]
Thus the supremum is \(0\), but no finite \(y\in C\) attains it. ∎

## Lemma SOL.10 — A simple unbounded nonconvex set loses uniqueness

Let
\[
C=\{(t,0):t\ge0\}\cup\{(0,t):t\ge0\}.
\]

**Conclusion.** \(C^*\) is nonconvex and \(\overline{C^*}\not\subset U^*\), but \(C\) is not right \(D\)-Chebyshev.

**Proof.**

The dual set is the union of two radial intervals along the positive coordinate axes, so it is nonconvex and accumulates at \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\).

For \(x=(1,1)\), the score on either ray is
\[
\phi(t)=\frac{1+t}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
\phi'(t)
=
\frac{1-t}{(1+t^2)^{3/2}}.
\]
Thus \(\phi\) has its unique maximum on each ray at \(t=1\), with value
\[
\phi(1)=\sqrt2.
\]
Hence both \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) are right projections of \(x\). ∎

## Lemma SOL.11 — A large class where (b) fails but convexity survives

Let \(f(x)=\sqrt{1+|x|^2}\). Suppose \(C\subset X\) is closed, convex and contains \(0\).

**Conclusion.**

1. \(C\) is right \(D\)-Chebyshev.
2. \(C^*=\nabla f(C)\) is convex.
3. \(C\) may be unbounded, so \(\overline{C^*}\subset U^*\) need not hold.

**Proof.**

Define the closed convex cone
\[
L:=\overline{\operatorname{cone}}\big(\{(1,y):y\in C\}\big)
\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}.
\]
Since \(0\in C\),
\[
e_0:=(1,0)\in L.
\]

Fix \(x\in X\), put \(v=(1,x)\), and let \(z=P_Lv\), the Euclidean projection onto \(L\). The cone projection identities give
\[
w:=v-z\in L^\circ,
\qquad
\langle w,z\rangle=0.
\]
If \(z_0=0\), then
\[
\langle w,e_0\rangle
=\langle v-z,e_0\rangle=1.
\]
But \(w\in L^\circ\) and \(e_0\in L\) require
\[
\langle w,e_0\rangle\le0,
\]
a contradiction. Therefore \(z_0>0\).

For every unit \(q\in L\),
\[
\langle v,q\rangle
=\langle z,q\rangle+\langle w,q\rangle
\le |z|.
\]
Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz forces
\[
q=\frac z{|z|}.
\]
Thus the maximizer is unique. Since \(z_0>0\), the normalized point belongs to \(\Psi(C)\), proving right \(D\)-Chebyshevness by SOL.7.

For convexity of \(C^*\), take \(y_0,y_1\in C\) and write
\[
\Psi(y_i)=(a_i,p_i),
\qquad
a_i=\frac1{\sqrt{1+|y_i|^2}},
\quad
p_i=\nabla f(y_i).
\]
For \(0\le t\le1\), put
\[
p=(1-t)p_0+tp_1,\qquad
s=(1-t)a_0+ta_1.
\]
Since each \((a_i,p_i)\) is a unit vector,
\[
s^2+|p|^2\le1.
\]
Because \(s>0\), one has \(|p|<1\). Put
\[
\lambda=\frac1{\sqrt{1-|p|^2}}.
\]
Then
\[
\lambda s\le1.
\]
Define
\[
\alpha_0=\lambda(1-t)a_0,\qquad
\alpha_1=\lambda t a_1.
\]
These coefficients are nonnegative and satisfy
\[
\alpha_0+\alpha_1\le1.
\]
Therefore
\[
y:=\lambda p=\alpha_0y_0+\alpha_1y_1
 +(1-\alpha_0-\alpha_1)0\in C.
\]
Finally,
\[
\sqrt{1+|y|^2}=\lambda,
\]
and hence
\[
\nabla f(y)=\frac y\lambda=p.
\]
Thus \(p\in C^*\), proving convexity. ∎

This rules out a broad class of tempting counterexamples for the radial kernel.

### 1. Dual formula for the square-root kernel

Using SymPy:

```python
import sympy as sp
t = sp.symbols('t', real=True)
score = t/sp.sqrt(t**2 + 2)
sp.simplify(1-score**2)
# 2/(t**2 + 2)
sp.limit(score, t, sp.oo)
# 1
```

This certifies the nonattainment in SOL.8.

### 2. Nonconvexity witness for the affine line

```python
pplus  = sp.Matrix([1,1])/sp.sqrt(3)
pminus = sp.Matrix([-1,1])/sp.sqrt(3)
mid = sp.simplify((pplus+pminus)/2)
# Matrix([0, 1/sqrt(3)])
```

The only affine-line image with first coordinate \(0\) is
\[
(0,1/\sqrt2).
\]

### 3. Upper-halfspace witness

```python
m2 = 1/sp.sqrt(3)
inverse_second = sp.simplify(m2/sp.sqrt(1-m2**2))
# 1/sqrt(2)
```

Since \(1/\sqrt2<1\), the midpoint is outside \(C^*\).

For nonattainment:

```python
r = sp.symbols('r', real=True)
halfspace_score = -1/sp.sqrt(r**2+2)
sp.limit(halfspace_score, r, sp.oo)
# 0
```

Every finite score is negative.

### 4. Two-ray nonuniqueness

```python
phi = (1+t)/sp.sqrt(1+t**2)
sp.factor(sp.diff(phi,t))
# -(t - 1)/(t**2 + 1)**(3/2)
sp.simplify(phi.subs(t,1))
# sqrt(2)
```

The two distinct rays give the same maximum.

### 5. Any proposed counterexample should be tested in dual form

For a proposed \(f,C\):

1. Compute
   \[
   g=f^*,\qquad S=\nabla f(C).
   \]
2. Replace right projection by
   \[
   \min_{p\in S}\big(g(p)-\langle x,p\rangle\big).
   \]
3. Compute the lower semicontinuous hull
   \[
   \bar h=\operatorname{cl}(g+\iota_S).
   \]
4. Check exact boundary limits, not only stationary points.
5. Check whether
   \[
   G_x=
   \arg\min(\bar h-\langle x,\cdot\rangle)
   \cap\operatorname{bd}U^*
   \]
   is nonempty.
6. If two finite candidates are claimed not to tie, eliminate the parameter symbolically using a resultant or exact monotonicity inequalities. Numerical optimization is insufficient.
7. For existence, compare every finite critical value with every boundary limit.

# WHAT REMAINS

1. **The general \(n\ge2\) question is unresolved in this answer.** I have neither an example satisfying (A) nor a proof of (B).

2. The only unresolved situation is the **finite-height boundary case**:
   \[
   p_k\in C^*,\qquad
   p_k\to\bar p\in\operatorname{bd}U^*,
   \qquad
   \sup_k f^*(p_k)<+\infty.
   \]
   This includes
   \[
   f(x)=\sqrt{1+|x|^2},
   \qquad
   f^*(p)\to0\quad\text{as }|p|\uparrow1.
   \]

3. The smallest missing statement is the following ghost-face exclusion principle:

   > If every tilt of \(f^*+\iota_{C^*}\) has a unique attained minimizer in \(C^*\), can a boundary point in \(G_x\) coexist with that minimizer in such a way that \(C^*\) remains nonconvex?

   Proving that all \(G_x\) are empty would prove (B). Constructing a nonconvex \(S\) with nonempty \(G_x\) while preserving one actual minimizer for every tilt would give (A).

4. Lower semicontinuity of \(f^*+\iota_{C^*}\) is sufficient but is **not claimed necessary**. For example \(C=X\) with the square-root kernel has \(C^*=U^*\), and the corresponding indicator extension is not lower semicontinuous at the unit sphere, yet \(C^*\) is convex.

5. Lemma SOL.11 only covers the radial square-root kernel and convex sets containing \(0\). It does not rule out nonconvex \(C\), convex \(C\) not containing \(0\), or other Legendre kernels.

6. I have not ruled out all candidates for
   \[
   f(x)=\sum_j\sqrt{1+x_j^2}
   \]
   or for box-valued gradient ranges. The same ghost-boundary issue remains there.
