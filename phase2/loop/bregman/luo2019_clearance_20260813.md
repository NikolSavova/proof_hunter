# Clearance memo — Luo, Meng, Wen & Yao (2019) does NOT resolve Problem 2

*2026-08-13. The prior-art sweep returned AMBER with one hard gate: Luo, Meng, Wen, Yao,
"Bregman distances without coercive condition: suns, Chebyshev sets and Klee sets",
**Optimization 68(8) (2019), 1599–1624**, DOI 10.1080/02331934.2019.1625898 — paywalled and
unread, and dangerous because full domain of `f` is dual to supercoercivity of `f*`, so a paper
about Chebyshev sets "without coercive condition" aims near our target. Sihao obtained the PDF;
this memo records the reading. **Verdict: the gate is CLEARED.***

## The threat, stated precisely

Their **Lemma 3.2** is exactly the duality that could have killed us: for `x in U`, `y in C`,

```
y in ->Pi^f_C(x)   <=>   grad f(y) in <-Pi^{f*}_{grad f(C)}(grad f(x))
```

i.e. the RIGHT projection onto `C` for `f` is the LEFT projection onto `C* = grad f(C)` for `f*`.
So any theorem of theirs of the form "left-Chebyshev ⇒ convex", applied to `f*`, would yield
"`C` right-Chebyshev ⇒ `C*` convex" — precisely Fact 3.2's conclusion. If that theorem needed no
full-domain-type hypothesis, our counterexample would be false.

## Why it does not fire

**Their right-projection theorem carries the same hypothesis, in disguise.**

> **Theorem 3.12.** Suppose `C` is `->D_f`-proximinal.
> (1) (i) ⇒ (ii) [(i) = the variational characterisation (34); (ii) = `C` is a `->D_f`-sun].
> (2) **If `U = X`**, then (i) ⟺ (ii) ⟺ (iii).
> (3) If `grad f(U) = U*`, and `f*` is Gâteaux differentiable and strictly convex on `U*`, then
> (i) is equivalent to (iv) **`grad f(C)` is convex**; consequently, if in addition **`U = X`**,
> then all of the above are equivalent mutually.

`U = X` means `int dom f = X`, i.e. **`dom f = X` — Bauschke–Macklem–Wang's full-domain
hypothesis verbatim.** The same condition governs the left case: Theorem 3.9's implications
`(i)⇒(ii)⇒(iii)⇒(iv)` hold generally, but the mutual equivalence — the direction that turns
Chebyshev into convex — needs the extra `grad f(U) = X*`.

So Luo et al. **relax 1-coercivity, not full domain.** Their title's "without coercive
condition" refers to dropping the 1-coercivity assumption of Bauschke et al.; the full-domain /
`U = X` requirement survives intact in exactly the place we care about.

## Our example against their theorem — consistent, and illustrative

For `f` = negative entropy on `R^2`: `U = R^2_++`, `X = R^2`, so **`U != X`** and parts (2) and
the "consequently" of (3) do not apply. But part (3)'s own hypotheses DO hold for us:
`grad f(U) = R^2 = U*`, and `f*(y) = sum_j e^{y_j}` is Gâteaux differentiable and strictly convex
on `U* = R^2`. So Theorem 3.12(3) applies and asserts

```
(i) holds   <=>   grad f(C) is convex.
```

Our `C*` is a strictly concave arc, hence nonconvex — so **(i) must fail for our example**. That
is consistent: `C` being right-Chebyshev does not imply (i); the route from Chebyshev back to (i)
is Theorem 3.12(2), which needs `U = X`. No contradiction anywhere.

Nor does **Theorem 3.13** threaten us: it concludes that a boundedly compact right-Chebyshev set
is a `->D_f`-sun. Being a sun does not give convexity of `grad f(C)` without `U = X`. Section 4
(Theorems 4.6, 4.7) concerns Klee sets — a different problem of the survey.

## What this does to our claim

**It strengthens it.** Two independent research groups' machinery for this problem — Bauschke,
Macklem & Wang's original, and Luo, Meng, Wen & Yao's coercivity-free reworking — both stop at
the same `dom f = X` wall, from opposite directions (one assuming it, one re-deriving it as
`U = X`). Our example shows the wall is real rather than an artefact of either proof technique:
the conclusion genuinely fails without it, even when the other hypothesis `cl C* subset U*` holds.
