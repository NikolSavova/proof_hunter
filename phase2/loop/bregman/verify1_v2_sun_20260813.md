## 1. Exact definitions and hypotheses from the attachment

### Modulus of total convexity

The attachment defines

\[
\nu_f(y,t)
:=
\inf\{D_f(x,y):x\in\dom f,\ \|x-y\|=t\},
\qquad t\ge 0.
\]

The infimum is over \(\boldsymbol{\dom f}\), **not over \(X\)**.

The locally uniform modulus is

\[
\nu_f^{\mathrm{loc}}(y,t)
:=
\lim_{\delta\to0+}
\inf\{\nu_f(u,t):u\in B(y,\delta)\cap\dom f\}.
\]

Here \(B(y,\delta)\) is the closed ball, according to the preliminaries.

Definition 2.1 then says:

- \(f\) is totally convex at \(y\) if \(\nu_f(y,t)>0\) for every \(t>0\);
- \(f\) is locally uniformly totally convex at \(y\) if
  \(\nu_f^{\mathrm{loc}}(y,t)>0\) for every \(t>0\).

In particular, pointwise positivity of \(\nu_f(u,t)\) for each nearby \(u\) is not by itself enough for local uniform total convexity; one needs a lower bound uniform in nearby \(u\).

## 2. Check of (A): total convexity of entropy on \(U\)

Let \(y\in U=\mathbb R_{++}^2\) and \(x\in\dom f=\mathbb R_+^2\). Then

\[
D_f(x,y)
=
\sum_{j=1}^2
\left[
x_j\log\frac{x_j}{y_j}-x_j+y_j
\right],
\]

with \(0\log 0=0\).

For fixed \(a>0\), define

\[
\phi_a(r)=r\log(r/a)-r+a,\qquad r\ge0,
\]

where \(\phi_a(0)=a\). This is continuous on \([0,\infty)\), nonnegative, and

\[
\phi_a(r)=0\iff r=a.
\]

Consequently \(D_f(\cdot,y)\) is continuous on \(\mathbb R_+^2\) and

\[
D_f(x,y)>0\quad\text{whenever }x\neq y.
\]

For \(t>0\), the set

\[
K_{y,t}
=
\{x\in\dom f:\|x-y\|=t\}
\]

is closed and bounded in \(\mathbb R^2\), hence compact. It excludes \(y\), and is nonempty. Therefore

\[
\nu_f(y,t)
=
\min_{x\in K_{y,t}}D_f(x,y)>0.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{f\text{ is totally convex at every point of }U.}
\]

### Boundary issue

The fact that a varying point \(x\) may lie on \(\partial\dom f\) causes no difficulty. The derivative in \(D_f(x,y)\) is taken at the second argument \(y\in U\), not at \(x\). In particular, if \(x_j=0\),

\[
x_j\log(x_j/y_j)-x_j+y_j=y_j<\infty.
\]

So the compactness argument genuinely includes boundary points \(x\in\partial\dom f\).

The parenthetical “indeed, at every point of \(\dom f\)” needs different treatment. Under the gradient-only definition stated in the question, \(D_f(x,y)\) is not defined when the base point \(y\in\partial\dom f\). If one instead uses the directional-derivative extension in Remark 2.1, the parenthetical can be rescued, but the preceding continuity argument no longer applies: when \(y_j=0<x_j\), the corresponding divergence is typically \(+\infty\). On the finite locus \(x_j=0\) whenever \(y_j=0\), the problem reduces to the same compact positive argument on the corresponding face.

Therefore the theorem-relevant part of (A) is correct, but the cleanest manuscript formulation is simply:

> \(f\) is totally convex at every point of \(U\).

## 3. Checks of (B) and (C)

### (B) Gradient range

For \(x\in U\),

\[
\nabla f(x)=(\log x_1,\log x_2).
\]

This maps \(\mathbb R_{++}^2\) bijectively onto \(\mathbb R^2\). Since

\[
f^*(u)=e^{u_1}+e^{u_2},
\qquad
\dom f^*=\mathbb R^2,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{\nabla f(U)=\mathbb R^2=U^*.}
\]

So (B) is correct.

### (C) Locally uniform total convexity of \(f^*\)

For \(u,v\in\mathbb R^2\),

\[
D_{f^*}(v,u)
=
\sum_{j=1}^2
\left[
e^{v_j}-e^{u_j}-e^{u_j}(v_j-u_j)
\right].
\]

Equivalently,

\[
D_{f^*}(v,u)
=
\sum_{j=1}^2
e^{u_j}
\left(e^{v_j-u_j}-1-(v_j-u_j)\right).
\]

This is jointly continuous in \((u,v)\), nonnegative, and strictly positive when \(v\neq u\).

Fix \(y\in\mathbb R^2\) and \(t>0\). Choose any \(\rho>0\), and consider the **joint shell**

\[
K=
\{(u,v):
u\in B(y,\rho),\ \|v-u\|=t\}.
\]

This set is compact in \(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2\). Because \(t>0\), it contains no pair with \(v=u\). Hence

\[
m:=\min_{(u,v)\in K}D_{f^*}(v,u)>0.
\]

For every \(0<\delta\le\rho\) and every \(u\in B(y,\delta)\),

\[
\nu_{f^*}(u,t)
=
\inf_{\|v-u\|=t}D_{f^*}(v,u)
\ge m.
\]

Therefore

\[
\nu_{f^*}^{\mathrm{loc}}(y,t)
=
\lim_{\delta\to0+}
\inf_{u\in B(y,\delta)}\nu_{f^*}(u,t)
\ge m>0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{f^*\text{ is locally uniformly totally convex at every }y\in U^*.}
\]

So (C) is correct. The required argument is joint compactness in \((u,v)\), not merely compactness for each fixed \(u\).

## 5. Direct verification of the sun property

This can be proved without Theorem 3.13.

For \(w=(w_1,w_2)\in U\), define

\[
h_w(t)=e^t+e^{-t^2}-w_1t+w_2t^2,\qquad 1\le t\le2.
\]

Then

\[
h_w'(t)
=
e^t-2te^{-t^2}-w_1+2w_2t
\]

and

\[
h_w''(t)
=
e^t+(4t^2-2)e^{-t^2}+2w_2>0
\]

because \(t\in[1,2]\) and \(w_2>0\). Thus \(h_w\) is strictly convex.

Suppose \(y=c(s)\in P_C^\to(x)\), where \(s\in[1,2]\), and put

\[
z_\lambda=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y.
\]

At \(w=y=c(s)\),

\[
h_y'(s)
=
e^s-2se^{-s^2}-e^s+2se^{-s^2}
=0.
\]

Since \(h_w'(s)\) is affine in \(w\),

\[
h_{z_\lambda}'(s)
=
\lambda h_x'(s)+(1-\lambda)h_y'(s)
=
\lambda h_x'(s).
\]

There are three cases.

1. **\(s\in(1,2)\).**  
   Since \(s\) minimizes \(h_x\),
   \[
   h_x'(s)=0.
   \]
   Hence \(h_{z_\lambda}'(s)=0\). Strict convexity makes \(s\) the unique minimizer of \(h_{z_\lambda}\).

2. **\(s=1\).**  
   Endpoint optimality gives
   \[
   h_x'(1)\ge0.
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
   h_{z_\lambda}'(1)=\lambda h_x'(1)\ge0.
   \]
   Strict convexity makes \(1\) the unique minimizer.

3. **\(s=2\).**  
   Endpoint optimality gives
   \[
   h_x'(2)\le0.
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
   h_{z_\lambda}'(2)=\lambda h_x'(2)\le0.
   \]
   Strict convexity makes \(2\) the unique minimizer.

This is valid for every \(\lambda\ge0\) for which \(z_\lambda\in U\). Hence

\[
\boxed{C\text{ satisfies Definition 3.11 directly}.}
\]

There is no contradiction with Theorem 3.13.

## 6. Check of (F), including a direct failure of condition (i)

The theorem-based argument is valid:

- \(C\) is right proximinal.
- \(\nabla f(U)=U^*\).
- \(f^*\) is Gâteaux differentiable and strictly convex on \(U^*\).
- \(\nabla f(C)=\{(t,-t^2):1\le t\le2\}\) is nonconvex.

Therefore Theorem 3.12(3) gives

\[
(iv)\text{ fails}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(i)\text{ fails}.
\]

There is also an explicit witness.

Set

\[
s=\frac32,\qquad
\eta=e^{-9/4},
\]

and define

\[
y=c(s)=\left(e^{3/2},\eta\right),
\]

\[
x=
\left(
e^{3/2}-\frac32\eta,\,
\frac12\eta
\right)\in U.
\]

At \(s=3/2\),

\[
h_x'(s)=0.
\]

Since \(h_x\) is strictly convex, \(s\) is its unique minimizer, so

\[
y\in P_C^\to(x).
\]

For a general \(t\in[1,2]\), stationarity gives

\[
e^s-x_1
=
2s\left(e^{-s^2}-x_2\right).
\]

Using \(\nabla f(c(r))=(r,-r^2)\), one obtains

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left\langle
\nabla f(c(s))-\nabla f(c(t)),
c(s)-x
\right\rangle\\
&\qquad
=(s-t)^2\left(e^{-s^2}-x_2\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here

\[
e^{-s^2}-x_2=\frac12\eta>0.
\]

Taking \(t=1\) gives

\[
\left\langle
\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(c(1)),
y-x
\right\rangle
=
\left(\frac12\right)^2\frac{\eta}{2}
=
\frac18e^{-9/4}>0.
\]

This directly violates equation (34), which requires the quantity to be \(\le0\) for every \(c\in C\). Thus

\[
\boxed{(i)\text{ fails directly}.}
\]

### Why \(U=X\) matters here

For this same witness,

\[
(z_\lambda)_2
=
\lambda\frac{\eta}{2}+(1-\lambda)\eta
=
\eta\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right).
\]

Hence

\[
z_\lambda\in U
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
0\le\lambda<2.
\]

The proof of Theorem 3.12(2), \((ii)\Rightarrow(i)\), uses the sun property for every \(\lambda>0\), divides the resulting inequality by \(\lambda\), and sends \(\lambda\to\infty\). That step is unavailable here because the admissible ray leaves \(U\) at \(\lambda=2\).

This identifies the exact geometric obstruction.

## 7. Check of (G) and Corollary 3.14

We have established

\[
(ii)\text{ holds},\qquad (i)\text{ fails},\qquad U\neq X.
\]

Because Theorem 3.12(1) only asserts

\[
(i)\Rightarrow(ii),
\]

there is no contradiction. The example specifically disproves the unrestricted converse

\[
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
\]

when \(U=X\) is removed.

The precise conclusion should be:

> The hypothesis \(U=X\) in Theorem 3.12(2) is not redundant and cannot be deleted without replacement.

It would be slightly too strong to say that \(U=X\) is the weakest possible or logically necessary assumption in every variant; the example proves non-omissibility, not a weakest-hypothesis characterization.

Corollary 3.14 is not contradicted. It assumes:

\[
f\text{ is totally convex at every point of }X,
\qquad
\nabla f(X)=U^*.
\]

Under the Section 3 standing convention that \(\nabla f\) is defined on \(U\), the expression \(\nabla f(X)\) effectively requires \(U=X\). Negative entropy does not satisfy this.

VERDICT: MINOR_REPAIRS

1. **Location:** (A), “indeed of \(\dom f\).”  
   **Claim:** The same compact-continuity argument proves total convexity at boundary base points.  
   **Why wrong:** The gradient definition is unavailable when the second argument lies on \(\partial\dom f\), and under the directional extension the divergence may be \(+\infty\), not continuous and finite.  
   **Suggested fix:** State only total convexity on \(U\), which is exactly what Theorem 3.13 needs; or add a separate face-by-face argument using Remark 2.1.

2. **Location:** (C), “by the same compactness argument.”  
   **Claim:** Pointwise compactness for each fixed nearby \(u\) handles the locally uniform liminf.  
   **Why wrong:** Positive minima depending on \(u\) could in principle tend to zero.  
   **Suggested fix:** Use the joint compact set \(\{(u,v):u\in B(y,\rho),\|v-u\|=t\}\), as above, to obtain one uniform positive lower bound.

3. **Location:** (G), “genuinely requires \(U=X\).”  
   **Claim:** The example proves \(U=X\) is the uniquely necessary or weakest condition.  
   **Why wrong:** It proves only that \(U=X\) is not redundant in the theorem as stated. Other ray-completeness or replacement assumptions may suffice.  
   **Suggested fix:** Say “\(U=X\) cannot be dropped without replacement.”
