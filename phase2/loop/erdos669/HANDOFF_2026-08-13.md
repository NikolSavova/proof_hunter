## 2. The problem, correctly identified

For fixed `k`, let

- `f_k(n)` be the maximum number of lines containing **exactly** `k` of `n` planar points;
- `F_k(n)` be the maximum number of lines containing **at least** `k` of the points.

Erdős and Purdy ask for estimates and, in particular, whether

```text
lim_{n->infinity} f_k(n)/n^2     and     lim_{n->infinity} F_k(n)/n^2
```

exist and what their values are. The `k=2` and `k=3` constants are `1/2` and `1/6`, respectively.
The existence question is still open for `k >= 4` in every source checked.

Important repository correction: the old entry in
`phase2/loop/candidates/erdos/SHORTLIST.md` guessed that #669 was a Heilbronn/fixed-area-triangle
problem. That was wrong. The durable SQLite row in `problem-id/db/problems.sqlite`, the 1992
Erdős article, the 1997 source, and the current Erdős Problems page all identify #669 as this
generalized orchard/rich-lines problem. The shortlist has been corrected.

Primary statement sources:

## 3. Main rigorous candidate theorem: lattice zonotopes

Let `V={v_1,...,v_k}` be primitive, pairwise nonparallel integer normals spanning the full dual
lattice `Z^2`. Let `R` denote a 90-degree lattice rotation and set

```text
Z = sum_i [0, R v_i],
D = area(Z) = sum_{i<j} |det(v_i,v_j)|,
w_i = sum_j |det(v_i,v_j)|.
```

For an integer `q >= 1`, include, for each `i`, every integer-level line

```text
v_i . x = t
```

that intersects `qZ`. There are exactly `q w_i+1` lines in family `i`, hence

```text
n_q = 2Dq+k.
```

Every lattice point in `qZ` lies on one line from each of the `k` families. Conversely, because
the normals span `Z^2`, every finite `k`-fold intersection has integral coordinates and lies in
`qZ`. Thus the finite exact-`k` vertices are precisely `qZ intersect Z^2`. Pick/Ehrhart gives

```text
t_k^finite = |qZ intersect Z^2| = Dq^2+kq+1
           = (n_q^2-k^2)/(4D)+1.
```

At finite points there is at most one line from each parallel family, so these vertices have
exactly, not merely at least, multiplicity `k`. In the projective completion the family-`i`
infinity point has multiplicity `q w_i+1`. For `q >= 2` this is greater than `k`, so it does not
contaminate the exact-`k` count. At `q=1` an infinity point is accidentally exact-`k` precisely
when `w_i=k-1`; this harmless finite exception occurs in the displayed `k=5` example.

Projective duality turns arrangement lines into points and exact-`k` vertices into exact-`k`
rich lines. Choose a generic affine chart after dualizing, then pad to arbitrary `n` by adding
points outside the finite union of the already-counted exact-`k` lines. Therefore

```text
f_k(n) >= n^2/(4D)-O_k(n),
F_k(n) >= n^2/(4D)-O_k(n).
```

### Lattice-index caveat

For normals spanning a sublattice of index `h`, the coefficient is `h/(4D)`, not `1/(4D)`.
After changing to a basis of that sublattice, `D/h` is the area of a lattice `2k`-gon. This closes
the apparent loophole of choosing a coarse sublattice to beat the determinant minimum.

### Optimality inside the whole support-level zonotope scheme

Let `A(r)` be the minimum area of a convex lattice `r`-gon. Simpson proved that a minimum-area
`2k`-gon may be chosen parallel-sided. Consequently the best possible determinant area in this
scheme is `A(2k)`.

The conclusion is robust to unequal family lengths and more general convex strip windows. If
`Q` is the retained window and `Z` is the unit zonotope, the total leading support-level count is
`S=2V(Q,Z)`, where `V` is mixed area. Minkowski's first inequality gives

```text
V(Q,Z)^2 >= area(Q) area(Z),
```

so `area(Q)/S^2 <= 1/(4 area(Z))`, with equality only for a homothetic zonotope. Hence weighting
the generators or changing the convex window cannot improve the leading coefficient. The scheme's
sharp theorem is therefore

```text
f_k(n), F_k(n) >= n^2/[4A(2k)]-O_k(n).
```

This is a genuine theorem conditional only on standard Pick/Ehrhart, mixed-area, Simpson, and
projective-duality facts. It is **not** known to solve the global extremal problem.

Two exact examples useful in a note or referee check:

- `k=5`: `D=14`, `n_q=28q+5`, and `t_5^finite=14q^2+5q+1`. At `q=1` one infinity point is
  also exactly fivefold; for `q>=2` no infinity exception occurs.
- `k=6`: `D=24`, `n_q=48q+6`, and `t_6^finite=24q^2+6q+1`; there is no exact-six infinity
  exception even at `q=1`.

For `k=4`, one convenient family is given by normals
`(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,-1)`. In elementary coordinates this is the `3q,3q,4q,4q` four-direction
construction: 14q arrangement lines produce exactly `7q^2` finite quadruple vertices. The
continuous width ratio `4/3` uniquely maximizes this four-family square/diamond template and gives
`1/28`; Palásti used ratio `3/2`, giving `7/200`.

The general construction also has the right large-`k` order. Bárány–Tokushige prove
`A(r)=Theta(r^3)` and existence of a limiting normalized minimum-area constant. The best numerical
candidate quoted in the sources gives approximately `1.68858/k^3` for the zonotope coefficient.
Szemerédi–Trotter gives `F_k(n)=O(n^2/k^3+n/k)`, so this construction matches the correct `k^-3`
order up to an absolute factor. That order comparison does not determine any fixed-`k` constant.

## 7. Upper bounds established or recovered

Let `t_r` count `r`-fold vertices of a real projective line arrangement and let
`N=binom(n,2)`. Pair counting and Melchior give, for fixed `k>=4`,

```text
limsup F_k(n)/n^2 <= 1/[(k-2)(k+3)].
```

The denominator is `(k-2)(k+3)`, not `(k-2)(k+1)`. The latter appeared during the first pass and
was an arithmetic error. Shnurnikov and Bojanowski/Langer-type inequalities improve the global
fixed-`k` upper bounds to

```text
k=4:       1/14,
k=5,...,8: 1/(k^2+3k-15),
k>=9:      2/[3k(k-2)].
```

For Bojanowski's inequality, the stated theorem covers arrangements of maximum multiplicity at
most `2n/3`. If a point has multiplicity greater than `2n/3`, every other `k`-fold point consumes
at least `k-1` of the fewer than `n/3` remaining lines, and pair counting gives an even smaller
asymptotic coefficient. Thus the displayed bound is globally valid.

For `k=4`, there is a useful exact defect identity. Let
`T=sum_{r>=4}t_r` and `Delta=sum_{j>=4}(j-3)f_j`, where `f_j` counts `j`-gonal faces. Then

```text
N-7T = 3 + Delta + 3t_3
       + sum_{r>=5} ((r+5)(r-4)/2)t_r.
```

So asymptotic equality in the `1/14` upper bound would require an almost-simplicial arrangement
dominated by double and quadruple vertices, with triple, higher-multiplicity, and nonsimplicial
defects all `o(n^2)`. Known local simplicial adjacency inequalities improve this only by an
additive constant, not by `epsilon n^2`; the 13-line arrangement `A(13,2)` with
`(t_2,t_3,t_4)=(12,4,9)` is finite sharp evidence. No rigorous epsilon improvement was found.

## 8. Why the normalized-limit question remains open

For either `a_k=f_k` or `a_k=F_k`, three useful lemmas are rigorous:

1. **Generic projective union.** Move one finite configuration by a generic projectivity so no
   mixed line contains three points. Internal exact/rich lines are preserved, hence
   `a_k(n+m)>=a_k(n)+a_k(m)`.
2. **Downward sampling.** For `N>=n>=k`,
   `a_k(n)>=((n)_k/(N)_k)a_k(N)`. For `F_k`, mark a canonical `k`-subset on each rich line.
3. **One-point increments.** Generic padding and deletion give
   `0<=a_k(n+1)-a_k(n)<=floor(n/(k-1))`.

These facts do not imply convergence after division by `n^2`. Superadditivity controls `a(n)/n`,
not `a(n)/n^2`, while sampling loses the factor `(n/N)^(k-2)` after quadratic normalization.

A rigorous numerical countermodel shows that this is a genuine logical gap. For lacunary `M_j`,
define

```text
H_M(x)=x^k/M^(k-2)  for x<=M,     H_M(x)=Mx for x>=M,
B(x)=epsilon sum_j H_{M_j}(x).
```

With sufficiently small `epsilon`, `B` is increasing, superadditive, obeys the sampling analogue,
has `O(n)` increments and `O(n^2)` growth, but `B(x)/x^2` has positive limsup and zero liminf.
Any proof of existence must use additional geometry.

### Failed quadratic amplifiers

- Generic overlays of `q` projective copies preserve only `q a(N)` lines on `qN` points.
- Replace `k` collinear centers by `q` transverse fibers. A line through endpoint labels `a,b`
  hits every intermediate fiber exactly when `a congruent b (mod k-1)`, so one source line produces
  only `q^2/(k-1)+O(q)` exact-`k` lines, not `q^2`.
- Abstract Steiner/design blow-ups can reach the pair budget, but real representability fails by
  Sylvester–Gallai/Melchior. Rank-three real-representable matroids are not closed under the needed
  substitutions.

A sufficient missing lemma would be a universal geometric quadratic amplifier: turn every `N`-point
configuration into `(1+o(1))qN` points with `(1-o(1))q^2 a_k(N)` inherited rich lines, densely enough
in output sizes to allow padding. The obvious versions above fail. A stability/classification
theorem reducing extremizers to bounded arithmetic templates would also suffice, but none is known.

## 9. Other useful baseline

For the square grid `P_m={0,...,m-1}^2`, primitive-direction counting gives, for fixed `k`,

```text
F_k(m^2)/m^4 -> 3(2k-1)/(pi^2 k^2(k-1)^2),
f_k(m^2)/m^4 -> 6(3k^2-1)/(pi^2 k^2(k-1)^2(k+1)^2).
```

This is rigorous via a standard coprime Riemann-sum lemma but weaker than the zonotope construction
for the small `k` values of interest. It is likely classical and no novelty claim should attach to
it.
