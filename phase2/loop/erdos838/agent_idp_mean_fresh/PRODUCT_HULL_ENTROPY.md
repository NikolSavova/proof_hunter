## 1. The finite IDP statement is false

Let

\[
 L(P)=\log {Z_P(1)\over Z_P(1/2)},\qquad
 R(P)=\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{1/2}}X.
\]

The exact path identity is

\[
 L(P)=R(P)+D(\mathbb P_{1/2}\Vert\mathbb P_1).       \tag{1}
\]

The saved rational 58-point configuration has

\[
 H(P)={58Z_P(1/2)\over Z_P(1)}
 ={33994061\over16990512}>2.
\]

Consequently, without computing the path distribution,

\[
 R(P)\le L(P)=\log {58\over H(P)}<\log29.             \tag{2}
\]

Thus the formerly proposed finite inequality `R(P)>=log(n/2)` is rigorously
false.  Its deficit is at least

\[
 \log{H(P)\over2}
 =\log{33994061\over33981024}>0,                      \tag{3}
\]

and is larger by the path KL divergence.  The viable target is only

\[
 R(P)\ge(1-o(1))\log n,                               \tag{IDP*}
\]

or a minimizer-only version.

## 2. An all-rank optimized product-hull identity

For a convex face `A`, put

\[
 h(A)=|A|,\qquad q(A)=|P\setminus\operatorname{conv}(A)|.
\]

Choose a Bernoulli-`p` subset `T` of `P` and take the vertex set of its
convex hull.  The exact hull-event partition is

\[
 \sum_A p^{h(A)}(1-p)^{q(A)}=1.                      \tag{4}
\]

Write `pi_0(A)=1/V` for the uniform law on all convex faces and

\[
 \mu=\mathbb E_{\pi_0}h,qquad
 \bar q=\mathbb E_{\pi_0}q,qquad s=\mu+\bar q.
\]

Equation (4) defines a probability law

\[
 \rho_p(A)=p^{h(A)}(1-p)^{q(A)}.
\]

The following is an exact identity, rather than merely Jensen's inequality.

> **Theorem 1 (all-rank product-hull KL identity).**  For every finite
> planar point set, and in fact every finite convex geometry,
> \[
> \boxed{
> D_2(\pi_0\Vert\rho_p)
> =-\log V-\mu\log p-\bar q\log(1-p).}              \tag{5}
> \]
> At the optimizing value `p*=mu/s`,
> \[
> \boxed{
> \log V=\Psi(\mu,\bar q)
>          -D_2(\pi_0\Vert\rho_{p_*}),}             \tag{6}
> \]
> where
> \[
> \Psi(x,y)=(x+y)H_2\!\left({x\over x+y}\right).
> \]

**Proof.**  Divide the uniform mass `1/V` by the mass in (4), take a
base-two logarithm, and average.  Minimizing the cross-entropy in `p` gives
`p*=mu/(mu+qbar)`.  This proves (5)--(6).  QED.

The fixed-rank optimized hull theorem already in the attack applies (4) to
one rank.  Equation (6) is its all-rank/KL form.  It yields, without first
extracting a rank slice,

\[
 \boxed{\log V\le\Psi(\mu,\bar q)}                  \tag{7}
\]

and the explicit inverse

\[
 \boxed{
 \bar q\ge\mu\left({2^{(\log V)/\mu}\over e}-1\right).}    \tag{8}
\]

Indeed `Psi(mu,q)<=mu log(e(1+q/mu))`.  The KL term in (6) records exactly
how far the uniform face law is from the Bernoulli hull law; it is the slack
discarded by (7).

### Consequence at the hard scales

Put `L=log n` and use the banked lower bound

\[
 \log V\ge {L^2\over4}-E(L),\qquad E(L)=o(L^2).
\]

Then (8) gives

\[
 \log\bar q
 \ge {L^2\over4\mu}-{E(L)\over\mu}+\log\mu-O(1).   \tag{9}
\]

Comparing with the rankwise demand `D_mu=2^(L-mu)` gives the exact main-term
surplus

\[
 {L^2\over4\mu}-(L-\mu)
 ={(\mu-L/2)^2\over\mu}\ge0.                       \tag{10}
\]

Thus a low-mean hard state cannot fail because it lacks exterior labels.
At the unique critical scale `mu=L/2`, (9) still supplies the demand up to
the existing `n^o(1)` error; away from that scale it supplies an exponential
surplus.  This is only an average supply statement.  Routing those labels
to ordinary convex target faces with capped congestion remains the genuine
geometric obstruction.

There is also a direct minimizer-dichotomy consequence.  If the sharp
quadratic mean alternative fails at a configuration, namely

\[
 \log V>{\mu^2\over2},
\]

then (8) forces

\[
 \boxed{
 \bar q>\mu\left({2^{\mu/2}\over e}-1\right).}      \tag{10a}
\]

So a low-mean minimizer can violate QMS only by putting an exponential-in-
its-mean exterior cloud around the typical uniform face.  This converts the
surviving minimizer dichotomy into a concrete planar repair statement: such
a cloud must make enough distinct replacement faces unless its tangent
pockets reuse targets at the full capped-Hall scale.

## 3. Product-hull factorial toggling

Let `T` be a Bernoulli-`p` subset, let

\[
 K=\operatorname{cl}T,\qquad
 H=|\operatorname{ext}K|,\qquad O=|P\setminus K|,
 \qquad a={1-p\over p}.
\]

For a set `X` of labels, call an omitted occurrence **jointly good** if

\[
 X\subseteq P\setminus K
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 X\subseteq\operatorname{ext}(K\cup X).             \tag{11}
\]

Otherwise it is a **bad exterior cluster**.  Let `B_j(p)` be the expected
number of bad exterior `j`-subsets of `T`.

> **Theorem 2 (factorial Efron--toggle identity).**  For every `j>=0`,
> \[
> \boxed{
> \mathbb E_p {O\choose j}
> =a^j\mathbb E_p {H\choose j}+B_j(p),}             \tag{12}
> \]
> where `B_0=B_1=0` and `B_j>=0`.

**Proof.**  Toggle all labels of a jointly good omitted `X` from absent to
present.  The resulting sample has `X` among its hull vertices.  Conversely,
removing any selected set of hull vertices makes every removed vertex lie
outside the remaining hull and is jointly good.  This is a bijection.  Its
Bernoulli probability ratio is `a^j`.  The omitted configurations not in
the bijection are exactly the bad clusters.  QED.

The case `j=1` is the finite Efron identity

\[
 \mathbb E_p O={1-p\over p}\mathbb E_p H.           \tag{13}
\]

At `p=1/2`, (12) gives

\[
 B_2(1/2)={\operatorname{Var}(O)-\operatorname{Var}(H)\over2}. \tag{14}
\]

Geometrically, a bad exterior pair has a unique direction: one point is
swallowed when the other is added to `K`.  Anti-exchange forbids swallowing
in both directions.  Thus `B_2` is exactly the averaged one-point tangent
capture count.

Summing (12) at `p=1/2` yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E 2^O=\mathbb E2^H+\sum_{j\ge2}B_j(1/2).}  \tag{15}
\]

Grouping half-samples by their closed hull gives the two exact face sums

\[
 \mathbb E2^O=\sum_A2^{-h(A)}=Z_P(1/2),\qquad
 \mathbb E2^H=\sum_A2^{-q(A)}.                     \tag{15a}
\]

Thus (15) is the explicit decomposition

\[
 \boxed{
 Z_P(1/2)=\sum_A2^{-q(A)}+\sum_{j\ge2}B_j(1/2).}   \tag{15b}
\]

The first term counts jointly toggleable exterior sets; the second is the
entire rooted-circuit defect.  This is useful even though it is not by itself
an upper bound: the low-`q` tail of the first term and the weighted reuse in
the second term are separate proof obligations.

## 4. Rank three reduces every defect to clusters of size at most four

> **Theorem 3 (relative rooted-cluster reduction).**  Fix a closed set `K`.
> If `X subseteq P-K` is a bad exterior cluster, then it contains a bad
> cluster `Y` with
> \[
> 2\le|Y|\le4.                                      \tag{16}
> \]

**Proof.**  Choose `x in X` which is not extreme in `K union X`.  By planar
Caratheodory, `x` lies in the convex hull of three points from
`K union (X-x)`.  Let `Y` consist of `x` and those witnesses which lie in
`X`.  At least one witness lies in `X`, because `x` is outside the closed
set `K`.  Hence `2<=|Y|<=4`, and `x` is already nonextreme in `K union Y`.
QED.

For a sample `T`, let `b_s(T)` count its bad exterior `s`-subsets.  Covering
each bad set by one witness from (16) gives the rigorous inequality

\[
 \boxed{
 2^{O(T)}-g(T)
 \le\sum_{s=2}^4 b_s(T)2^{O(T)-s},}                \tag{17}
\]

where `g(T)` is the number of jointly good exterior subsets.  Averaging at
one half and applying the toggle bijection gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E2^O-\mathbb E2^H
 \le\mathbb E\sum_{s=2}^4b_s(T)2^{O(T)-s}.}        \tag{18}
\]

This is the cleanest product-hull version of the remaining geometric gate:
control exponentially weighted reuse of relative rooted circuits of orders
2, 3, and 4.  Ordinary first moments, unweighted circuit counts, or a
single-root cyclic formula do not see the factor `2^O`.

The upper endpoint four in (16) is sharp even in planar general position.
Take a triangle and one point strictly inside it, and let `K` be empty.
Every exterior pair and every exterior triple is jointly good, but the full
four-set is bad.  Hence

\[
 b_2(K)=b_3(K)=0,qquad b_4(K)=1.                   \tag{19}
\]

This is an exact counterexample to every pointwise pair-capture or
pair-plus-triple potential.  A viable averaged tangent potential must retain
the rooted quadruple term and its ambient exponential weight.

## 5. What this changes in the live attack

The product-measure route now has a precise division of labor.

1. Equation (8) proves that the hard low-mean branch has enough exterior
   labels on average; scalar label supply is finished.
2. Equations (15)--(18) show that the missing exponential-moment control is
   exactly a weighted bounded-reuse theorem for small relative rooted
   clusters.  Circuit size is bounded, but reuse along large tangent pockets
   is not.
3. The four-point example forbids dropping the genuine rooted-circuit term.
   Long one-sided pockets forbid dropping the weight.  The next proof should
   therefore try a multiroot elimination inequality for
   `sum b_s(T)2^O`, not another first-moment Gordon/Efron calculation.

This is compatible with the current capped-Hall/C4 program: a product-rich
family of rooted clusters should yield cross-splices, while persistent
component surplus must be charged recursively inside the tangent pocket.
