# Erdős 838 prior-art kill-search — 2026-08-12

> Updated 2026-08-13 to include the iterated-blow-up coefficient \(1/2\).

## Verdict

No public source found in this search states the Pascal-row coefficient

\[
\frac{1}{2\ln 2}=0.7213475204\ldots
\],

the stronger central-cell coefficient

\[
1-\frac{1}{4\ln 2}=0.6393262398\ldots,
\]

or the still stronger iterated-blow-up coefficient

\[
\frac12.
\]

The \(1/2\) theorem in proof_blowup_half.md is therefore the current
high-confidence *apparently novel*
quantitative improvement, not yet a claim of exhaustive global novelty. A
MathSciNet citation search and confirmation from an expert in the
Erdős--Szekeres literature remain appropriate before submission.

## Baseline located

With base-2 logarithms, the strongest explicit public window located was

\[
\frac14\leq\liminf
\frac{\log_2 f(N)}{(\log_2N)^2}
\leq\limsup
\frac{\log_2 f(N)}{(\log_2N)^2}
\leq1.
\]

The lower constant follows by combining the modern
`ES(k)=2^{k+o(k)}` theorem with the standard double count. The upper constant
comes from the classical `2^{k-2}`-point construction and counting all subsets
of size at most `log_2 N+O(1)`. The 1978 argument already implies constants
`1/8` and `1`; the `1/4` lower constant is recorded in the April 2026 public
discussion.

The sweep included exact symbolic and decimal searches for all three
constants; phrases involving convex-subset enumeration, caps/cups, Pascal
cells, iterated and lexicographic blow-ups; the original paper's OpenAlex and
zbMATH citation neighborhoods; recent construction papers; the Erdős
Problems thread; blogs; and public GitHub material. Eppstein explains the
same Pascal geometry but does not enumerate caps, cups, or all convex
subsets. Baek--Balko introduce a more general blow-up operation to construct
sets avoiding large convex subsets, but neither their conference text nor
the searchable full-text record gives a total convex-subset count or an
application to problem 838.

There is a close but separate graph analogue: L. A. Székely, *On the number
of homogeneous subgraphs of a graph*, Combinatorica 4 (1984), 363--372,
<https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579149>. Its random-graph upper coefficient
\(0.7214\ldots\) in the paper's natural-log convention converts to \(1/2\)
in the present base-2 normalization. The paper concerns complete and
independent subgraphs of arbitrary graphs, not convex subsets of planar
point sets, and supplies no implication in either direction. It is,
however, a warning that matching the apparent \(1/2\) lower bound may be a
genuinely Ramsey-multiplicity-level problem.

## Log-base warning

If the normalization instead uses natural logarithms, a base-2 coefficient
\(a\) becomes \(a/\ln 2\). Thus the current \(1/2\) coefficient becomes

\[
\frac{1}{2\ln2}=0.7213475204\ldots .
\]
