# A fixed-power EIC split: central downshadows above one half and forward rectangles below

**Date:** 2026-08-14.  All logarithms are base two.

## Verdict

Let `L=log n`, let `r=(alpha+o(1))L`, and put

\[
                         d_0={n\over2^r}=n^{1-\alpha+o(1)}.  \tag{1}
\]

The optimized hull-activity tail does not give all `d_0` marks pointwise;
it gives `D=d_0/n^o(1)` exterior blockers above every unresolved source.
Choose exactly `D` of them.  The fixed-power target is

\[
                    |E|\le n^{o(1)}d_0^{,1-\varepsilon}V(P) \tag{2}
\]

for some fixed `epsilon>0`.

Two rigorous positive regimes are available.

1. For `alpha>=1/2`, a central proper-downshadow Hall map proves (2)
   whenever no central prefix is shared by more than
   `n^o binom(r,floor(r/2)) d_0^(-epsilon)` selected sources.  Failure
   produces one explicit heavy released prefix; there is no diffuse
   collision left.
2. For every fixed `alpha<1/2`, the selected source--blocker graph has
   polynomial average codegree because `D^2/n=n^(1-2alpha-o(1))`.
   If a non-negligible fraction of its codegree **second moment** lies on
   singleton-ear source pairs admitting constant-density forward splices,
   then
   \[
                         |E|\le n^{\alpha+o(1)}V(P),          \tag{3}
   \]
   which implies (2) for every
   \[
                    0<\varepsilon<{1-2\alpha\over1-\alpha}. \tag{4}
   \]

This is a genuine power saving, not merely a coefficient-scale estimate.
The exact residuals are also clear: a heavy central prefix above one half,
or below one half a codegree-square mass dominated by nested/incompatible
rotations.  The latter is the OAI rotation branch.

The statements below are global-EIC theorems.  Proper downfaces and
nonlocal forward targets need not belong to a narrower record-local RPH
neighbourhood.

## 1. Tail and selector quantifiers

Let `S` be a rank-`r` unresolved source family.  The optimized activity
tail supplies, for a single factor `K_0=n^o(1)`, at least

\[
                           D=\left\lfloor{d_0\over K_0}\right\rfloor \tag{5}
\]

eligible exterior blocked labels above every member of `S`.  Discard a
subexponential fringe if necessary and select exactly `D` distinct labels
above every source.  The resulting incidence graph `G subset S times P`
is left `D`-regular and

\[
                              |E|=D|S|.                       \tag{6}
\]

All estimates will be stated using `D`.  Since
`D=d_0/n^o(1)`, replacing `D` by `d_0` changes only the displayed
`n^o(1)` factors.  No estimate below assumes that all exterior incidences
are selected.

## 2. The high-rank central-downshadow branch

For `t=floor(r/2)`, let

\[
 \Lambda_t=\max_{|Q|=r-t}|\{A\in S:Q\subseteq A\}|.          \tag{7}
\]

Every record above `A` is joined to all `binom(r,t)` proper downfaces of
`A`.  The duplicated-Hall proof in `CROSS_CORE_DOWNSHADOW.md` gives a
global map from selected records to ordinary faces of load

\[
                       K_t\le\left\lceil
                       {D\Lambda_t\over\binom rt}\right\rceil. \tag{8}
\]

> **Theorem 1 (central-prefix fixed saving).**  Fix `epsilon>0`.  If
> \[
>             \Lambda_t\le n^{o(1)}{\binom rt\over d_0^\varepsilon}, \tag{9}
> \]
> then
> \[
>                         |E|\le n^{o(1)}d_0^{1-\varepsilon}V(P). \tag{10}
> \]

**Proof.**  Substitute `D<=d_0` and (9) in (8), and count the fibres of
the Hall map.  QED.

Since

\[
              {r\choose\lfloor r/2\rfloor}=2^{r-o(r)}
                         =n^{\alpha-o(1)},                    \tag{11}
\]

condition (9) is

\[
             \Lambda_t\le n^{\alpha-\varepsilon(1-\alpha)+o(1)}. \tag{12}
\]

For `alpha>=1/2` and sufficiently small fixed `epsilon`, this permits a
polynomial number of cores above every prefix.  If it fails, one obtains a
specific `(r-t)`-face `Q` contained in more than the right side of (12)
sources.  All remaining source labels lie in the released `t`-sets
`A-Q`, which are ordinary convex faces.  Thus the exact heavy alternative
is to charge the complete face complex generated by those released labels,
not to repeat the same rank-`t` shadow at every core.

The theorem actually applies for every `alpha`; `alpha>=1/2` is the range
where the central Boolean reservoir is at least the cap up to
subexponential factors.  For `alpha<1/2`, even one source has
`2^r<d_0`, so a core-downface-only proof cannot give a fixed saving across
the full cap.

## 3. Codegree entry below one half

Now assume every selected repair hides a singleton.  Write the source
indexed by `a` as

\[
                         A_a=R_a\cup\{x_a\}.                  \tag{13}
\]

Let `N(a)` be its `D` selected blockers.  For ordered distinct sources put

\[
                 Q_{ab}=N(a)\cap N(b),\qquad q_{ab}=|Q_{ab}|. \tag{14}
\]

Assume the pair is separated:

\[
                      x_b\notin R_a,\qquad x_a\notin R_b.    \tag{15}
\]

For this pair define the two forward compatibility sets

\[
\begin{split}
 C_a(ab)&=\{p\in Q_{ab}:R_a\cup\{p,x_b\}\in\mathcal F(P)\},\\
 C_b(ab)&=\{p\in Q_{ab}:R_b\cup\{p,x_a\}\in\mathcal F(P)\}.
\end{split}                                                   \tag{16}
\]

The singleton repair-rectangle forward-splice theorem gives the global
inequality

\[
 \sum_{a\ne b}
 \bigl(|C_a(ab)||C_b(ab)|-|C_a(ab)\cap C_b(ab)|\bigr)
                 \le(r+1)^4V(P)^2.                           \tag{17}
\]

An ordered pair is called `(tau)`-forward if it satisfies (15) and

\[
                  |C_a(ab)|,|C_b(ab)|\ge\tau q_{ab}.          \tag{18}
\]

Let `H` be a collection of such pairs and suppose it carries an `eta`
fraction of the codegree second moment:

\[
                \sum_{(a,b)\in H}q_{ab}^2
                   \ge\eta\sum_{a\ne b}q_{ab}^2.             \tag{19}

\]

> **Theorem 2 (dense forward-codegree fixed saving).**  Suppose
> `|S|D>=2n`, `D^2/n>=8/(eta tau^2)`, and (19) holds.  Then
> \[
>       |E|=D|S|\le
>       {4(r+1)^2\over\tau\sqrt\eta}\,{n\over D},V(P).      \tag{20}
> \]

**Proof.**  Put `s=|S|` and

\[
 Q_1=\sum_{a\ne b}q_{ab},\qquad
 Q_2=\sum_{a\ne b}q_{ab}^2.                                 \tag{21}
\]

If `d_p` is the selected degree of blocker `p`, then Cauchy--Schwarz and
`sD>=2n` give

\[
 Q_1=\sum_p d_p(d_p-1)
     \ge{(sD)^2\over n}-sD
     \ge{s^2D^2\over2n}.                                    \tag{22}
\]

A second Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives

\[
                 Q_2\ge{Q_1^2\over s(s-1)}
                       \ge{s^2D^4\over4n^2}.                 \tag{23}
\]

Every forward pair contributes at least
`tau^2 q_ab^2-q_ab` to (17).  The negative terms obey

\[
 \sum_{(a,b)\in H}q_{ab}\le Q_1
       \le s\sqrt{Q_2}.                                     \tag{24}
\]

Also (22)--(23) imply `sqrt(Q_2)>=sD^2/(2n)`.  The hypotheses on
`D^2/n`, `eta`, and `tau` therefore ensure

\[
 \tau^2\sum_Hq_{ab}^2-\sum_Hq_{ab}
   \ge\eta\tau^2Q_2-s\sqrt{Q_2}
   \ge{\eta\tau^2\over2}Q_2.                                \tag{25}

Combining (17), (23), and (25), then taking square roots, yields (20)
(with slack in the displayed constant).  QED.

For fixed `alpha<1/2`, equation (5) gives

\[
 {D^2\over n}=n^{1-2\alpha-o(1)}\longrightarrow\infty.      \tag{26}
\]

If `eta^(-1),tau^(-1)=n^o(1)`, Theorem 2 becomes

\[
                         |E|\le n^{\alpha+o(1)}V(P).          \tag{27}
\]

For every epsilon satisfying (4),

\[
 n^\alpha\le n^{o(1)}d_0^{1-\varepsilon},                   \tag{28}
\]

which proves the fixed-power target (2).

If `|S|D<2n`, then `|E|<2n`.  The established universal
`V(P)>=2^{(1/4-o(1))L^2}` bound dominates every fixed power of `n`, so this
small-demand branch satisfies (2) for all sufficiently large `n`.  It is
kept separate above only because it does not enter the codegree moments.

## 4. What failure means: the rotation branch

Theorem 2 does not assert that (19) is automatic.  Its negation has exact
geometric content.  A substantial fraction of `Q_2` then lies on source
pairs for which at least one of the two directions in (16) contains fewer
than `tau q_ab` compatible blockers.  For each incompatible blocker `p`,

\[
               T=R_a\cup\{p\}\quad\hbox{is a face, but}\quad
               T\cup\{x_b\}\quad\hbox{is not}.              \tag{29}
\]

If `x_b` is exterior to `conv(T)`, this is a new monotone exterior rotation;
if it is interior, it is a strict containment child.  Thus failure is not
an arbitrary dense graph: it is precisely a codegree-square mass of nested
rotation children.  Paying those children without reusing the same released
prefix complex is the remaining OAI theorem.

At `alpha=1/2`, `D^2/n=n^o(1)` need not grow, so the codegree entry gives no
fixed saving; this is the honest seam between Theorems 1 and 2.  The central
downshadow theorem handles the light-prefix part at the seam, but a heavy
prefix still needs released-complex accounting.

## 5. Scope and local/global warning

Theorem 1 uses arbitrary proper downfaces of the actual source.  Theorem 2
uses the nonlocal cross-source faces in (16).  Both are legitimate global
ordinary faces, hence legitimate for EIC.  Neither theorem proves a local
RPH statement whose neighbourhood is required to remain inside one guarded
core.  Linear shield examples show that such local mixed faces can have
quadratic fibres even while a huge unrestricted shield-face bank pays the
global inequality.

The result is therefore a rigorous partial split, not a proof of EIC'.  It
reduces a fixed-power counterexample to one of two sharply stated atoms:

\[
 \boxed{\begin{array}{l}
 \alpha\ge1/2:\ \text{a central prefix with multiplicity above (12);}\quad\\
 \alpha<1/2:\ \text{nested/incompatible pairs carrying most of }Q_2.
 \end{array}}                                               \tag{30}
\]
