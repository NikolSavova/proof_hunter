# Almost-full base words cannot absorb a central detached shield

**Date:** 2026-08-15.  All logarithms are base two.  This attacks the
endpoint left by `DETACHED_BOOLEAN_SHIELD_CONTEXT_REUSE_GATE.md`: retain
all but (O(loglog n)) of the base roles in the detached output.

## Verdict

The proposed almost-full-word mixed bank is false.  There is a scalable
rational construction with

* (q=2k=Theta(log n)) base roles of alphabet size (D), hence
  (M=D^q=2^{Theta((log n)^2)}) actual convex base words;
* an arbitrary prescribed rational low-face child (Y); and
* a family (mathcal J) of ordinary detached faces of (Y), losing only
  a quadratic factor from the whole child face complex,

such that

[
 Fcup S	ext{ is nonconvex for every }Finmathcal J              \tag{1}
]

whenever the partial base transversal (S) contains one label from each
of the two base arcs.  In particular,

[
              |S|>q/2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Fcup S
              \text{ is nonconvex}.                              \tag{2}
]

Thus no output retaining (q-O(loglog n)) roles exists at all once
(qggloglog n).  Every successful deletion mask must erase one whole
side.  The erased side is a single consecutive run of length (q/2), so
the long-run alternative is present in its strongest form.

The run's Boolean history does not repair the context load.  Across all
(D^q) base words, the distinct partial transversals number only

[
                              (D+1)^q,                            \tag{3}
]

not (D^q2^q).  A partial transversal occupying (t) named roles is
generated by exactly (D^{q-t}) full words.  The union of both one-sided
run banks has only

[
                           2(D+1)^{q/2}-1                          \tag{4}
]

base states.  Even if every one-sided union with every (Finmathcal J)
were ordinary, that operation could produce at most

[
                    |mathcal J|\,[2(D+1)^{q/2}-1]                \tag{5}
]

outputs, rather than (M|mathcal J|).  For
(D=Theta(n/q)),

[
 { (D+1)^qover D^q}=(1+1/D)^q=1+o(1),                           \tag{6}
]

and the one-sided bank retains only half of the quadratic base entropy.
At coefficients (a=c=1/4), its optimistic coefficient is
(c+a/2=3/8), not (1/2).

This is an exact extension of the central root-bad ramp: the central child
need not be a convex arc.  A fixed pair belongs to at least

[
                  {V(Y)-|Y|-1\over {|Y|\choose2}}                 \tag{7}
]

ordinary faces, and an affine embedding turns that pair into the common
central circuit without changing any of those faces.  Hence arbitrary
projectively universal low-face children preserve the barrier at
quadratic-logarithmic coefficient scale.

The construction is not a global sub-half example.  Other directional
child profiles and other occupied masks may create additional faces.  It
does prove that the quantified endpoint cannot be closed by
almost-full-word retention or by quoting the exact long-run Boolean bank.
The remaining positive operation must multiply the two one-sided banks,
or charge the common central (1+3) circuit family globally.

## 1. Quadratic-entropy convex base words

Let (q=2k).  Use the ordered macro roles

[
                 I={-k,ldots,-1,1,ldots,k}                    \tag{8}
]

at the rational parabola points

[
                              z_i=(i,i^2).                         \tag{9}
]

Every subset of the macro points is in convex position.  Around each
(z_i), choose a sufficiently small disjoint rational neighbourhood and
place a (D)-point role cell (Z_i).  The order type inside each cell may
be prescribed arbitrarily.  The cells can be shrunk so that every partial
transversal

[
                    S\subseteq\bigcup_iZ_i,
                    \qquad |S\cap Z_i|\le1                       \tag{10}
]

is in convex position.

Indeed, for every fixed macro support of rank at least three, strict
convexity is an open collection of orientation inequalities.  There are
finitely many supports and transversal choices.  Shrink the cells until
all these inequalities have their macro signs; ranks zero, one, and two
are automatic.  A generic rational choice inside the resulting open
cells gives ambient general position while preserving every prescribed
local order type.

In particular every full word

[
                       B_\omega=\{z_{i,\omega_i}:i\in I\},
                       \qquad\omega\in[D]^q                         \tag{11}
]

is ordinary, and the role labels recover (omega).  Hence there are
exactly (M=D^q) selected base contexts.

## 2. A large pair-star in every child face complex

Let (Y) be any (s)-point rational general-position order type and let
(mathcal F(Y)) include the empty face.  Put (H=V(Y)).  Count incidences
between unordered pairs of labels and ordinary faces of rank at least two:

[
 \sum_{\{o,p\}\in{Y\choose2}}
       |\{F\in\mathcal F(Y):\{o,p\}\subseteq F\}|
   =\sum_{F\in\mathcal F(Y)}{|F|\choose2}
   \ge H-s-1.                                             \tag{12}
]

Therefore some pair ({o,p}) has the coface family

[
 \mathcal J=\{F\in\mathcal F(Y):\{o,p\}\subseteq F\},
 \qquad
 |mathcal J|\ge {H-s-1\over{s\choose2}}.                 \tag{13}
]

This costs only (O(log s)) in logarithmic face count.  In particular,
if (H=2^{(c-o(1))(log s)^2}), then (mathcal J) has the same
coefficient (c).

Apply an invertible rational affine map to (Y) so that

[
                         o=(x_0,-1),\qquad
                         p=(x_0,-1+\eta)                 \tag{14}
]

for a sufficiently small rational (eta>0), and the whole image lies in
a small neighbourhood of ((x_0,-1)).  This is always possible: send the
nonzero vector (p-o) to the positive vertical direction and then scale
the finite configuration.  Affine maps preserve the complete labelled
face complex, hence preserve (mathcal J).

Choose (x_0) near zero and shrink all cells so that for every

[
                    a\in\bigcup_{i<0}Z_i,
                    \qquad b\in\bigcup_{i>0}Z_i                      \tag{15}
]

one has the strict containment

[
                              p\in\operatorname{int}
                              \operatorname{conv}\{o,a,b\}.       \tag{16}
]

At the macro limit this is immediate: the vertical ray upward from
((0,-1)) lies strictly inside the angle between every vector to a left
parabola point and every vector to a right parabola point.  Take (eta)
smaller than the minimum intersection distance with the finitely many
opposite sides.  Condition (16) is open, so it survives the role-cell and
general-position perturbations.

## 3. Almost-full coexistence is impossible

Take (Finmathcal J) and a partial transversal (S).  If (S) meets
both base arcs, choose (a,b) as in (15).  Since (o,pin F), equation
(16) says

[
             p\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}
                    ((F\cup S)\setminus\{p\}).                    \tag{17}
]

Thus (Fcup S) is nonconvex, proving (1).  Each side contains only (k)
roles.  Therefore any partial transversal of rank greater than (k=q/2)
meets both sides, proving (2).

Equivalently, a deletion mask which leaves a possibly admissible union
must contain all (k) roles of the left arc or all (k) roles of the
right arc.  In the cyclic order inherited from the parabola polygon, each
arc is one consecutive block.  Hence every such mask contains a deleted
run of length at least (q/2).  This is stronger than the
(Omega(q/\log q)) run supplied by the abstract mask-run lemma.

The obstruction is one fixed rooted (1+3) circuit class: its two child
marks (o,p) are common, while the two outer circuit points range over the
two physical base arcs.  There is no uncertainty about seams, jets, or the
first bad reset.

## 4. Exact overlap of the run Boolean bank

For one fixed full word, all (2^q) masks are ordinary base subfaces.  But
this is a history count, not a distinct-face count.  A partial transversal
with occupied role set (T) and (|T|=t) fixes exactly (t) word
coordinates and is contained in exactly

[
                              D^{q-t}                              \tag{18}
]

full words.  Consequently

[
 \sum_{T\subseteq[q]}D^{|T|}=(D+1)^q                              \tag{19}
]

is both the number of distinct partial transversals and the quotient of
the (M2^q) word-mask incidences by their exact variable loads.

If the left side is completely deleted, the retained right-side state has
((D+1)^k) possibilities; the symmetric bank has the same size, and the
empty state is shared.  This proves (4).  Appending a recoverable child
face (F) can multiply this by at most (|mathcal J|), proving (5), even
under the optimistic assumption that every candidate union is ordinary.

At the live scaling (q=\rho\log n+o(\log n)) and
(D=\Theta(n/q)),

[
 \log (D+1)^q-log D^q
       =q\log(1+1/D)=o(1).                              \tag{20}
]

Thus the full mask cube supplies no
(n^{\Theta(\log\log n)}) multiplier.  Restricting to the necessary long
run loses half the base exponent:

[
        \log[2(D+1)^{q/2}-1]
                    ={1\over2}\log M+o((\log n)^2).     \tag{21}
]

This is precisely the source--profile anti-aligned ramp rather than an
almost-full-word release.

## 5. Scope and next operation

The theorem is a sharp kill of the almost-full-word endpoint, including
its most favorable run structure.  It also preserves arbitrary low-face
children in every base role and in the detached central block.

It does not upper-bound all faces of the assembled configuration.
Directional subfamilies of (mathcal J) may coexist with one base arc,
and other masks may expose additional composition banks.  Even granting
all one-sided candidates gives only (5).  Therefore a coefficient-half
closure must do something not present in the endpoint proposal:

* multiply recoverable left- and right-directional child profiles;
* release the common central circuit into a globally bounded-load shield;
  or
* charge an ambient composition bank outside the selected
  source--shield records.
