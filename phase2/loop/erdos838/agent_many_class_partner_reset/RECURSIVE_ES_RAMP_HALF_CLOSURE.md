# Recursive realization of the reset ramp: an exact half barrier

**Date:** 2026-08-15. All logarithms are base two. This report concerns
only the construction class generated by rational high-left/low-right
separated composition. It does not prove the unrestricted Erdős problem.

## Verdict

The scalar ramp from `ARBITRARY_CHILD_ES_POWER_PROFILE_BELLMAN.md` cannot
be turned into a coefficient-below-$1/2$ construction by recursively
realizing its children with nonstationary $E(r,s)$ cells, reflected cells,
or perfect-reset $E(7,7)$ modules.

The reason is exact and geometric. Every such substitution is obtained by
grafting ordered binary strong-decomposition trees. The grafted object is
again one ordered strong tree, and the audited weighted one-turn theorem
gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \log V(P)\ge {1\over2}(\log |P|)^2
                   -O((\log |P|)^{3/2}).}            \tag{1}
\]

This includes arbitrary depth-dependent choices of $(r,s)$, arbitrary
reflections, different modules in all $252^h$ leaves, and the complete
many-level endpoint ramp. Hence no member of this recursive template class
has limiting coefficient below $1/2$.

The lower coefficient is sharp for the class. Iterating a fixed balanced
$E(k,k)$ macro gives the exact tropical coefficient

\[
 \kappa_k={k-2\over
       \log {2k-4\choose k-2}}>{1\over2},
 \qquad \kappa_k\longrightarrow {1\over2}.          \tag{2}
\]

Thus the construction-side closure test returns an **exact obstruction**,
not a disproof. The scalar Bellman ramp was not a false calculation: it
forgets the internal $V,C,U$ payment of the child modules. Once those
children are themselves recursively separated, repeated anti-alignment
either resets a persistent endpoint coordinate or creates an exponentially
large pure cup/cap comb. Those are precisely the two branches behind (1).

An actual sub-half realization of the reset ramp would therefore require
genuinely non-strong-decomposable child order types. Nonstationary Pascal
cells and perfect-reset powers do not suffice.

## 1. Exact profile and support recurrences

For a rational separated composition $T=A\searrow B$, let $n,C,U,V$
denote size, nonempty caps, nonempty cups, and nonempty convex-position
subsets. The mixed triple signs give

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
n(T)&=n(A)+n(B),\\
C(T)&=C(A)(1+n(B))+C(B),\\
U(T)&=U(A)+U(B)(1+n(A)),\\
V(T)&=V(A)+V(B)+C(A)U(B).
\end{aligned}}                                      \tag{3}
\]

These are equalities. A spanning convex subset has a cap trace in $A$ and
a cup trace in $B$; conversely every such pair is convex. The rational
anisotropic insertion used by the reset realizes the two mixed sign rules
at every graft.

The rank state closes in parallel:

\[
\begin{aligned}
a(T)&=\max\{a(A)+1,a(B)\},\\
b(T)&=\max\{b(A),b(B)+1\},\\
v(T)&=\max\{v(A),v(B),a(A)+b(B)\}.                 \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Here $a,b,v$ are maximum cap, cup, and ordinary-face support sizes. For the
cup--cap module

\[
 E(r,s)=E(r,s-1)\searrow E(r-1,s),                 \tag{5}
\]

equation (4) gives exactly

\[
 |E(r,s)|={r+s-4\choose r-2},\quad
 a=s-1,\quad b=r-1,\quad v=r+s-4.                 \tag{6}
\]

In particular the $h$-fold perfect-reset macro has

\[
 |B^h|=252^h,\qquad a=b=5h+1,\qquad v=10h.        \tag{7}
\]

This is the additive-rank correction to the earlier loose $10^h$ audit.

For a fixed $m$-point macro skeleton $S$ and a common $N$-point child $Q$,
flattening the endpoint classification gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(S[Q])&=C(Q)A_S(N),\\
U(S[Q])&=U(Q)B_S(N),\\
V(S[Q])&=mV(Q)+C(Q)U(Q)F_S(N),                    \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]

where $A_S,B_S,F_S$ are nonnegative integer polynomials with

\[
 \deg A_S=a(S)-1,\qquad
 \deg B_S=b(S)-1,\qquad
 \deg F_S=v(S)-2.                                \tag{9}
\]

The constant term of $F_S$ is positive because every macro pair is a
convex support. Equations (8)--(9) retain all three enumerators and the
support sizes requested by the closure test.

## 2. Stationary tropical fixed point

Iterate a fixed macro $S$ from a singleton and write
$L_d=\log |S^d|=d\ell$, where $\ell=\log m$. From (8),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log C_d&={a-1\over2}\ell d^2+O_S(d),\\
\log U_d&={b-1\over2}\ell d^2+O_S(d),\\
\log V_d&={a+b-2\over2}\ell d^2+O_S(d).           \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]

The $v-2$ support factor in $F_S$ is linear in the current logarithmic
size, so it changes only the lower-order term; the accumulated endpoint
profiles determine the quadratic fixed point. Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 (c,u,w)=
 \left({a-1\over2\ell},
       {b-1\over2\ell},
       {a+b-2\over2\ell}\right)}                 \tag{11}
\]

after normalization by $L_d^2$.

The elementary cup--cap theorem gives

\[
 m\le {a+b-2\choose a-1}\le2^{a+b-2}.             \tag{12}
\]

Consequently $w\ge1/2$ for every stationary macro. For $E(k,k)$, (11)
is exactly (2). For a perfect-reset macro $B^h$, it is

\[
 w={10h\over2\log(252^h)}={5\over\log252}
   =0.62678\ldots,                                 \tag{13}
\]

independent of $h$. Taking deeper reset powers does not approach the half
barrier; taking larger balanced $E(k,k)$ macros does.

## 3. Nonstationary tropical ledger

For a homogeneous but depth-dependent sequence of macros $S_j$, put

\[
 \ell_j=\log |S_j|,\qquad L_j=\sum_{q\le j}\ell_q,
\]

and let $a_j,b_j,v_j$ be their three support ranks. Define the exact
coefficient corrections by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_j(z)&=\log A_{S_j}(2^z)-(a_j-1)z,\\
\beta_j(z)&=\log B_{S_j}(2^z)-(b_j-1)z,\\
\phi_j(z)&=\log F_{S_j}(2^z)-(v_j-2)z.
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{14}
\]

Nonnegative coefficients and a positive leading coefficient give

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\le\alpha_j(z)\le\log C(S_j),\\
0&\le\beta_j(z)\le\log U(S_j),\\
0&\le\phi_j(z)\le\log V(S_j),
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{15}
\]

with the harmless convention that the last line is omitted for a
one-point macro. If lower-case letters denote logarithms of the three
child profiles, (8) gives the finite exact ledger

\[
\begin{aligned}
c_j&=c_{j-1}+(a_j-1)L_{j-1}+\alpha_j(L_{j-1}),\\
u_j&=u_{j-1}+(b_j-1)L_{j-1}+\beta_j(L_{j-1}),\\
2^{w_j}&=2^{w_{j-1}+\ell_j}+
 2^{c_{j-1}+u_{j-1}+(v_j-2)L_{j-1}+\phi_j(L_{j-1})}.
                                                               \tag{16}
\end{aligned}
\]

The cup--cap constraint at every depth says

\[
                 a_j+b_j-2\ge\ell_j.              \tag{17}
\]

Summing the first two lines yields the product-mass floor

\[
 c_d+u_d\ge
 {1\over2}\left(L_d^2-\sum_{j\le d}\ell_j^2\right).
                                                    \tag{18}
\]

If the mesh $\max_j\ell_j/L_d$ vanishes, (18) already has coefficient
$1/2$. What it does not by itself control is the orientation of the product
used in the last line of (16). That alignment issue is exactly what the
all-tree theorem resolves, including macroscopic and fully heterogeneous
jumps.

## 4. Why every recursive ramp is one strong tree

Expand every $E(r,s)$ module by (5), rather than treating it as an opaque
macro. Reflecting a module swaps the cap/cup labels but leaves it an ordered
strong tree after reversing the local chart: concretely, a half-turn
reverses the $x$-order, swaps the two children at every node, and preserves
the high-left/low-right separated rule. Replacing a leaf by another expanded
module simply grafts its root at that leaf. Hence:

> **Grafting lemma.** Any finite construction obtained from singletons by
> arbitrary rational separated compositions, arbitrary $E(r,s)$ macros,
> reflected macros, perfect-reset $E(7,7)$ powers, and arbitrary recursive
> substitution among them expands to one ordered full binary strong tree.

This includes different children in every leaf of the 252-point reset. The
reset matchings are additional marked bad circuits; they do not alter the
ordinary-face signs or remove any subsets counted by the expanded tree.

## 5. The all-tree half obstruction

For completeness, here is the mechanism of the weighted one-turn theorem
specialized to this construction class. For a subtree $R$, let $X_R$ be
the largest number of caps with a common left endpoint, $Y_R$ the reflected
common-right cup count, and $M_R$ the largest compatible endpoint product.
At $R=A\searrow B$, with child sizes $a,b$, the endpoint classification is
the exact max-plus state

\[
\begin{aligned}
X_R&=\max\{(b+1)X_A,X_B\},\\
Y_R&=\max\{Y_A,(a+1)Y_B\},\\
M_R&=\max\{M_A,M_B,X_AY_B\}.                      \tag{19}
\end{aligned}
\]

Moreover

\[
 M_R\le V(R)\le2|R|^2\max\{1,M_R\}.              \tag{20}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz in the exact $C,U$ recurrences, followed down a larger-child
path, gives for every $s$-leaf subtree

\[
 C(R)U(R)\ge2^{\frac12(\log s)^2-\log s}.          \tag{21}
\]

Since $C\le sX$ and $U\le sY$, writing $x=\log X$, $y=\log Y$ gives

\[
                    x+y\ge {1\over2}(\log s)^2-3\log s. \tag{22}
\]

Now follow a larger child from the root, stopping after $4\sqrt L$ bits of
size loss, where $L=\log n$. If fewer than $\sqrt L$ discarded siblings
have relative size at least $L^{-2}$, there are
$\Omega(L^{5/2})$ same-side tiny siblings. Choosing or omitting one fixed
point in each gives that many independent pure cup/cap bits, far exceeding
(1).

Otherwise there are $\sqrt L$ nested nodes whose two children all have
log-size $L-O(\sqrt L)$. Equation (20) supplies nearly $L^2/2$ endpoint
product mass in both children. If the forward product at the deepest node
is small, its two reverse endpoint coordinates become large. At every
later nested attachment, (17) either pays a large forward product or
increases one persistent coordinate. One attachment direction occurs at
least half the time, so after $\Omega(\sqrt L)$ resets,

\[
       \log M_R\ge {1\over2}L^2-O(L^{3/2}).          \tag{23}
\]

Together with (20), this proves (1). The argument is insensitive to module
stationarity, balance, reflection, or the number of distinct endpoint
profiles. It is exactly the missing global constraint in the scalar ramp
LP.

## 6. Consequences for the perfect-reset branch

1. **No recursive sub-half child bank.** If every $D$-point child inserted
   into the perfect reset is made from $E(r,s)$ or other separated modules,
   then the full substituted configuration satisfies (1). It is impossible
   for all local, mixed, and representative banks to remain sub-half.
2. **The scalar Bellman remains a valid relaxation.** Its coherent ordered
   potentials can suppress the outer $K_{ij}C_iU_j$ terms at only
   $O(L\log L)$ cost, but recursively realizing those potentials creates
   internal $V$ mass that the scalar state did not charge.
3. **One/two/many profiles make no difference recursively.** The finite
   profile pigeonhole payments in the preceding report are weaker than
   (1) once every profile comes with its actual strong-tree history.
4. **Exact surviving barrier.** Any construction-side continuation must use
   child order types with no near-spanning strong-decomposition history.
   Merely varying Pascal densities, alternating reflections, or nesting the
   252-point reset cannot evade the half coefficient.

This closes the construction-side recursive-template residue without
claiming global closure of Erdős 838.
