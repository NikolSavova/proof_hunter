# Erdős 838: strong-glue recurrence transfer search

**Sweep date:** 2026-08-13. This is an agent-only report. All asymptotic
logarithms are base (2).

## Bottom line

There is one clean exact transfer, but it is to **leaf-induced patterns in a
rooted ordered binary tree**, not to cograph/series-parallel graph
polynomials:

* (C(T)) counts leaf subsets whose reduced induced ordered tree is a left
  comb;
* (U(T)) counts those whose reduced induced ordered tree is a right comb;
* apart from the singleton, (W(T)) counts those whose reduced induced
  ordered tree has a left-comb left branch and a right-comb right branch.

The last family is called a **left-right comb** in some binary-tree papers and
is one of the one-turn/path-tree families. This exactly explains

\[
C=C_B+(b+1)C_A,\qquad
U=U_A+(a+1)U_B,\qquad
W=W_A+W_B+C_AU_B.
\]

I found **no theorem** in cographs, series-parallel posets, separable
permutations, plane-tree pattern containment, or phylogenetic inducibility
that implies either

\[
W(T)\ge 2^{(1/2-o(1))(\log n)^2}
\quad\text{or}\quad
C(T)U(T)\le W(T)n^{o(\log n)}.
\]

Moreover, an exact clique/independence-polynomial identification on the leaf
ground set is impossible: these cap/cup/convex complexes are non-flag, whereas
clique and independence complexes of graphs are flag. The nearest serious
prior art is Dossou-Olory's exact minimum-density theorem for **unordered
rooted caterpillar** restrictions. It has a formula whose purely formal
growing-(k) optimization gives coefficient (1/2), but the theorem is for
fixed (k), its quantitative error is useless at (k\asymp\log n), and its
pattern has its root at an endpoint of the spine, whereas the nontrivial
patterns counted by (W) generally have their root at the turn, in the
interior of the spine. It is not a valid transfer.

## 1. Exact ordered-tree interpretation

Let (T) be the ordered full binary strong-decomposition tree and, for a
nonempty leaf set (S), let (T\vert S) be the minimal rooted ordered subtree
spanning (S), with every unary vertex suppressed. The root of (T\vert S)
is the lowest common ancestor of (S).

Write (L_k) and (R_k) for the (k)-leaf left and right combs. Then an
induction at (T=A\prec B) gives:

\[
\begin{aligned}
S\text{ is a cap}
&\iff T\vert S\cong L_{|S|},\\
S\text{ is a cup}
&\iff T\vert S\cong R_{|S|},\\
S\text{ is convex}
&\iff |S|=1\ \text{ or }\ T\vert S\cong L_p\wedge R_q
   \quad\text{for some }p,q\ge1, p+q=|S|.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here (X\wedge Y) denotes the ordered tree with left branch (X) and right
branch (Y). Indeed, a cap meeting both root branches consists of a cap in
(A) and zero or one leaf of (B); a cup is the reflected statement; and a
convex set meeting both branches consists of a cap in (A) and a cup in
(B). If the set lies in one branch, its LCA moves into that branch and the
same characterization recurses.

Thus (W) is the number of leaf-induced **left-right combs**, summed over all
sizes and all possible turn positions. Equivalently these are ordered thin
trees whose spine direction changes at most once. The exact shape is standard:

* Anoop--Sarma call a subtree whose left branch is a left comb and right branch
  a right comb the
  ["left-right-comb property"](https://fi.episciences.org/13643/pdf#page=16).
* Pilaud--Santos--Ziegler identify precisely these trees as the (n) common
  vertices of Loday's associahedron and its parallelepiped
  ([survey, p. 6, item (3)](https://www.ub.edu/comb/vincentpilaud/documents/publications/PilaudSantosZiegler_CelebratingLodaysAssociahedron.pdf#page=6)).
* The older parse-tree literature calls the corresponding word
  (L^mR^r) a **LeftTurnTree**, a two-parameter path-tree family
  ([Oberwolfach report, Theorems 34--37](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46296)).

None of these sources studies the minimum number of leaf subsets inducing
this growing family in an arbitrary fixed ordered tree.

It is also useful to retain the polynomial form. If size is marked by (x),
with the empty set omitted, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_T(x)&=C_B(x)+(1+bx)C_A(x),\\
U_T(x)&=U_A(x)+(1+ax)U_B(x),\\
W_T(x)&=W_A(x)+W_B(x)+C_A(x)U_B(x).
\end{aligned}
\]

The factors (1+bx) and (1+ax) encode the condition "choose at most one
leaf from the opposite branch." This higher-order conditional restriction is
exactly what ordinary graph composition cannot encode.

## 2. Why this is not a cograph clique/independence polynomial

For graphs (G,H), clique polynomials (including the empty clique) obey

\[
K_{G\sqcup H}=K_G+K_H-1,\qquad K_{G\vee H}=K_GK_H,
\]

and independence polynomials obey the two formulas with union and join
interchanged. Cographs are generated by precisely these two operations. The
strong-glue factors ((b+1)) and ((a+1)), and especially their directional
use, are neither operation.

There is also a structural impossibility proof, independent of recurrence
matching. Every two-leaf subset is a cap, a cup, and convex. Yet there are
strong-glue trees with a three-leaf subset that is a cup but not a cap, and a
four-leaf subset that is not convex. Therefore:

* if cap sets were graph cliques, every pair being a cap would force the graph
  to be complete and hence every triple to be a cap;
* if cap sets were graph independent sets, every pair being a cap would force
  the graph to be empty and again every triple would be a cap;
* the identical pair/four-set argument excludes graph clique or independence
  representations of convex subsets.

In simplicial-complex language, the families are downward closed but are not
flag complexes: their minimal nonfaces can have size (3) (caps/cups) or
(4) (convexity). Graph clique and independence complexes are flag. This
rules out any same-ground-set cograph transfer, not merely the most obvious
choice of cotree.

There is an exact **3-uniform hypergraph** description for (C) and (U):
cap sets are homogeneous/independent sets for one orientation class of
triples, and cup sets for the other. At a glue, the new forbidden cap triples
are (A\times\binom{B}{2}), with the reflected rule for cups. This recovers
the factors (b+1) and (a+1), but it is just the original rank-three
structure in new language and supplies no graph theorem.

## 3. Series-parallel posets and separable permutations: same mismatch

The natural enumerators on series-parallel posets have the same two semiring
rules as cographs. Under parallel versus ordinal composition, chain and
antichain polynomials are respectively sum-minus-one or product. Thus they do
not produce an asymmetric "arbitrary valid subset on one side, at most one
point on the other" rule.

Likewise, if (pi\oplus\sigma) and (pi\ominus\sigma) are direct and skew
sums of permutations, the increasing-subsequence polynomial is product for a
direct sum and sum-minus-one for a skew sum; the decreasing-subsequence
polynomial has the roles reversed. This is the well-known cotree/separating-
tree analogy between cographs and separable permutations, but it does not
match the strong-glue recurrence. The literature does study increasing
subsequences in random separable permutations (for example Bassino et al.,
[primary arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07444)), not a deterministic
left-turn-subsequence minimum of the required strength.

## 4. The closest near-hit: minimum density of binary caterpillars

Dossou-Olory proved an exact minimum asymptotic density for the unordered
rooted (k)-leaf binary caterpillar (F_k^2). In the binary case the value is

\[
\delta_k=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\min_{|T|=n}
\frac{c(F_k^2,T)}{\binom nk}
=\frac{k!}{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(2^j-1)^{-1}.
\]

See Theorem 2 of the
[primary arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05731), published as
Graphs and Combinatorics 35 (2019), 303--320,
[DOI 10.1007/s00373-018-1984-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-018-1984-7).
A recent tree-profile paper also quotes exactly this formula
([Blekherman et al., 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-025-02305-1)).

This deserves attention because, if one could use the displayed density
uniformly for (k\sim\log n), then

\[
\log_2\!\left(\delta_k\binom nk\right)
=k\log_2n-\frac{k(k-1)}2+O(k\log k),
\]

whose optimum at (k=(1+o(1))\log_2 n) has coefficient (1/2).

However, there are two decisive failures.

### 4.1 The theorem is not uniform in growing (k)

The proof establishes, for strictly (d)-ary trees and fixed (k),

\[
c(F_k^2,T)\ge b_k n^k-\frac{n^{k-1}}{(k-1)!},
\qquad
b_k=\frac12(d-1)^{k-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(d^j-1)^{-1}.
\]

For (d=2) and (k\asymp\log n), the subtractive error is exponentially
larger than the main term, so this finite estimate is vacuous. The paper's
minimum density sequence is nondecreasing *toward* (delta_k); it does not
give the inequality
(c(F_k^2,T)\ge\delta_k\binom nk) at finite (n). Corollary 3 is an
asymptotic with (k) fixed. Consequently the formal coefficient-(1/2)
optimization is not a theorem.

### 4.2 It counts the wrong rooted pattern family

(F_k^2) is unordered and its root is an **endpoint** of the internal spine:
its root recurrence always has one singleton branch and one copy of
(F_{k-1}^2). After restoring the ambient left/right order, its copies may
realize any skew-tree spine word. By contrast, a nontrivial (W)-pattern
(L_p\wedge R_q), with (p,q>1), has the root at the **turn in the interior**
of the internal spine. So these are different rooted shapes, not merely
different plane orientations. They coincide only in the endpoint cases
(p=1) or (q=1), which are just pure combs.

The discrepancy is already visible at four leaves. A root with a three-leaf
comb on one side and a singleton on the other is an (F_4^2) copy after
forgetting plane order. A root with a two-leaf cherry on each side is a
(W)-copy (L_2\wedge R_2), but is the balanced rooted four-leaf tree, not
(F_4^2).

There are (2^{\Theta(k)}) plane orientations of the endpoint-rooted
caterpillar and only (O(k)) root positions along an unrooted caterpillar
spine; neither averaging changes the root of a leaf-induced restriction.
No deterministic transfer to (W) follows.

## 5. Other binary-tree pattern literature does not give the needed minimum

Pudwell--Scholten--Schrock--Serrato study noncontiguous ordered binary-tree
patterns and give a multivariate functional equation for occurrences of an
arbitrary fixed path-tree pattern
([open-access article, DOI 10.1155/2014/316535](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/316535)).
This is adjacent in terminology, but their containment is defined by paired
edge contractions, not by choosing an arbitrary subset of leaves and
suppressing unary vertices. Their results enumerate occurrences over the
class of Catalan trees / distributions of fixed-pattern counts; they do not
prove a minimum over every fixed ambient tree, and they do not treat pattern
size (k\asymp\log n).

The phylogenetic "inducibility" literature does use exactly the leaf-induced
restriction notion. Czabarka--Székely--Wagner introduced the density of a
fixed leaf-induced tree pattern
([SIAM J. Discrete Math. 2017](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/16M1060741));
subsequent work and current flag-algebra/tree-profile work optimize densities
of fixed, usually unordered patterns. I found no ordered-pattern minimum
theorem with estimates uniform when the pattern has (Theta(\log n)) leaves.

## 6. Precise no-hit verdict

The searches covered exact recurrence strings and variants, cograph
clique/independence polynomials, chain/antichain polynomials of
series-parallel posets, increasing/decreasing subsequences of separable
permutations, ordered binary-tree path/turn patterns, leaf-induced subtree
inducibility, caterpillar minimum density, and symbolic forms
(2^{(\log n)^2/2}) and (n^{(\log n)/2}).

**Valid transfer found:** (C,U,W) are respectively the total leaf-induced
left-comb, right-comb, and left-right-comb (one-turn path-tree) occurrence
counts in the ordered strong-decomposition tree.

**No valid quantitative transfer found:** no indexed paper, arXiv preprint,
or directly cited note found in this sweep proves the coefficient-(1/2)
minimum for that ordered one-turn family, nor an inequality of the form
(CU\le W n^{o(\log n)}). Ordinary cograph/series-parallel/separable-
permutation polynomial recurrences cannot supply it, and the sharp unordered
caterpillar-density formula is a tempting but invalid near-hit.

The actionable reformulation is therefore a new ordered-tree extremal
problem:

> Among all (n)-leaf ordered full binary trees, lower-bound the total number
> of leaf subsets whose reduced induced tree is (L_p\wedge R_q), summed over
> all (p,q\ge1).

A theorem with exponent (1/2-o(1)), or a weighted comparison between all
left/right comb pairs and aligned one-turn copies with only
(2^{o((\log n)^2)}) loss, appears to be genuinely new rather than a standard
corollary of the neighboring literatures.
