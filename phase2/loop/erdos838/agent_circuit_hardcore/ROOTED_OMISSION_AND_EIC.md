# Rooted marked omission and the exact EIC interface

**Date:** 2026-08-14  
**Verdict:** this note does not prove EIC.  It gives a new, strictly cleaner
sufficient inequality for the surviving two-root target, derives its exact
QuickHull recurrence, and identifies the precise extra Hall statement needed
to turn any pointwise rooted theorem into EIC.  This also explains why proving
the previously proposed rooted inequality, by itself, would not close
Erdős 838.

Throughout, `u,v` are fixed roots and `Q` is an `m`-point set strictly on one
side of `uv`.  Put

\[
 R_Q(z)=\sum_{\substack{C\subseteq Q\\
                 \{u,v\}\cup C\text{ convex}}}z^{|C|},
 \qquad V_Q=Z_Q(1),\qquad h=\tfrac12.                 \tag{1}
\]

## 1. A marked-omission inequality implies the rooted target

The rooted target proposed in the bivariate-potential lane is

\[
 \boxed{mR_Q(h)\le R_Q(1)+V_Q.}                       \tag{RT}
\]

Define the marked-omission mass

\[
 D_Q:=mR_Q(h)-hR'_Q(h)
     =\sum_{q\in Q}R_{Q-q}(h)
     =\sum_{C\in\mathcal R_Q}(m-|C|)2^{-|C|}.         \tag{2}
\]

> **Lemma 1 (marked omission is sufficient).**  The inequality
> \[
>  \boxed{D_Q\le V_Q}                                 \tag{MO}
> \]
> implies `(RT)`.

**Proof.**  If `R_Q(z)=sum_k r_k z^k`, then

\[
 hR'_Q(h)=\sum_{k\ge1}{k r_k\over2^k}
          \le\sum_{k\ge1}r_k<R_Q(1),                 \tag{3}
\]

because `k<=2^k`.  Add (3) to `(MO)`.  QED.

This reduction is useful because a term of `D_Q` is a rooted chain with one
marked *omitted* point.  It is exactly the object generated by a string of
QuickHull exclude decisions.

There is also a coefficient consequence with the correct unbalanced loss.
If `r_i=[z^i]R_Q(z)`, then `(RT)` gives

\[
 \boxed{m r_i\le2^i\{R_Q(1)+V_Q\}.}                  \tag{4}
\]

Thus a hidden interval of size `i=o(r)` costs only `2^i=2^{o(r)}`.  This is
the right scale for the unbalanced branch of EIC; the elementary estimate
`r_i<=binom(m,i)` instead loses as much as `n^i`.

There is an independent interpolation route to `(RT)`.  Log-convexity of a
polynomial with nonnegative coefficients gives

\[
 R_Q(1/2)^2\le R_Q(1)R_Q(1/4).
\]

Hence `(RT)` also follows from

\[
 \boxed{m^2R_Q(1/4)\le4V_Q.}                        \tag{QI}
\]

Indeed
`mR_Q(1/2)<=2\sqrt{R_Q(1)V_Q}<=R_Q(1)+V_Q`.
`(QI)` survived the exact search in this lane, but its two obvious
inductions are false.  Pointwise QuickHull monotonicity fails on the same
apex/chain family.  Averaging a minimal counterexample over its one-point
deletions would require the mean-rank inequality

\[
 \mu_V\ge2-{1\over m}+\left(1-{1\over m}\right)^2\mu_R,         \tag{4a}
\]

where `mu_R` is the rooted mean at activity `1/4` and `mu_V` is the uniform
unrooted mean.  The verifier contains an exact eight-point stretchable
counterexample to (4a).  Thus both `(MO)` and `(QI)` remain plausible global
targets, but neither admits the naive deletion induction.

## 2. Exact QuickHull recurrence for marked omission

Choose the outermost QuickHull pivot `x`.  Let `L,R,D` be the left, right,
and discarded middle pockets, of sizes `ell,s,d`, and write

\[
 A(z)=R_{ux}(L;z),\quad B(z)=R_{xv}(R;z),\quad
 R_0(z)=R_{uv}(Q-x;z).
\]

The exact rooted recurrence is

\[
 R_Q(z)=R_0(z)+zA(z)B(z).                            \tag{5}
\]

For any rooted instance `T` with `t` ground points, put

\[
 D(T)=tR_T(h)-hR'_T(h).
\]

Differentiating (5), and using `m-1=ell+s+d`, gives the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{
 D(Q)=D(Q-x)+R_0(h)
  +h\{dA(h)B(h)+D(L)B(h)+A(h)D(R)\}.}               \tag{6}
\]

Every term has a direct meaning:

* `R_0(h)` is the newly available choice of marking the excluded pivot `x`;
* `d A(h)B(h)` marks a discarded middle-pocket point after selecting `x`;
* the last two terms move the marked omission into one selected child.

Identity (6) is the clean cumulative Bellman state.  It also shows why a
one-pivot induction is too strong.  If one substitutes the inductive bounds
`D(L)<=V_L` and `D(R)<=V_R`, the required local payment is

\[
 \Delta_xV\ge R_0(h)+h\{dA(h)B(h)+V_LB(h)+A(h)V_R\}. \tag{7}
\]

On the exact apex/ten-point concave-chain instance from the
bivariate-potential report,

\[
 \Delta_xV=56,quad R_0(h)=(3/2)^{10},\quad
 d=10,quad A=B=V_L=V_R=1.
\]

The margin in (7) is

\[
 56-(3/2)^{10}-5=-{6825\over1024}<0.                \tag{8}
\]

Thus `(MO)`, if true, also needs cumulative amortization over later exclude
pivots.  The failure is not an artefact of the older slack.

## 3. The residual is exactly repair mass versus unused face capacity

Let `\mathcal R_Q` be the rooted-chain complex.  Expand the left side of
`(MO)` into pairs `(C,q)` with `C\in\mathcal R_Q` and `q\notin C`, of weight
`2^{-|C|}`.

Call the pair **good** when `C+q` is convex as a subset of `Q`.  Group good
pairs by their target face `S=C+q`.  If `g(S)` is the number of `q in S`
for which `S-q` is rooted, the good load on an `s`-face is

\[
 2^{1-s}g(S)\le {s\over2^{s-1}}\le1.                \tag{9}
\]

All remaining pairs are repair incidences: `C` is convex, `C+q` is not,
and deleting `q` repairs it.  The planar repair theorem says that each is
exactly one of:

1. `q` is the unique interior point; or
2. `q` is a hull vertex which hides one consecutive ear of `C`.

Consequently `(MO)` is equivalent to the following completely explicit
repair-capacity inequality:

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{C\in\mathcal R_Q,\ q\notin C\\C+q\text{ nonconvex}}}
       2^{-|C|}
 \le
 \sum_{S\in F(Q)}\left(1-2^{1-|S|}g(S)\right).}     \tag{RC}
\]

Every summand on the right is nonnegative by (9).  There is no longer an
unclassified geometric case in the local problem; `(RC)` is the exact
remaining rooted repair charge.

## 4. Why pointwise RT still does not prove EIC

For one pocket, (4) is exactly the desired `2^i` conversion from an
unbalanced coefficient to ordinary rooted/unrooted capacity.  EIC,
however, sums over many repaired boundaries.  The same ordinary pocket
face can occur in the `V_Q` term for exponentially many different
boundaries and tangent pairs.  Summing `(RT)` therefore yields a repeated
credit, not a global injection.

This is the same obstruction seen in the exact all-chord sum.  If the two
sides of a chord are `Q^+,Q^-`, summing `(RT)` produces the mixed leftover

\[
 T=\sum_{\{u,v\}}
 \{Z_{Q^+}(1)R^-_{uv}(h)+Z_{Q^-}(1)R^+_{uv}(h)\},   \tag{10}
\]

in addition to the rooted endpoint products.  Neither four-corner
positivity nor the scalar QuickHull recurrence bounds `T` by `V(P)`.

The exact missing statement is a Hall strengthening, not a stronger
singleton-pocket inequality.  One convenient formulation is the following.
For each unbalanced repair frame `f`, let `\mathcal D_f` be its marked
exclude histories, and let `\mathcal T_f` consist of

* boundary-completed rooted target faces; and
* ordinary convex faces lying in its discarded pockets.

> **Rooted-pocket Hall gate (RPH).**  For every subfamily `G` of repair
> frames at rank `r`,
> \[
>  \sum_{f\in G}|\mathcal D_f|
>  \le 2^{o(r)}\left|\bigcup_{f\in G}\mathcal T_f\right|.       \tag{RPH}
> \]

With capacities included, the identical statement applies to the
half-weighted demands in (2).  Hall's theorem then gives a
`2^{o(r)}=n^{o(1)}` congestion allocation.  Coefficient extraction via
(4) closes all `i=o(r)` ears, while balanced ears are handled by the
quadratic-potential recursion.

`(RT)` is precisely the `|G|=1` inequality behind `(RPH)`.  It does not
imply the inequalities for larger `G`, because the unrooted target classes
overlap.  Therefore a proof of `(RT)` without a multi-pocket packing theorem
would be genuine progress, but not a completion of EIC.

## 5. What is rigorously discharged

The polynomial-frame theorem in this directory handles any family covered
by subquadratically many small frames.  In the unbalanced setting an even
simpler entropy observation applies.  If the hidden ear has size
`i=o(r)` and a subfamily uses only `T=2^{o(r^2)}` repaired boundary frames,
then

\[
 |\mathcal I|\le T\sum_{j\le i}\binom nj=2^{o(r^2)}.            \tag{11}
\]

The universal Erdős--Szekeres lower bound gives
`V(P)>=2^{r^2/10+o(r^2)}` at `r=log_2 n-O(1)`, so such a subfamily is
negligible at the quadratic entropy scale.  What (11) does **not** give is
the `2^{o(r)}V(P)` precision of EIC.  Reaching that precision is exactly
the role of `(RPH)`.

Thus a surviving EIC obstruction must simultaneously have quadratic
boundary-frame entropy and reuse the same pocket-face capacity across those
frames.  Common-apex and frozen-swap examples have the reuse but not the
frame entropy; balanced Pascal families have the entropy but are handled by
the balanced recursion.  The unresolved case is their genuine hybrid.
