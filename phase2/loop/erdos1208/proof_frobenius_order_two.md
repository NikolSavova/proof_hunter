# Quadratic-Frobenius towers for Erdős #1208

## Status

This note gives a further explicit upper-bound improvement for Erdős problem
#1208.  It uses the non-uniform prime-power master inequality proved in
`proof_placewise_depths.md`, but replaces complete splitting of the useful
rational primes by the much cheaper condition that their Frobenius elements
have order at most two.  The resulting certified exponent is

\[
  \boxed{F_2(n)\ll n^{0.4947}.}
\]

As in the earlier notes, the standard tame totally-real Shafarevich
presentation theorem and the Minkowski-box estimates are external inputs.
The new group-theoretic quotient, the residue-degree-two local calculation,
and the numerical phase certificate are given below.

## 1. The order-two Frobenius quotient

Let \(T\) be the first 401 odd rational primes and let \(G_T\) be the Galois
group of the maximal totally real pro-2 extension of \(\mathbb Q\) unramified
away from \(T\).  The positive odd quadratic discriminants supported on
\(T\) form a 400-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-space: the number of factors
which are \(3\pmod4\) must be even, and this is one nonzero linear condition.
Consequently the standard tame Shafarevich presentation has

\[
  d(G_T)=400,\qquad r(G_T)\leq 400.                 \tag{1}
\]

Set

\[
  k=39599.
\]

Fix the following explicit basis of the 400-dimensional Frattini square-class
space.  Take every singleton \(p\in T\) with \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), and take
\(3p\) for every \(p\in T\setminus\{3\}\) with
\(p\equiv3\pmod4\).

Let \(q_1<\cdots<q_k\) be the first \(k\) unramified odd primes satisfying
one of the following conditions:

* \(q_i\equiv1\pmod4\); or
* \(q_i\equiv3\pmod4\), and at least one of the displayed Frattini basis
  classes is a quadratic nonresidue modulo \(q_i\).

For each \(q_i\), choose a Frobenius element \(g_i\in G_T\), and form

\[
  G=G_T/\langle\!\langle g_1^2,\ldots,g_k^2
                 \rangle\!\rangle.                \tag{2}
\]

Every square belongs to the Frattini subgroup of a pro-2 group.  Thus the
new relators do not change the generator rank, while (1) gives

\[
  d(G)=400,\qquad r(G)\leq400+39599=39999
       <\frac{400^2}{4}=40000.                     \tag{3}
\]

The Golod--Shafarevich inequality therefore makes \(G\) infinite.  In the
corresponding totally real Galois extension \(L/\mathbb Q\), every \(q_i\)
is unramified and its decomposition group is generated by the image of
\(g_i\), which has order at most two.  If \(q_i\equiv3\pmod4\), its Frattini
class is nonzero by construction, so this image has order exactly two.  The
root discriminants are bounded by

\[
  D=\prod_{p\in T}p.                               \tag{4}
\]

We use finite Galois quotients whose kernels lie inside the Frattini subgroup.
They retain every nonzero Frattini class, so the selected
\(3\pmod4\) Frobenius elements have residue degree exactly two.  Every finite
2-group quotient has a normal series inside its Frattini subgroup with
successive factors of order two.  Since the finite quotient orders are
unbounded, this supplies fields of every sufficiently large degree \(2^j\)
while retaining the full Frattini quotient.  The \(1\pmod4\) primes may have
residue degree one or two.

## 2. Local gain with residue degree at most two

Fix a layer \(K\subset L\) of degree \(m\).  If
\(\mathfrak p\mid q_i\), write

\[
  f_{\mathfrak p}=[\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p:\mathbb F_{q_i}]
  \in\{1,2\}.
\]

For \(q_i\equiv1\pmod4\), a rational square root of \(-1\) lifts at every
prime ideal above \(q_i\).  For \(q_i\equiv3\pmod4\), every residue degree is
exactly two and \(-1\) is a square in \(\mathbb F_{q_i^2}\).  Thus the local
prime-power construction from `proof_placewise_depths.md` works verbatim in
both cases, with \(N(\mathfrak p)=q_i^{f_{\mathfrak p}}\).  If the depth at
\(\mathfrak p\) is \(K_{i,\mathfrak p}\), its contributions to the per-degree
cost and gain are

\[
 \frac{K_{i,\mathfrak p}f_{\mathfrak p}\log q_i}{m},
 \qquad
 \frac1m\log\!\left(
   \frac{K_{i,\mathfrak p}+1}
   {\sum_{e=0}^{K_{i,\mathfrak p}}q_i^{-f_{\mathfrak p}e}}
 \right).                                         \tag{5}
\]

We use only depths 0, 1, and 2.  Put

\[
 h_{q,1}=\frac12\log\frac{2}{1+q^{-2}},
 \qquad
 h_{q,2}=\frac12\log
 \frac{3(1+q^{-2})}{2(1+q^{-2}+q^{-4})}.           \tag{6}
\]

For either residue degree \(f\in\{1,2\}\), the gain per unit cost of the
first, respectively second, depth increment is at least

\[
  \frac{h_{q,1}}{\log q},\qquad
  \frac{h_{q,2}}{\log q}.                          \tag{7}
\]

For \(f=2\) this is equality.  For \(f=1\), the first inequality follows
after squaring from

\[
  2(1+t^2)-(1+t)^2=(1-t)^2\geq0,
\]

and the second from

\[
\begin{split}
 &3(1+t)^2(1+t^2+t^4)
   -2(1+t+t^2)^2(1+t^2)\\
 &\hspace{25mm}=(1-t)^2(1+4t+5t^2+4t^3+t^4)\geq0,
\end{split}
\]

where \(t=q^{-1}\).

If all primes above \(q\) receive one increment, their total cost per degree
is \(\log q\), because \(\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid q}f_{\mathfrak p}=m\), and
their gain is at least the corresponding \(h_{q,j}\).  A fraction \(\theta\)
of an increment is implemented by taking prime ideals whose total residue
degree is at most \(\theta m\) and maximal with that property.  The unused
residue-degree weight is less than two, so the cost and gain errors are
\(O_q(1/m)\).  This is the residue-degree-two version of the placewise
rounding lemma.

## 3. A two-stage concave path

We use the following deliberately simple path through the available local
increments.

1. Give depth one to the primes \(q_1,q_2,\ldots\), in increasing order,
   allowing one fractional final prime.
2. After every \(q_i\) has depth one, give the second increment to
   \(q_1,q_2,\ldots\), again in increasing order with one fractional final
   prime.

For the present primes (all exceed 2753), elementary differentiation shows
that both ratios in (7) decrease with \(q\).  To see this, put
\(x=\log q\) and \(t=e^{-2x}\).  Then

\[
 h_{q,1}'(x)=\frac{t}{1+t},\qquad
 h_{q,2}'(x)=
 \frac{t^2(2+t)}{(1+t)(1+t+t^2)}.
\]

For \(x\geq\log2753\), the quantities \(xh_{q,j}'(x)\) are respectively
less than \(10^{-5}\) and \(10^{-10}\), whereas
\(h_{q,1}>0.34\) and \(h_{q,2}>0.20\).  Hence
\((h_{q,j}/x)'=(xh_{q,j}'-h_{q,j})/x^2<0\).  Thus the cost/gain function is
concave on each of the two stages.  This monotonicity is also checked
numerically with 80-digit arithmetic by the verifier.

Let \(F(L)\) denote the guaranteed gain on this path.  For a target exponent
\(\alpha<1/2\), the master inequality (8) of
`proof_placewise_depths.md`, with \(L=2\alpha w\), is satisfied provided

\[
 F(2\alpha w)\geq
 \log(4D)+(2-4\alpha)w+
 \log\!\left(1+
   \frac{e^{2(2\alpha-1)w}}{4D}\right).            \tag{8}
\]

On each stage, the left side minus the right side is concave in \(w\), by
the same argument as the concave-envelope lemma in the placewise note.
Therefore it is enough to check the two ends of each stage.

## 4. The numerical certificate

Take

\[
  \alpha=0.4947,\qquad w_0=345000.
\]

Let

\[
 L_1=\sum_{i=1}^{39599}\log q_i,qquad
 G_1=\sum_{i=1}^{39599}h_{q_i,1},qquad
 w_*=\frac{L_1}{2\alpha}.
\]

The verifier `verify_frobenius_order_two_rank400.py` generates all primes
from their definitions, checks (3), computes (4), checks the decreasing
local slopes, and evaluates (8).  Its conservative lower bounds for the
three relevant margins are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 w&w\text{-endpoint margin in (8)}\\ \hline
 w_0&76.38\\
 w_*&804.49\\
 2w_0&106.25.
\end{array}                                       \tag{9}
\]

The packing correction in the last logarithm of (8) is less than
\(e^{-7000}\), and is included in the displayed certified lower bounds.
The verifier uses correctly rounded 80-digit `Decimal` logarithms.  A total
allowance of \(10^{-30}\) is subtracted from every margin; this is still much
larger than \(10^{-40}\) times the number of arithmetic operations and is
negligible compared with (9).

For every sufficiently large \(n\), choose a degree-\(2^j\) layer with

\[
  w=\frac{\log n}{2\,[K:\mathbb Q]}\in[w_0,2w_0).
\]

Use stage one if \(w\leq w_*\), and stage two otherwise.  The placewise
rounding loss is \(O(1/[K:\mathbb Q])\), so the fixed margins in (9) absorb
it once the layer degree is large.  The master inequality gives

\[
  |A|\leq(1+\sqrt2)n^{0.4947}
\]

for every distance-Sidon subset of the resulting planar \(n\)-point set.
The finite initial range is absorbed into the implied constant.  Hence

\[
  \boxed{F_2(n)\ll n^{0.4947}.}
\]

This is still a partial upper-bound result, not a determination of the true
order of magnitude.  The unconditional lower bound remains
\(F_2(n)\gg n^{1/3}\).
