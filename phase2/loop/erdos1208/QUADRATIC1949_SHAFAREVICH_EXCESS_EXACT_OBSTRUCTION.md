# The quadratic-1949 Shafarevich excess is exactly one

## 1. Verdict

The proposed improvement `r_0<=d` is impossible for the explicit
`Q(sqrt(1949))` prefixes used by the CM/Eisenstein record.  For the
227-ideal prefix one has exactly

\[
 d(G_T)=225,\qquad r(G_T)=226=d(G_T)+1.             \tag{1.1}
\]

For the neighboring 229-ideal prefix the corresponding exact values are

\[
 d(G_T)=227,\qquad r(G_T)=228=d(G_T)+1.             \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus the conservative Shafarevich--Koch upper bound is sharp in both
certificates.  There is no relation to free by replacing `r_0<=d+1` with
`r_0<=d`.  In fact the computed cup products span all of `H^2`, so the
extra relation cannot merely be moved to Zassenhaus degree at least three:
the complete base relation module is already detected in degree two.

The proof is not a heuristic about generic presentations.  It computes a
`d+1`-dimensional subspace of `H^2(G_T,F_2)` from explicit global cup
products.  The verifier is
`verify_quadratic1949_shafarevich_excess_obstruction.py`.

## 2. The exact `H^1` space

Put

\[
 E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{1949}),\qquad
 \omega={1+\sqrt{1949}\over2}.
\]

The field has class number one.  Its unit squareclasses are generated by

\[
 -1,\qquad \epsilon=81333+3770\omega,qquad N(\epsilon)=-1. \tag{2.1}
\]

For a prefix `T` of `s` odd prime ideals, the squareclass space supported on
`T` consequently has dimension `s+2`: the two unit classes and one chosen
principal generator for each ideal.  Requiring both real places to split
and the dyadic prime to be unramified imposes the same four independent
conditions reconstructed in the record verifiers.  Hence

\[
 d(G_T)=\dim H^1(G_T,\mathbb F_2)=s-2.             \tag{2.2}
\]

This gives `225` and `227` at `s=227` and `s=229`, respectively; it is an
equality, not merely the lower bound used by the original numerical proof.
Indeed the odd-supported classes are dyadic units.  At the unramified
quadratic completion `E_2/Q_2`, the unit squareclass space has dimension
three, while its unramified Kummer line has dimension one.  Congruence to a
unit square modulo four is a sufficient unramified condition and the
verifier finds that it has codimension two.  It is therefore exactly the
unramified local line, not a proper subspace of it.

There is also a useful saturation check.  At the prime ideal of norm five
with `omega=2`, the local nonsquare row on `(-1,epsilon)` is `(0,1)`.  At
the prime ideal of norm nineteen with `omega=4`, it is `(1,0)`.  Thus the
two unit squareclasses are separated by `T`.  Class number one then gives

\[
 V_T/E^{\times2}=0.                                \tag{2.3}
\]

In the standard terminology `T` is saturated, so its tame Shafarevich
kernel injects into the zero Kummer obstruction group.  Saturation by
itself only gives an upper-bound interface; the cup-product computation
below supplies the decisive lower bound.

## 3. Localized global cup products

Choose a principal generator `pi_i` of every ideal `p_i in T`.  For a
global Kummer class `x`, write

\[
 a_i(x)=v_{p_i}(x)\pmod2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_i(x)=
 \left({\text{unit part of }x\over p_i}\right)\in\mathbb F_2, \tag{3.1}
\]

where `lambda_i=1` means nonsquare in the residue field.  The generator
`pi_i` itself is the chosen uniformizer, so its own contribution to the
unit part is one.

Let `Q_i=N(p_i)` and put

\[
 e_i={Q_i-1\over2}\pmod2.
\]

For two Kummer classes `x,y`, the tame local Hilbert-symbol formula gives
the localization of their cup product:

\[
 h_i(x,y)=e_i a_i(x)a_i(y)
          +a_i(y)\lambda_i(x)
          +a_i(x)\lambda_i(y).                   \tag{3.2}
\]

Formula (3.2) works uniformly for split, inert, and ramified prime ideals;
the verifier performs the residue-field arithmetic directly, including in
`F_{p^2}` at inert primes.

Take the explicit basis of the four-condition Kummer kernel from Section 2
and form every vector

\[
 h(x,y)=(h_i(x,y))_{p_i\in T}.                    \tag{3.3}
\]

Because all classes are positive, unramified at two, and unramified away
from `T`, their omitted local Hilbert symbols vanish.  Global reciprocity
therefore places (3.3) in the even-parity hyperplane, of dimension `s-1`.
Exact row reduction gives

\[
 \dim\langle h(x,y):x,y\in H^1(G_T,\mathbb F_2)\rangle
 =s-1                                                   \tag{3.4}
\]

for both `s=227` and `s=229`.  Every vector in (3.3) is the localization of
a cup product in `H^2(G_T,F_2)`, so (3.4) proves

\[
 r(G_T)=\dim H^2(G_T,\mathbb F_2)\ge s-1=d(G_T)+1. \tag{3.5}
\]

The tame totally-real Shafarevich--Koch theorem already gives the reverse
inequality `r(G_T)<=d(G_T)+1=s-1`.  Equations (3.5) and that bound prove
(1.1)--(1.2).  They also show that the cup-product map

\[
 H^1(G_T,\mathbb F_2)\otimes H^1(G_T,\mathbb F_2)
 \longrightarrow H^2(G_T,\mathbb F_2)               \tag{3.6}
\]

is surjective.  By the standard minimal-presentation/cup-product duality,
the dual map sends the relation module injectively into the space of
quadratic initial forms.  Consequently all `s-1` minimal base relations
are visible in Zassenhaus degree two; no nonzero linear combination can be
promoted to degree three.
