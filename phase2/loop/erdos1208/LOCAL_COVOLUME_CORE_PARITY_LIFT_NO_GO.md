# Local covolume cores: a bounded-index fourth-energy no-go

## 1. Verdict

For a finite noncollinear `B subset Z^2`, write

\[
 \Delta(B)=[\mathbb Z^2:\langle B-B\rangle].        \tag{1.1}
\]

The hoped-for local input

\[
 E_3^{\rm clean}(B)
 \le |B|^{3+o(1)}\Delta(B)                           \tag{1.2}
\]

is false, even when `B` itself is a genuine dense lattice core rather than
a high-index core spoiled by one generic outlier.

### Theorem A (bounded-covolume fourth-energy core)

There are infinitely many Euclidean distance-Sidon sets `B_p subset Z^2`
such that, writing `b=|B_p|` and `m` for coordinate height,

\[
 \boxed{
 b={p+1\over2},\qquad m=\Theta(p^2),\qquad
 2\le\Delta(B_p)\le16,\qquad
 E_3^{\rm clean}(B_p)=\Omega(p^4).}                 \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus

\[
 E_3^{\rm clean}(B_p)
 \ge b^{4-o(1)}
 \gg b^{3+o(1)}\Delta(B_p).                         \tag{1.4}

The construction lies inside one fixed-index lattice coset.  Consequently
neither global nor local generated-lattice covolume explains the large
equal-centroid energy.

This does **not** disprove the target

\[
 E_3^{\rm clean}(B)\le m^{o(1)}(|B|^3+m^2),         \tag{1.5}
\]

because (1.3) has `m^2=Theta(p^4)` and sharply spends that term.  It proves
that a viable core decomposition must attach and Carleson-pack a genuine
two-dimensional ambient footprint to each core.  A charge depending only
on `(|B_j|,Delta(B_j))` cannot work.

There is a second, endpoint-level warning.  In the full lifted modular
parabola at `p=43`, 8,602 of the 10,571 equal-centroid matching blocks have
six-endpoint generated covolume one.  If one instead takes the union of all
triples at each centroid, covolume-one centroid cores carry 10,246 of the
10,571 blocks.  The natural endpoint-local covolume is therefore trivial
on almost the entire equality model.

## 2. The parity core

Let `p>17` be a prime with `p=1 mod 4`, put

\[
 y_x=[x^2]_p\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\},                    \tag{2.1}
\]

and define the base parity core

\[
 C_p=\{(x,y_x):0\le x<p,\ y_x\equiv0\pmod2\}.       \tag{2.2}
\]

Apply the unimodular shear

\[
 L_p=\begin{pmatrix}1&p\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B_p=L_pC_p
 =\{(x+py_x,y_x):(x,y_x)\in C_p\}.                 \tag{2.3}

There are infinitely many eligible primes.  The restriction `p=1 mod 4`
also gives the core size without any character-sum estimate.  The nonzero
quadratic residues are invariant under `r -> p-r`; each such pair contains
one even and one odd integer.  Hence exactly `(p-1)/4` nonzero quadratic
residue values are even.  Each has two square roots, while zero has one, so

\[
 \boxed{|C_p|=|B_p|=1+2{p-1\over4}={p+1\over2}.}    \tag{2.4}

## 3. Exact local covolume

Every point of `C_p` has even second coordinate.  Therefore

\[
 \langle C_p-C_p\rangle\subseteq\mathbb Z\times2\mathbb Z,
 \qquad 2\mid\Delta(C_p).                            \tag{3.1}
\]

On the other hand, because `p>17`, the three labels `0,2,4` occur without
modular wrap and give the points

\[
 (0,0),\qquad(2,4),\qquad(4,16).                    \tag{3.2}
\]

Their determinant is

\[
 \det((2,4),(4,16))=16.                             \tag{3.3}
\]

The index of the lattice generated by a finite set of integer vectors is
the gcd of all its `2 by 2` minors.  Equations (3.1)--(3.3) prove

\[
 2\le\Delta(C_p)\le16.                              \tag{3.4}

Since `det L_p=1`, the shear preserves generated-lattice index, proving the
covolume assertion in (1.3).  Notice that the whole high-energy set is the
bounded-index core; no exceptional point is being used to collapse its
covolume.

## 4. Distance-Sidonicity and endpoint cleanliness

The full lifted modular parabola

\[
 A_p=\{(x+py_x,y_x):0\le x<p\}                     \tag{4.1}
\]

is Euclidean distance-Sidon.  For an edge oriented by increasing original
label, put `h=x-x' in {1,...,p-1}` and `z=y_x-y_(x')`.  Its squared length is

\[
 Q_p(h,z)=(h+pz)^2+z^2.                             \tag{4.2}

The intervals of (4.2) for consecutive nonnegative `z`, consecutive
negative `z`, and opposite signs are disjoint; equality therefore recovers
`(h,z)`.  Modulo `p`,

\[
 z\equiv h(x+x')\pmod p,                            \tag{4.3}

so `h` and `z` recover both endpoints.  This is the exact interval proof in
`LARGE_GAUSSIAN_CELL_SUPPORT_TAIL_AUDIT.md`.  Its subset `B_p` is therefore
distance-Sidon.

The base modular parabola is also additive Sidon over the integers: an
integer difference equality reduces modulo `p`, where `(h,h(x+x'))`
recovers the ordered endpoints.  Consequently two distinct three-subsets
with the same integer sum cannot share a point.  Cancelling a shared point
would give two different unordered pairs with the same sum.  Thus every
off-diagonal triple-sum collision below is a genuine six-endpoint matching
block.

## 5. Fourth-order clean energy inside one bounded-index core

The `binom(b,3)` unordered triples of `C_p` have integer coordinate sums in
`[0,3p)^2`, hence occupy fewer than `9p^2` cells.  If `r(s)` is the number
of triples in cell `s`, Cauchy gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_s r(s)(r(s)-1)
 &\ge {\binom b3^2\over9p^2}-{b\choose3}\\
 &=\Omega(p^4).                                     \tag{5.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

Every ordered collision is six-endpoint clean by Section 4.  Each ordered
pair of triples has six endpoint matchings, so

\[
 |\mathcal H_{B_p}|
 =6\sum_s r(s)(r(s)-1)
 =\Omega(p^4).                                      \tag{5.2}

The shear preserves all additive equalities, proving the energy assertion.

Finally, (2.4) shows there are `(p-1)/4` distinct positive even residues.
Their maximum is at least `(p-1)/2`; hence `B_p` contains a first coordinate
at least `p(p-1)/2`.  All its coordinates are below `p^2`, so

\[
 m=\Theta(p^2).                                     \tag{5.3}

The example is therefore an exact `m^2` equality model for (1.5).

## 6. Why lattice-core decomposition does not repair the route

Suppose a proposed decomposition assigns blocks to subsets/cosets `B_j`
and invokes a local estimate of the shape

\[
 E_3^{\rm clean}(B_j)
 \le |B_j|^{3+o(1)}\Delta(B_j).                      \tag{6.1}

Taking the decomposition with the single genuine core `B_p` contradicts
(6.1).  Splitting `B_p` into smaller pieces does not prove a bound for its
cross-piece matching blocks; those are precisely the terms the
decomposition was meant to control.  Merging enough pieces to recover the
energy returns to a bounded-covolume set of density `Theta(p)` and hence to
the same obstruction.

The only robust repair is to include a local footprint term, schematically

\[
 E_3^{\rm clean}(B_j)
 \lesssim |B_j|^{3+o(1)}\Delta(B_j)
          +m^{o(1)}\operatorname{AreaFootprint}(B_j), \tag{6.2}
\]

and then prove a global packing inequality for the footprints.  But on
`B_p` the footprint must already be `Omega(p^4)=Omega(m^2)`.  Thus (6.2)
is no longer a covolume shortcut: its hard part is the original flat
ambient payment.

## 7. Six-endpoint and centroid-core profiles

For a block formed by two equal-centroid triples, let `Delta_6` be the
covolume generated by its six endpoints.  For an entire centroid cell, let
`Delta_cell` use the union of every triple in that cell.  Exact profiles are

\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r|r}
p&\#\text{blocks}&\#(\Delta_6=1)&
\#\text{block mass with }\Delta_{cell}=1&\text{fraction}\\ \hline
23&721&583&640&0.887656\\
43&10571&8602&10246&0.969256
\end{array}                                           \tag{7.1}
\]

These finite data are not needed for Theorem A.  They show that even the
most canonical endpoint-local and centroid-local cores usually have
covolume one on the full equality model.
