# Audit of the remaining matrix-projection inverse

## 1. The proposed implication needs an endpoint-density threshold

Let

\[
 \mathcal E=\{uu^T:u\text{ is an edge vector of }A\}
 \subset\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb Z),
\]

let `E_q` be the subset belonging to the clean source edges, and put

\[
 \mathcal K=\mathcal E_q+18\mathcal E.          \tag{1.1}
\]

For the Euclidean metric, write

\[
 \pi(M)=\operatorname{tr}M,
 \qquad \mathcal P=\ker\pi.                    \tag{1.2}
\]

Distance-Sidonicity is exactly

\[
 (\mathcal E-\mathcal E)\cap\mathcal P=\{0\}. \tag{1.3}
\]

The metric scalar energy is

\[
 \mathcal M_{q,18}
 =\#\{(K,K')\in\mathcal K^2:K-K'\in\mathcal P\},            \tag{1.4}
\]

with multiplicity inherited from the representations in (1.1).

The literal statement

> many differences of `K` in `P` force a nonzero difference of `E` in `P`

is false, even for integral rank-one matrices and, below the sharp heavy
fibre threshold, even after installing genuine clean endpoints.  What might
still be true is the quantitatively calibrated statement

\[
 \mathcal M_{q,18}
 \le m^{o(1)}N(|H_q|+k),                       \tag{1.5}
\]

whose `+k` term is essential.  This note proves the countermodels, performs
the BSG exponent bookkeeping, and isolates the endpoint-aware strengthening
that a proof of (1.5) would require.

## 2. An integral rank-one countermodel to any cone-only inverse

Let

\[
 S_X=\{n\le X:n=x^2+y^2\text{ for some }x,y\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]

Choose one representing vector `u_n=(x_n,y_n)` for every `n in S_X`, and
put

\[
 \mathcal E_X=\{u_nu_n^T:n\in S_X\},
 \qquad \mathcal E_{q,X}=\mathcal E_X.          \tag{2.1}
\]

The trace is injective on `E_X`, so (1.3) holds.  The
Landau--Ramanujan theorem gives

\[
 R:=|S_X|\asymp {X\over\sqrt{\log X}}.          \tag{2.2}
\]

On the other hand, all projected charges `a+18b`, with `a,b in S_X`, lie in
an interval of length at most `19X`.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \#\{K-K'\in\mathcal P\}
 &=E(S_X,18S_X)\\
 &\ge {R^4\over19X}
  ={R^3\over(\log R)^{1/2+o(1)}}.              \tag{2.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the off-diagonal mass exceeds the `R^2` diagonal by
`R^(1-o(1))`, but there is no nonzero trace-zero difference in `E_X`.
All matrices in (2.1) are rank-one, positive semidefinite, and generated by
integer vectors of size at most `sqrt(X)`.

This refutes any attempt based only on

* rank-one positivity;
* integrality and polynomial height;
* trace injectivity; and
* additive BSG applied to the projected labels.

Endpoint compatibility is not optional.

## 3. Cleanly dressing the countermodel, and why it still does not kill the gate

The preceding model can be installed as genuine clean source edges.  Fix
distinct anchors `a,b` with

\[
 q=a-b\in2\mathbb Z^2.
\]

For every chosen `u_i`, introduce four points

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_i&=P_i+u_i,&d_i&=P_i-u_i,\\
 e_i&=P_i+q/2+w_i,&f_i&=P_i+q/2-w_i.           \tag{3.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

Then

\[
 c_i+d_i+q=e_i+f_i,                            \tag{3.2}
\]

so `c_i+d_i` is a clean `q`-start once all six labels are distinct.  Its
source squared distance is `4|u_i|^2`, and the same source edges may also be
used as the ordinary-edge subset in the scalar charge.

The free integral parameters `P_i,w_i` may be chosen so that the entire set
is distance-Sidon and these are the only `q`-starts.  Indeed, the prescribed
source distances are already distinct (omit the one possible value colliding
with the anchor distance).  Every other unwanted equality of two squared
distances is a nonzero polynomial in the free coordinates.  Every unwanted
pair-sum translate by `q` similarly excludes a nonzero linear polynomial in
at least one coordinate.  The product of these finitely many polynomials is
nonzero, and an integer grid with side exceeding its total degree contains a
nonvanishing choice.  The six-endpoint inequalities are avoided in the same
way.  Since there are only polynomially many forbidden equalities, this also
gives polynomial height.

This is a genuine clean-endpoint countermodel to every *uncalibrated*
projection inverse.  It has

\[
 k=4R+2,\qquad h=R=\Theta(k),                   \tag{3.3}
\]

and the controlled subsystem has energy

\[
 {R^3\over(\log R)^{1/2+o(1)}}.                \tag{3.4}
\]

But the weak target is

\[
 N(h+k)=\Theta(k^3),                           \tag{3.5}
\]

so (3.4) is smaller by a subpolynomial factor.  The `+k` in (1.5) pays for
the construction exactly.  To disprove the live gate one must share
endpoints efficiently enough to obtain `h>>k`, or control superlinearly many
ordinary edges.  Disjointly planted clean gadgets cannot do either.

## 4. Quantitative BSG loses the endpoint scale

Write

\[
 B=\pi(\mathcal E_q),\qquad D=\pi(\mathcal E),
 \qquad |B|=h,\quad |D|=N.                     \tag{4.1}
\]

Both projections are injective.  In the hardest regime `h asymp N asymp
k^2`; put this common scale equal to `n`.  Suppose the scalar energy has a
fixed-power excess

\[
 E(B,18D)\ge n^{2+\eta}.                       \tag{4.2}
\]

Relative to the maximal `n^3` energy, the BSG density parameter is only

\[
 \alpha=n^{-1+\eta}.                           \tag{4.3}
\]

Standard asymmetric Balog--Szemeredi--Gowers therefore guarantees, at best
at the first extraction step, structured subsets on the scale

\[
 \alpha n=n^\eta=k^{2\eta},                   \tag{4.4}
\]

with polynomial losses in `alpha^(-1)` in the doubling constant.  Expressed
as a violation of (1.5) by `k^epsilon`, one has `eta=epsilon/2`, so the
extracted set has only

\[
 k^\epsilon                                   \tag{4.5}
\]

edge labels.  For every small exponent `epsilon<1`, these edges can be an
endpoint-disjoint matching.  BSG has not reached even the `k`-edge scale at
which endpoint reuse becomes compulsory.

The same loss is visible without invoking the full theorem.  The average
size-biased load of a scalar charge value in (4.2) is `n^eta`; hence one
obtains a charge fibre containing `n^eta=k^epsilon` matched pairs of source
and ordinary distance labels.  That is again a local gadget, not a dense
endpoint patch.

Consequently, ordinary BSG/Freiman theory in the one-dimensional quotient
`Sym_2/P` cannot by itself prove (1.5).  It extracts exactly the sort of
sub-`k` structure that Section 3 shows is harmless.

## 5. The projective-direction reformulation

For a nonzero symmetric matrix `R`, write `[R]` for its primitive projective
direction.  Define

\[
 \mathscr D_E=\{[E-E']:E,E'\in\mathcal E, E\ne E'\}.       \tag{5.1}
\]

If a metric plane `P_G={M:tr(GM)=0}` contains a direction in `D_E`, then two
different edges have the same `G`-length.  Thus distance-Sidonicity says

\[
 \mathbb P(\mathcal P_G)\cap\mathscr D_E=\varnothing.      \tag{5.2}
\]

Every scalar collision supplies instead a direction in

\[
 \mathscr D_K
 =\{[(E_q-E_q')+18(E-E')]:\cdots\}.             \tag{5.3}
\]

The remaining inverse question is therefore:

> Can a distance-avoiding projective line contain polynomially large total
> multiplicity from `D_K`, once `E_q` comes from one clean pair-sum fibre?

This is more precise than asking for one popular direction.  A plane can
contain many distinct integer directions, so bounding the multiplicity of
each direction separately is insufficient.

Nevertheless the exact data support a direction-capture phenomenon.  On the
largest `p=13` finite-field-parabola fibre, the 78 edge matrices determine
2,417 forbidden directions in `D_E`.  After deleting them, the remaining
164,639 directions between distinct charge-matrix keys have maximum
multiplicity only four.  At `p=17`, the analogous safe maximum is seven,
while the charge has 1,904 records.  Before the deletion, all highly popular
directions are single-edge difference directions and would force a repeated
distance under any orthogonal metric.

The `p=13` count is checked in
`verify_metric_scalar_universal_matrix_and_ruler_stress.py`.

## 6. What the clean fibre adds—and what has not yet been extracted

Fix the unique ordered endpoints `a,b` realizing `q`.  Every source
`s=c+d in H_q` has a unique target `s+q=e+f`, with

\[
 a+c+d=b+e+f                                   \tag{6.1}
\]

and six distinct endpoints.  Thus `H_q` is not an arbitrary subset of edge
matrices: it is a graph of source edges equipped with an injective translated
pair-sum partner.  If `h>>k`, the source edges and target edges necessarily
reuse endpoints heavily.

Neither the scalar projection nor ordinary BSG remembers this partner map.
Section 4 extracts at most `k^epsilon` source edges, on which (6.1) can still
look like disjoint planted gadgets.  The missing step must retain many
different scalar fibres simultaneously and use their common endpoint
incidence.

A quantitatively sufficient endpoint-aware inverse would have the following
shape.

**Needed dense extraction.**  If

\[
 \mathcal M_{q,18}\ge k^\epsilon N(h+k),        \tag{6.2}
\]

then there are vertex sets `W,W' subset A` and source/target subgraphs linked
by (6.1) such that

\[
 |E(H_q[W])|\ge |W|^{1+\eta},
 \qquad |E(T_q(H_q)[W'])|\ge |W'|^{1+\eta}     \tag{6.3}
\]

for some `eta=eta(epsilon)>0`, while a positive proportion of the scalar
collisions survives inside these subgraphs.

Once (6.3) is available, dependent random choice can seek repeated endpoint
patterns, and the matrix directions in (5.3) can be compared with genuine
single-edge differences.  Current BSG yields no estimate resembling (6.3).

This is the exact bridge: **amplify quotient additive energy into a
superlinear endpoint patch before applying rank-one geometry.**

## 7. Rigorous branches already closed

The companion note
`METRIC_SCALAR_UNIVERSAL_MATRIX_AND_RULER_STRESS.md` proves two cases of this
inverse without needing the missing extraction:

1. If source and ordinary edges are internal to a subpolynomial collection
   of ruler arms, variable-coefficient binary quadratic-form bounds make the
   energy near-diagonal.
2. If the whole set lies on a subpolynomial collection of parallel lines,
   completing the square reduces every source/ordinary line-type block to
   `X^2+18Y^2=M`, proving the full scalar gate.

It also proves that universal matrix collisions
`uu^T+18vv^T=u'u'^T+18v'v'^T` have only subpolynomial load.  Hence a future
counterexample or proof must use many *different* nonzero matrix directions
inside the trace-zero plane; neither universal collisions nor one-direction
ruler resonance remains available.

## 8. Verdict

The matrix-projection inverse is false as a cone-only or collision-count
statement.  It remains plausible only in the sharp form (1.5), where the
`+k` term removes local clean gadgets and a violation must be amplified
through the dense endpoint system (6.1).

The decisive barrier is quantitative: scalar BSG sees a structured set of
only `k^epsilon` distance labels, while endpoint geometry becomes compulsory
only beyond `k` edges.  Closing that full factor requires a new
endpoint-aware, many-fibre extraction theorem.  This is not a routine BSG or
Freiman step.
