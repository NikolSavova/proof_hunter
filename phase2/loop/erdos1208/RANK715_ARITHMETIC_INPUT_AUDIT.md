# Arithmetic input audit for the rank-715 Frobenius-square tower

## 1. Verdict

The arithmetic tower used in `proof_frobenius_all_depth_rank715.md` is
justified by the standard tame totally-real pro-2 presentation theorem.
The one subtle point not made explicit in the construction note is also
valid: the infinite quotient has **Galois** layers of every sufficiently
large dyadic degree whose kernels lie in its Frattini subgroup.  Hence every
selected prime congruent to `3 mod 4` retains residue degree exactly two in
every layer used by the Minkowski-grid argument.

Consequently the arithmetic input to the certified estimate

\[
 F_2(n)\ll n^{0.494586}
\]

is no longer merely an asserted tower presentation.  It follows from the
same published/standard input used in the human-verified account of the
unit-distance construction, together with the elementary pro-2 lemmas
proved below.  The finite prime, rank, local-slope, and exponent calculations
remain certified by `verify_frobenius_all_depth_rank715.py`.

This audit does not turn the estimate into a solution of Erdős 1208.  It
validates the arithmetic side of a new explicit partial upper bound.

## 2. The base totally-real presentation

Let `T` be a finite set of odd rational primes and let

\[
 G_T^{\{\infty\}}
\]

be the Galois group of the maximal pro-2 extension of `Q` which is unramified
outside `T` and in which the real place splits completely.  Thus every finite
subextension is totally real.

The proof of the main theorem in Alon--Bloom--Gowers--Litt--Sawin--Shankar--
Tsimerman--Wang--Wood, *Remarks on the disproof of the unit distance
conjecture*, explicitly records the two facts needed here (their proof cites
Koch, Theorems 11.5 and 11.8):

1. the Frattini quotient is the maximal totally-real multiquadratic field
   unramified outside `T`; and
2. if `T` contains a prime congruent to `3 mod 4`, then

   \[
   d(G_T^{\{\infty\}})=|T|-1,
   \qquad
   r(G_T^{\{\infty\}})\le d(G_T^{\{\infty\}}).
   \tag{2.1}
   \]

For completeness, the first equality can also be read directly from
quadratic discriminants.  Positive odd squarefree discriminants supported
on `T` are exactly the products containing an even number of prime factors
congruent to `3 mod 4`.  Since `T` contains such primes, this is one nonzero
linear condition on the `|T|` square classes.

In the rank-715 construction, `T` is the first 716 odd primes.  Therefore

\[
 d(G_T^{\{\infty\}})=715,
 \qquad r(G_T^{\{\infty\}})\le715.             \tag{2.2}
\]

All ramification is tame, because the ramified rational primes are odd in a
pro-2 extension.  Hence every finite layer `K` satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{rd}(K)
 \le \prod_{p\in T}p=:D.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

There is no missing factor at 2 and no archimedean discriminant factor:
the real place splits and 2 is unramified.

## 3. Adding the Frobenius-square relations

Let `q_1,...,q_s` be unramified rational primes, choose Frobenius elements
`g_i`, and put

\[
 G=G_T^{\{\infty\}}/
   \langle\!\langle g_1^2,\ldots,g_s^2\rangle\!\rangle.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

In every pro-2 group, every square belongs to the Frattini subgroup.  The
closed normal subgroup generated by the `g_i^2` is therefore contained in
the Frattini subgroup.  It follows that quotienting by (3.1) does not change
the Frattini quotient, while a presentation is obtained by adjoining at most
one relator per selected prime.  Thus

\[
 d(G)=715,
 \qquad r(G)\le715+s.                           \tag{3.2}
\]

For `s=127091`,

\[
 715+127091=127806
 <\frac{715^2}{4}=127806.25.                   \tag{3.3}
\]

The strict Golod--Shafarevich inequality makes `G` infinite.

The relation `g_i^2=1` says that the decomposition group of `q_i` in the
resulting Galois extension has order at most two.  If `q_i=1 mod 4`, either
residue degree one or two is suitable because `-1` is already a square in
`F_{q_i}`.  If `q_i=3 mod 4`, the construction selects it only when its
Frobenius class is nonzero in the Frattini quotient.  The quotient (3.1)
has the same Frattini quotient, so this Frobenius remains nontrivial and has
order exactly two.  Its residue field is therefore `F_{q_i^2}`, where `-1`
is a square.

The verifier checks the nonzero Frattini criterion by evaluating the
Legendre symbols of the displayed 715-element quadratic basis.  At least
one nonsquare basis class is equivalent to a nontrivial Frobenius vector in
the multiquadratic Frattini field.

## 4. Galois layers of every dyadic degree

The construction needs more than a sequence of unbounded degrees.  It uses
degree `2^j` fields for every sufficiently large `j`, and the
`3 mod 4` Frobenius elements must remain nontrivial in those layers.

### Lemma 4.1

Let `G` be an infinite finitely generated pro-2 group.  Put
`d=d(G)`.  For every sufficiently large `j` there is an open normal subgroup

\[
 N_j\triangleleft G,
 \qquad N_j\subseteq\Phi(G),
 \qquad [G:N_j]=2^j.                            \tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

The Frattini subgroup `Phi(G)` is open of index `2^d` and is infinite.  Take
an open normal subgroup `K` contained in `Phi(G)` with `[G:K]=2^M`, where
`M` is arbitrarily large.  In the finite 2-group `P=G/K`, the subgroup
`H=Phi(G)/K` is normal.

Every normal subgroup of a finite p-group admits a series, normal in the
whole group, whose successive factors have order `p`.  Indeed a nontrivial
normal subgroup meets the centre nontrivially; choose a central subgroup of
order `p` in that intersection and induct in the quotient.  Applying this
with `p=2` gives a `P`-normal series from `1` to `H` with factors of order
two.  Pulling it back to `G` produces, for every `d<=j<=M`, a normal subgroup
`N_j` satisfying (4.1).  Since `M` is arbitrarily large, the claim follows.
QED.

The fixed fields of the `N_j` are Galois and totally real.  Their degrees
are exactly `2^j`, and (2.3) persists.  Because `N_j` lies in `Phi(G)`, every
element nonzero in the Frattini quotient remains nontrivial in `G/N_j`.
Thus the selected `3 mod 4` Frobenius elements have order and residue degree
exactly two in every layer.  This repairs the possible gap that would arise
from using arbitrary nonnormal intermediate fields, where an order-two
Frobenius could fix some cosets and produce degree-one primes.

## 5. Interface with the placewise master inequality

In a Galois layer of degree `m`, every prime above a selected rational `q`
has residue degree `f=1` or `2`; for `q=3 mod 4` it is `f=2`.  At a prime
ideal `p|q`, depth `K_p` has cost and gain

\[
 \frac{K_p f\log q}{m},
 \qquad
 \frac1m\log\left(
  \frac{K_p+1}{\sum_{e=0}^{K_p}q^{-fe}}
 \right).                                      \tag{5.1}
\]

The local factorization of `x^2+y^2` works because `-1` is a square in the
residue field.  Summing the costs over all primes above `q` uses

\[
 \sum_{\mathfrak p\mid q}f_{\mathfrak p}=m.    \tag{5.2}
\]

The residue-degree-two gains used in the rank-715 optimizer are lower bounds
for the degree-one gains.  The depth-one and depth-two comparisons are the
positive `(1-t)^2` identities in `proof_frobenius_order_two.md`; the
depth-three comparison is the positive polynomial identity in equation (4)
of `proof_frobenius_all_depth_rank715.md`.

For a fractional final increment, select prime ideals whose accumulated
residue degree is maximal below the target fraction.  The unused weight is
less than two, so both cost and gain errors are `O_q(1/m)`.  Only finitely
many rational primes and depths occur in the certificate.  Hence the total
rounding error is `O(1/m)` uniformly along the chosen layers, and the fixed
endpoint margins greater than three absorb it.

The ideal CRT, valuation-pattern multiplicity, divisor switch, and box
packing arguments are proved in `proof_placewise_depths.md`.  They do not
require complete splitting or a rational modulus; residue degrees enter only
through ideal norms, (5.1), and (5.2).

## 6. Numerical and citation scope

`verify_frobenius_all_depth_rank715.py` independently checks:

* all 716 ramified primes and all 127,091 selected useful primes;
* the 715-dimensional square-class space and every useful-prime criterion;
* the strict relation inequality (3.3);
* the 2,312-digit root-discriminant product;
* all first three local increments and their prefix order; and
* the two phase margins for `alpha=0.494586`.

Its integer computations are exact.  Python's `Decimal.ln` and
`Decimal.exp` are correctly rounded at precision 80; even summing the
127,091 local terms leaves an error many dozens of decimal places below the
smallest displayed margin, which is greater than three.  A directed-interval
rewrite would improve publication ergonomics but cannot change any certified
sign.
