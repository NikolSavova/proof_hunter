# Balanced Costas separators: an exact cutoff and the unresolved asymptotic gate

## 1. Verdict

Let

\[
 W_p=\{(i,g^i\bmod p):0\le i<p-1\}
\]

be the Welch--Costas array, with `g` the least primitive root modulo the
prime `p`.  For an integral matrix `T`, call `T` a separator if all
unordered nonzero edge vectors of `T W_p` have different Euclidean norms.

The desired asymptotic statement

\[
 \exists T_p:\quad \|T_p\|_\infty=p^{1/2+o(1)},
 \qquad T_pW_p\text{ is distance-Sidon}              \tag{1.1}
\]

is **not proved or disproved** here.  The following exact result corrects
the strongest finite extrapolation previously available.

### Exact cutoff theorem

For `p=263`, among integral matrices of determinant `+/-263`,

\[
 \boxed{
  \min\{\|T\|_\infty:T W_{263}\text{ is distance-Sidon}\}=67.} \tag{1.2}
\]

There are exactly `9,840` distinct Gram forms `T^T T` with
`det T=263` and `||T||_infty<=66`; every one has a repeated edge norm.  On
the other hand

\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}-67&-45\\52&31\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det T=263,                                  \tag{1.3}
\]

separates all `34,191` unordered edges of `W_263`.

Since `ceil(4 sqrt(263))=65`, this proves that the coefficient bound
`4 sqrt(p)` which worked for every tested prime through `257` does not
continue uniformly.  It does not rule out `C sqrt(p)` for a larger absolute
constant, still less the `p^(1/2+o(1))` statement (1.1).

The decisive status for the `Q/P/clean-centroid` route is therefore:

* the three ambient estimates are conditionally false if (1.1) holds;
* the present computations do not make that conditional counterexample
  asymptotic;
* the exact finite threshold fluctuates and its constant has begun to grow,
  so extrapolation from `p<=251` is not evidence of a theorem by itself.

## 2. Why the exhaustive cutoff is complete

Write

\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad ad-bc=p. \tag{2.1}
\]

When `max(|a|,|c|)<p`, primality implies `gcd(a,c)=1`: that gcd divides
`det T`, and it cannot equal `p`.  Choose Bezout coefficients `x,y` with
`ax+cy=1`.  Every solution of (2.1) is then

\[
 b=-py+at,\qquad d=px+ct,\qquad t\in\mathbb Z.       \tag{2.2}
\]

Thus looping over primitive first columns and the bounded interval of `t`
enumerates every determinant-`p` matrix in the coefficient box.  Matrices
with determinant `-p` need not be enumerated separately: multiplying one
row by `-1` changes the determinant sign and preserves `T^T T`.

Distance separation depends only on the Gram triple

\[
 (A,B,C)=(a^2+c^2,ab+cd,b^2+d^2),                   \tag{2.3}
\]

because

\[
 \|T(u_1,u_2)\|^2=Au_1^2+2Bu_1u_2+Cu_2^2.          \tag{2.4}
\]

Deduplicating (2.3) therefore loses no possible separator.  The verifier
implements (2.2), checks the count `9,840`, rejects all of their Gram forms,
and then checks (1.3) directly.

## 3. The exact asymptotic covering problem

Let `V_p` be the canonical unordered edge-vector set of `W_p`.  For
distinct `u,v in V_p`, put

\[
 n_{u,v}=(u_1^2-v_1^2,\;2(u_1u_2-v_1v_2),\;
                 u_2^2-v_2^2).                     \tag{3.1}
\]

A Gram triple `G=(A,B,C)` has a norm collision precisely when

\[
 \langle G,n_{u,v}\rangle=0                        \tag{3.2}
\]

for some pair `u!=v`.  Consequently (1.1) is exactly the assertion that the
integral Gram points generated by matrices in a `p^(1/2+o(1))` coefficient
box are not covered by the collision hyperplanes (3.2).

For the determinant-prime subfamily these Gram points additionally lie on

\[
 AC-B^2=p^2.                                        \tag{3.3}
\]

The difficulty is quantitative rather than algebraic.  There are roughly
`p^4` edge pairs but only `p^(1+o(1))` determinant-`p` Gram points at the
balanced scale.  A union bound is therefore useless.  Conversely, the
collision hyperplanes have enormous overlap: at `p=263` they cover every
point through radius `66`, then miss the Gram point (1.3) at radius `67`.
Any proof of (1.1) needs a non-covering theorem exploiting this overlap, or
a number-theoretic construction of a missed Gram point.  Standard lattice
point counts on individual hyperplane sections do neither.

The equal-norm equation admits a useful exact factorization.  If
`U=Tu,V=Tv` and `||U||=||V||`, then

\[
 U+V=s z,\qquad U-V=tJz_0                           \tag{3.4}
\]

for parallel primitive directions `z,z_0` related by a quarter-turn; more
canonically, after writing `U+V=s z` with primitive `z`, orthogonality gives
`U-V=tJz`.  Hence

\[
 st\,\|z\|^2=-2\det(U,V)=-2\det(T)\det(u,v).       \tag{3.5}
\]

This gives only divisor-many candidate image pairs for fixed `u,v`, but the
integrality conditions in `T=[U\ V][u\ v]^{-1}` have denominator
`det(u,v)`.  The missing analytic input is an aggregate congruence gain from
those denominators.  Pointwise divisor bounds alone lose approximately a
full factor `p`.

## 4. What Lefmann--Thiele does and does not provide

Lefmann and Thiele, [*Point Sets with Distinct
Distances*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01299744), prove that the `m` by `m`
grid contains a distance-Sidon subset of size `Omega(m^(2/3))`.  Their proof
colors the complete graph on all `m^2` grid points by squared distance,
uses Ramanujan's estimate

\[
 \sum_{n\le X}r_2(n)^2=\Theta(X\log X),              \tag{4.1}
\]

and applies an uncrowded-hypergraph anti-Ramsey theorem.  It extracts a
rainbow subset from the whole grid.  It does not start with a dense
vector-Sidon array and does not produce an affine map of one.

Thus their optimal-order construction does not imply (1.1).  Its set is
already sparse at the critical scale; the generic triple-sum lower bound is
then only `Omega(k^3)`, not the `Omega(k^4)` inherited by a balanced affine
image of a dense array.  It therefore does not by itself falsify any of the
ambient centroid estimates.

The same sums-of-two-squares theorem gives a useful necessary condition.
If a `k`-point distance-Sidon set lies in an `m` by `m` integer box, then

\[
 \binom{k}{2}\ll {m^2\over\sqrt{\log m}}.            \tag{4.2}
\]

For an affine image of `W_p` by a matrix of coefficient height `L`, one has
`m=O(Lp)`, so (4.2) forces only

\[
 L\gg(\log p)^{1/4}.                                \tag{4.3}
\]

This is compatible with `L=p^(1/2+o(1))` and supplies no obstruction at the
critical affine scale.
