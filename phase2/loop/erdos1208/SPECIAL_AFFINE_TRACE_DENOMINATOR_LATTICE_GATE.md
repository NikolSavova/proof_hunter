# Special-affine trace denominator and overlap-lattice gate

## Status

For a fully transverse equal-area triangle pair, let $g(x)=Mx+t$ be its
special-affine map and let

\[
 B_g=A\cap g^{-1}A.
\]

The cross-determinant matrix contains the rational conjugacy invariant
$\operatorname{tr}M$.  If

\[
 \operatorname{tr}M=\frac pq,qquad (p,q)=1,quad q>0,  \tag{0.1}
\]

then $q$ divides the index of the difference lattice generated by $B_g$.
Distance-Sidonicity then gives the quantitative overlap bound

\[
 \boxed{
 |B_g|\ll m^{3/2}q^{-3/4}+m^{1/2}+1.}                 \tag{0.2}
\]

Thus every overlap substantially larger than $m^{1/2}$ has small trace
denominator.  This is a genuine arithmetic restriction on the remaining
fully transverse special-affine maps.

It does not yet close the total energy: integer-trace and other
small-denominator maps remain numerous, and the bound (0.2) is pointwise
rather than an aggregate estimate.  The exact residual problem is now a
low-denominator special-affine overlap energy.

## 1. Trace inside the cross-determinant matrix

Let the source triangle have cyclic side vectors $e_1,e_2,e_3$ and the
target triangle $e'_i=Me_i$, with

\[
 e_1+e_2+e_3=e'_1+e'_2+e'_3=0,qquad
 \det(e_1,e_2)=\det(e'_1,e'_2)=d\ne0.
\]

Put $K_{ij}=\det(e_i,e'_j)$.  In the basis $(e_1,e_2)$, the columns of $M$
are

\[
 [M]_{(e_1,e_2)}
 =\frac1d
 \begin{pmatrix}
  -K_{21}&-K_{22}\\
   K_{11}& K_{12}
 \end{pmatrix}.                                        \tag{1.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{d\operatorname{tr}M=K_{12}-K_{21}=:t.}         \tag{1.2}
\]

The determinant-one condition is the Plücker identity

\[
 K_{11}K_{22}-K_{12}K_{21}=d^2.                        \tag{1.3}
\]

Writing $s=K_{12}+K_{21}$ and combining (1.2)--(1.3) gives the exact
discriminant equation

\[
 \boxed{
 4K_{11}K_{22}-s^2=4d^2-t^2.}                         \tag{1.4}
\]

For fixed $d,t$, equation (1.4) gives a modest divisor compression: after
choosing $s=O(m^2)$, the product $K_{11}K_{22}$ is fixed and has only
$m^{o(1)}$ factorizations.  Hence there are at most $m^{2+o(1)}$ leading
blocks for fixed $(d,t)$.  This is not by itself enough: the pair $(d,t)$
has an $O(m^4)$ ambient range.

The more useful consequence is that the reduced denominator of $t/d$ is
visible in every triangle contained in the same overlap $B_g$.

## 2. Denominator divides the overlap lattice

For a finite noncollinear $B\subseteq\mathbb Z^2$, define its difference
lattice

\[
 L(B)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}(B-B)\subseteq\mathbb Z^2
\]

and its index

\[
 \Delta(B)=[\mathbb Z^2:L(B)].                         \tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently, $\Delta(B)$ is the greatest common divisor of all determinants
$\det(u,v)$ with $u,v\in B-B$.

### Lemma 2.1

Let $M\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Q)$ and suppose both $B$ and $MB$ (up to a
translation) lie in $\mathbb Z^2$.  If $B$ is noncollinear and
$\operatorname{tr}M=p/q$ is reduced, then

\[
 \boxed{q\mid\Delta(B).}                               \tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

For all $u,v\in B-B$, both $Mu$ and $Mv$ are integer vectors.  Every
$2\times2$ determinant-one matrix satisfies

\[
 \det(u,Mv)-\det(v,Mu)
 =\operatorname{tr}(M)\det(u,v).                       \tag{2.3}
\]

The left side is an integer.  Therefore $q\mid\det(u,v)$ for every
$u,v\in B-B$.  Taking their gcd proves (2.2).  QED.

For $B=B_g$, the hypotheses hold because $g(B)\subseteq A\subseteq\mathbb
Z^2$.  Formula (1.2) is the triangle-level version of the same divisibility.

## 3. Distance-Sidon points in a lattice coset

### Lemma 3.1

Let $A\subseteq\{0,\ldots,m\}^2$ be distance-Sidon.  If a noncollinear
subset $B\subseteq A$ lies in a coset of a rank-two lattice
$L\subseteq\mathbb Z^2$ of index $\Delta$, then

\[
 \boxed{
 |B|\ll m^{3/2}\Delta^{-3/4}+m^{1/2}+1.}               \tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Choose a shortest nonzero vector $u\in L$ and put
$s=\lVert u\rVert_\infty$.  Minkowski's theorem gives

\[
 s\ll\Delta^{1/2}.                                     \tag{3.2}
\]

The lines parallel to $u$ that meet a fixed coset of $L$ are spaced, in the
normal direction, by order $\Delta/\lVert u\rVert$.  Hence at most

\[
 O\!\left(\frac{ms}{\Delta}+1\right)                  \tag{3.3}
\]

such lines meet the $m$-box.

On one of these lines, differences of points in the coset are integer
multiples of the primitive lattice step in that direction, whose norm is at
least $s$ up to an absolute norm-equivalence constant.  If the line contains
$r$ points of the distance-Sidon set, their $\binom r2$ positive parameter
gaps are all distinct and lie in an interval of length $O(m/s)$.  Therefore

\[
 r\ll(m/s)^{1/2}+1.                                    \tag{3.4}
\]

Multiplying (3.3) and (3.4), and using (3.2), gives

\[
 |B|ll
 \frac{m^{3/2}s^{1/2}}{\Delta}
 +\frac{ms}{\Delta}
 +(m/s)^{1/2}+1
 \ll m^{3/2}\Delta^{-3/4}+m^{1/2}+1.                  \tag{3.5}
\]

Here the middle term is absorbed because a noncollinear lattice subset in
the box has $\Delta\ll m^2$, while $1\le s\ll m$.  QED.

### Theorem 3.2 (trace-denominator overlap bound)

Let $A\subseteq\{0,\ldots,m\}^2$ be distance-Sidon, let
$g(x)=Mx+t$ be special-affine, and suppose $B_g=A\cap g^{-1}A$ is
noncollinear.  If $\operatorname{tr}M=p/q$ is reduced, then

\[
 \boxed{
 |B_g|\ll m^{3/2}q^{-3/4}+m^{1/2}+1.}                 \tag{3.6}
\]

This follows immediately from Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1.

Equivalently, whenever $|B_g|\ge C m^{1/2}$ for a sufficiently large
absolute $C$,

\[
 q\ll \frac{m^2}{|B_g|^{4/3}}.                         \tag{3.7}
\]

## 4. Why this is not yet the aggregate bound

For a fixed rational trace and a nonparallel directed edge correspondence

\[
 x-y\longmapsto g(x)-g(y),                              \tag{4.1}
\]

the linear part $M$ is unique.  Indeed, in the basis formed by a vector $v$
and its nonparallel image $v'=Mv$, a determinant-one matrix of trace $T$
must be

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&T\end{pmatrix}.               \tag{4.2}
\]

The translation is then fixed by one endpoint image.  Thus fixed-trace maps
are determined by any one **nonparallel** edge correspondence.  If $P_g$
denotes the number of ordered pairs $x\ne y$ in $B_g$ for which
$M(y-x)\not\parallel y-x$, then

\[
 \sum_{g:\operatorname{tr}M=T}P_g\le k^2(k-1)^2.       \tag{4.3}
\]

For a non-scalar $M$, the excluded vectors occupy at most two
eigendirections.  Each direction contains at most $m$ unordered edges of
$A$ by the direction bound, so

\[
 P_g\ge |B_g|(|B_g|-1)-4m.                              \tag{4.4}
\]

In particular, for overlaps larger than a sufficiently large multiple of
$m^{1/2}$, (4.3) also controls a constant multiple of $|B_g|^2$.  Scalar
$M=\pm I$ cannot supply a fully transverse triangle.

Combining (3.6) and (4.3) is useful for a specified trace, but summing over
all small-denominator traces is still too expensive.  In particular,
integer-trace maps have $q=1$, for which (3.6) gives no gain over the ambient
scale.

The remaining arithmetic target can therefore be stated more precisely:
prove an aggregate saving for the fully transverse maps with

\[
 q\ll m^2/|B_g|^{4/3},                                  \tag{4.5}
\]

especially $q=1$, while retaining the six-distinct arc matching.  A bound on
individual $K$-matrix fibres, without aggregation over traces, will not
suffice.
