# Rank-five normal form for an anchored repeated track

## 1. Outcome

The amplified endpoint-pencil collision fixes one endpoint-labelled track
in each of two owner occurrences.  This note derives the exact relative
owner algebra.  The six track-difference map has complex rank five, with
one and only one gauge direction.

Let two arbitrary owner occurrences have variables

\[
 (V,W,a,b,e,q)_\alpha,qquad(V,W,a,b,e,q)_\beta,
\]

and put

\[
 U=\Delta V,\quad C=\Delta W,\quad A=\Delta a,
 \quad B=\Delta b,\quad E=\Delta e,\quad Q=\Delta q. \tag{1.1}
\]

Their six track differences are

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_0&=U-A-Q,\\
d_1&=C-LB+JQ+JA,\\
d_2&=C-LB+JQ+LA,\\
d_3&=U-A-Q+E,\\
d_4&=C-B+JQ-JE,\\
d_5&=C+JQ-JE.
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{1.2}
\]

They satisfy exactly one linear relation,

\[
\boxed{
 d_0+(I-J)d_1-d_2-d_3+(J-I)d_4+d_5=0.}           \tag{1.3}
\]

The kernel of (1.2) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
 (U,C,A,B,E,Q)=(h,-Jh,0,0,0,h),\qquad h\in\mathbb R^2.} \tag{1.4}
\]

Thus all six tracks are unchanged only when the parameter and first
physical edge translate by `h` and the second physical edge translates by
`-Jh`.  This gauge also preserves the coarse invariant:

\[
 \Delta(JV+W)=JU+C=Jh-Jh=0.                       \tag{1.5}
\]

No other local freedom exists.

## 2. Consequence of one repeated endpoint-labelled track

If the amplified star key reuses track role `j`, then

\[
 d_j=0.                                           \tag{2.1}
\]

Every five rows of the map (1.2) are independent.  Equivalently, for each
`j`, every choice of four among the remaining five track differences,
together with (2.1), has rank five.  Therefore those four differences
determine the full relative owner offset modulo the gauge (1.4), and the
fifth difference is forced by (1.3).

This gives the exact split required by the amplified endpoint-pencil gate:

* the **transverse quotient** has four independent `D-D` directions and no
  hidden local multiplicity;
* the **gauge fibre** consists of different physical wedges/parameters
  producing the same six directed tracks and the same invariant `R`.

The latter is precisely the freedom that the fixed-invariant planting
barrier exhibits.  It must be paid from physical wedge endpoints or by the
`k^3` allowance; it cannot be removed by another track equation.

## 3. Exact matrix proof

Identify the plane with `C`, so `J=i` and `L=1+i`.  In the variable order
`(U,C,A,B,E,Q)`, the matrix of (1.2) is

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&-1&0&0&-1\\
0&1&i&-(1+i)&0&i\\
0&1&1+i&-(1+i)&0&i\\
1&0&-1&0&1&-1\\
0&1&0&-1&-i&i\\
0&1&0&0&-i&i
\end{pmatrix}.                                    \tag{3.1}
\]

Exact Gaussian elimination over `Q(i)` gives

\[
 \operatorname{rank}M=5,                         \tag{3.2}
\]

right kernel generated by `(1,-i,0,0,0,1)`, and left kernel generated by

\[
 (1,1-i,-1,-1,-1+i,1).                           \tag{3.3}
\]

These are (1.4) and (1.3).  The same elimination checks that deletion of
any row leaves rank five.  Hence the unique row circuit uses all six rows
with nonzero coefficients; any five are independent.  Section 2 follows.

## 4. What remains

The rank theorem prevents an accidental high-dimensional anchored
collision.  It does not by itself bound the gauge fibre.  The next theorem
should split the collision mass `Q_star` into:

1. quotient-transverse records, charged through four represented `D-D`
   directions and the existing metric determinant; and
2. gauge-rich records, where many distinct physical wedges share the same
   six tracks and invariant.

For the second branch the tracks are already fixed, so only the physical
wedge endpoint triples move.  This is much smaller than the original
fixed-`R` support problem.  A target-scale theorem may use the exact
three-point equation for the wedge together with the fact that its two edge
vectors change as `(h,-Jh)`.

There is already a sharp first bound on this gauge.  For fixed `R` and fixed
endpoint roles, at most one physical wedge passes through each common
endpoint: two would give nonzero perpendicular differences of equal length
inside `A`.  Hence the gauge fibre contains at most `k` wedges per role pair
and at most `4k` in total.  The polynomial planting attains `Omega(k)`, so
this is optimal.  The remaining aggregate problem is to sum the resulting
linear gauge fibres over fixed six-track templates without losing a further
factor `k`.

One must not claim that the earlier same-`(R,T)` owner switch already covers
this situation: here the two parameter values may differ.  The theorem
above is the correct general replacement and identifies the missing gauge
explicitly.

The endpoint schedule has since been sharpened.  Pairing every pointed
record with all endpoint neighbours is unnecessary.  The cyclic
derangement in `SWAP_CYCLIC_ENDPOINT_DERANGEMENT_GATE.md` schedules every
decoration exactly once and retains the same `O(k^3)` two-track key
universe.  Its only off-diagonal collisions still satisfy the anchored
rank-five theorem above, while its internal collision mass has the exact
periodic formula (3.2) in that note.  Therefore the live aggregate is
`Q_cyc`, not the larger all-neighbours `Q_star`.
