# Bounded tame inertia plus disk packing: the rank-221 certificate

## 1. Outcome

Imposing order two on tame inertia improves the product-disk construction
substantially more than the original rank-713 calculation suggests.  The
relation cost is offset by using fewer Frobenius-square primes, while every
ramified prime contributes only one half of its old root-discriminant
exponent.

Optimizing the rank as well as the local frontier gives the rigorous bound

\[
 \boxed{F_2(n)\ll n^{0.49371397}}.                         \tag{1.1}
\]

The certified presentation has

\[
 d=221,\qquad |T|=222,\qquad |\mathcal Q|=11767,            \tag{1.2}
\]

and uses the first 222 odd rational primes as `T`, ending at 1409.  Every
tame inertia generator and every useful Frobenius receives a square relator.
The exact verifier is `verify_bounded_inertia_disk_rank221.py`.

The previous rank-713 bounded-inertia specialization is also valid and gives
`0.49398319`, but it is superseded by (1.1).  Its companion verifier remains
useful as a regression check.

## 2. Inertia squares and root discriminant

Retain the tame totally-real pro-2 presentation used by the existing
Frobenius construction.  Fix one prime of the maximal extension above an
odd rational prime `p in T`.  Wild inertia is pro-`p`, so its image in a
pro-2 extension is trivial.  The pro-2 Sylow of tame inertia is procyclic;
choose a topological generator of its image and call it `tau_p`.  Add the
single normal relation

\[
 \tau_p^2=1.                                             \tag{2.1}
\]

Every inertia subgroup over the same rational prime is conjugate to the
chosen subgroup.  Quotienting by the **closed normal** subgroup generated by
`tau_p^2` therefore forces every one of them to have order at most two; one
does not need a new relation at every prime in every layer.  Nor does the
local tame relation hide a second cost.  If `sigma_p` is a Frobenius lift,
then

\[
 \sigma_p\tau_p\sigma_p^{-1}=\tau_p^p.                    \tag{2.2}
\]

Since `p` is odd, after (2.1) its right side is `tau_p`.  The resulting
commutation is a consequence of the old local relation and (2.1).

The relator lies in the second Zassenhaus subgroup.  It therefore costs at
most one quadratic relation.  Moreover `tau_p^2` lies in
\(G^2\subseteq\Phi(G)\).  If `N_0` is its closed normal closure, then

\[
 (G/N_0)/\Phi(G/N_0)\cong G/\Phi(G),                       \tag{2.3}
\]

so the generator rank and the useful Frobenius Frattini classes are
unchanged.  Total reality is preserved because we pass to a quotient of the
already totally-real Galois group.  In every finite quotient, the inertia
group at `p` now has order at most two.  Tame discriminant theory gives

\[
 {v_p(\operatorname {Disc}L)\over[L:\mathbb Q]}
 =1-{1\over e_p}\le {1\over2}.                    \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the tower root discriminant satisfies

\[
 \log D\le {1\over2}\sum_{p\in T}\log p.                 \tag{2.5}
\]

For a rank-`d` presentation, use `d+1` ramified primes.  The inherited base
relation bound is `r_0<=d`.  If `N` Frobenius-square primes are capped, the
total quadratic relation bound is

\[
 r\le d+(d+1)+N.                                         \tag{2.6}
\]

The strict quadratic Golod--Shafarevich condition is `4r<d^2`; hence the
largest certified integer value is

\[
 N=\left\lfloor{d^2-1\over4}\right\rfloor-2d-1.          \tag{2.7}
\]

At `d=221`, this gives

\[
 r=12210,\qquad4r=221^2-1,\qquad N=11767.                \tag{2.8}
\]

The first 222 odd primes give 221 independent squareclasses by the same
explicit basis as the rank-713 construction.  The last ramified prime is
1409.  The first 11,767 usable unramified primes end at 128,047; an exact
Legendre-symbol pass finds no rejected prime in this prefix.  Finally,

\[
 \log D=
 672.4345398746246682227716611595607081615952462462477\ldots. \tag{2.9}
\]

## 3. Disk master inequality and exact endpoint certificate

Use the product-of-planar-disks master inequality with the safe rational
constant

\[
 {4\over\pi}<{424\over333}=:C_* .                         \tag{3.1}
\]

Let `F` be the globally sorted all-depth local frontier.  For target exponent
`alpha`, the dyadic endpoint condition is

\[
 F(2\alpha w)\ge
 \log(C_*D)+(2-4\alpha)w+
 \log\!\left(1+{e^{2(2\alpha-1)w}\over C_*D}\right).    \tag{3.2}
\]

Take

\[
 \alpha=0.49371397,\qquad w_0=84898.                      \tag{3.3}
\]

The exact 90-digit computation gives endpoint margins

\[
 0.0057978328337084\ldots,\qquad
 0.0066617014197227\ldots.                                \tag{3.4}
\]

The left endpoint stops fractionally in depth one after 8,100 complete
items.  The right endpoint uses 11,767 first-depth, 4,212 second-depth, and
70 third-depth items before its fractional item.  Its active slope is

\[
 0.0190267807767783\ldots,                                \tag{3.5}
\]

while the largest omitted fourth-depth slope is

\[
 0.0153669070166461\ldots.                                \tag{3.6}
\]

The inherited all-depth monotonicity theorem excludes every later depth.
Concavity between the two dyadic endpoints and the inherited placewise
rounding argument prove (1.1).

## 4. Rank and partial-cap optimization

The floating-point optimizer was rerun at every integer rank `20<=d<=400`,
using the optimistic assumption that every earliest unramified prime is
usable.  This assumption can only make a competing rank look better.  The
minimum occurred at `d=221`:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
d&\hbox{threshold }\alpha\\ \hline
160&0.49374020\\
180&0.49372463\\
200&0.49371652\\
220&0.49371401\\
221&0.4937139563\\
225&0.49371408\\
240&0.49371559\\
300&0.49373494\\
400&0.49379016
\end{array}                                                \tag{4.1}
\]

A broad diagnostic through rank 2,000 continued worsening, reaching about
`0.49448789` at rank 2,000.  This is a numerical scan, not a universal
dominance theorem over every possible arithmetic presentation.

At rank 221, a separate scan used only `s` inertia-square caps, placed on the
`s` largest ramified primes.  Then

\[
 N=\left\lfloor{d^2-1\over4}\right\rfloor-d-s,
 \qquad
 \log D=\sum_{p\in T}\log p-\frac12
          \sum_{p\text{ capped}}\log p.                    \tag{4.2}
\]

The result improved monotonically through all 222 caps:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
s&\alpha\\ \hline
0&0.49486775\\
50&0.49461781\\
100&0.49434863\\
150&0.49406615\\
200&0.49379469\\
220&0.49371687\\
221&0.49371512\\
222&0.4937139563
\end{array}                                                \tag{4.3}
\]

For fixed `s`, choosing the largest ramified primes is numerically dominant:
every cap has the same one-relation cost, while the discriminant saving is
`(log p)/2`.  This comparison assumes the squareclass presentation remains
available; the all-capped certificate itself checks that presentation
exactly.

A contiguous ramification-block offset scan was also performed.  Moving the
222-prime block upward so that the skipped small primes become local useful
primes worsened the result immediately: offsets `0,1,10,100,500` gave
approximately

\[
 0.49371396, 0.49372008, 0.49376034,
 0.49394139, 0.49420109.                                  \tag{4.4}
\]

This supports the initial-prime allocation, but it is explicitly not a
global optimization over all noncontiguous ramification sets.

## 5. Order-four inertia audit

There is enough Golod--Shafarevich slack at rank 713 to impose fourth powers
on all inertia generators without deleting any Frobenius cap.  Indeed,

\[
 P(t)=1-713t+127092t^2+714t^4,                            \tag{5.1}
\]

and at `t=2/713`,

\[
 P(2/713)={-713^2+16\cdot714\over713^4}<0.                \tag{5.2}
\]

These caps force inertia order at most four and change the root-discriminant
logarithm to three quarters of its uncapped value.  The optimized exponent is

\[
 \alpha=0.49430442\ldots.                                 \tag{5.3}
\]

Upgrade `s` of the largest ramified primes from order four to order two and
delete `s` Frobenius caps.  The quadratic coefficient stays 127,092 and the
quartic coefficient becomes `714-s`, so (5.2) remains negative.  Deleting
only `s-1` Frobenius caps is impossible: even after discarding the positive
quartic term, the quadratic coefficient 127,093 exceeds `713^2/4`.
Numerically this mixed family improves monotonically:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
s&\alpha\\ \hline
0&0.49430442\\
100&0.49425611\\
300&0.49415961\\
500&0.49406578\\
700&0.49398581\\
714&0.49398319
\end{array}                                                \tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the all-order-two endpoint is best in this family.  Higher
`2^j`-power caps are weaker geometrically than the already-affordable
order-four cap and cannot improve this particular weighted-GS trade.

## 6. Scope

The tame-inertia quotient used here is proved in Section 2: tame pro-2
inertia is procyclic, closed normal closure handles every conjugate prime,
the local tame relation is compatible because every ramified rational prime
is odd, and the square cap lies in the Frattini subgroup.  The independent
companion audit `BOUNDED_TAME_INERTIA_TOWER_IMPROVEMENT.md` gives the same
argument with the full presentation and discriminant bookkeeping separated
from the numerical optimization.  Subject only to the two external inputs
already declared in the preceding tower proof -- the standard tame totally
real Shafarevich presentation theorem and the placewise prime-power counting
theorem -- the quotient, discriminant calculation, Golod--Shafarevich
budget, disk master inequality, and finite certificate are rigorous.

The rank and allocation scans identify the best member of the tested natural
family; they do not assert a universal optimum over different ramification
sets, stronger presentations, or unrelated number fields.
