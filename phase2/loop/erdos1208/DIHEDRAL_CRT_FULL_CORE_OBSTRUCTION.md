# Generalized-dihedral three-coordinate full cores collapse

## 1. Outcome

The complete abelian obstruction leaves genuinely noncommuting corner
matchings as the only possible full-core branch. This note eliminates the
most natural transitive nonabelian replacement: a three-coordinate core in a
generalized dihedral group.

Let `p,q,r>=2`, put

\[
 H=\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_q\times\mathbb Z_r,
 \qquad G=H\rtimes\{\pm1\},                    \tag{1.1}
\]

where the nontrivial element acts on `H` by inversion. For
`(i,j,k) in {0,1}^3`, let

\[
 s_{ijk}=((i,j,k),1)\in G.                       \tag{1.2}
\]

Right multiplication by the eight reflections (1.2) gives eight
fixed-point-free matchings on the `2pqr` group records.

**Theorem 1.1.** For every `p,q,r>=2`, the system (1.2) is a linear full
eight-corner core. Every corner fibre has size exactly two, and the six
endpoint labels distinguish all `2pqr` formal records. Nevertheless, every
solution of the Gaussian relation equation makes each of the six
endpoint-role functions constant. All records therefore become the same
relation.

Thus this infinite family has no nontrivial geometric realization, let alone
a distance-Sidon realization. The group is genuinely noncommutative whenever
at least one of `p,q,r` is greater than two.

When `p,q,r` are pairwise coprime, `H` is cyclic. The theorem then includes
the ordinary dihedral group of order `2pqr`; after rescaling the three cyclic
coordinates, its eight reflection indices may be written

\[
 iqr+jpr+kpq\pmod {pqr}.                         \tag{1.3}
\]

## 2. Exact full-core structure

Write a group record as `(x,y,z,sigma)`, where `sigma` is zero or one. The
group law is

\[
 (h,\sigma)(h',\tau)
   =(h+(-1)^\sigma h',\sigma+\tau).            \tag{2.1}
\]

For the two endpoint roles in the first coordinate, define

\[
 \lambda_{a,i}(x,y,z,\sigma)=x-\sigma i\pmod p.\tag{2.2}
\]

Similarly,

\[
 \lambda_{b,j}=y-\sigma j\pmod q,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{c,k}=z-\sigma k\pmod r.             \tag{2.3}
\]

The connected components generated by the four reflections on a fixed cube
face are exactly the fibres of the corresponding label in (2.2)--(2.3).
Fixing one label from each coordinate leaves exactly two records, one in each
`sigma` layer, and they are paired by the corresponding `s_(ijk)`. Hence
every corner fibre has size two. Distinct reflections give edge-disjoint
matchings, so the corner hypergraph is linear.

For each coordinate, the two labels recover both the coordinate and
`sigma`: for example they are `x` and `x-sigma` modulo `p`. Since every
modulus is at least two, the six labels distinguish all records. Before the
Gaussian equations there are exactly

\[
 2(p+q+r)                                         \tag{2.4}
\]

formal endpoint labels.

## 3. Characteristic-zero collapse

Let the six endpoint functions be

\[
 A_0,A_1:\mathbb Z_p\to\mathbb C,
 \quad B_0,B_1:\mathbb Z_q\to\mathbb C,
 \quad C_0,C_1:\mathbb Z_r\to\mathbb C.
\]

At the record `(x,y,z,sigma)`, the relation

\[
 a_0-a_1-b_0+b_1-Jc_0+Jc_1=0                    \tag{3.1}
\]

becomes

\[
 A_0(x)-A_1(x-\sigma)-B_0(y)+B_1(y-\sigma)
 -iC_0(z)+iC_1(z-\sigma)=0.                     \tag{3.2}
\]

At `sigma=0`, the three coordinates are independent. The elementary
separation lemma

\[
 F(x)+G(y)+K(z)=0\ \hbox{ for all }(x,y,z)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad F,G,K\hbox{ are constant}        \tag{3.3}
\]

shows that

\[
 A_0-A_1,\qquad -B_0+B_1,\qquad -iC_0+iC_1      \tag{3.4}
\]

are constant functions.

Subtract the `sigma=0` instance of (3.2) from the `sigma=1` instance. Apply
(3.3) once more. Each of the three unit-step first differences of
`A_1,B_1,C_1` is constant on its finite cyclic group. Summing a constant
first difference around a full cycle makes that constant zero. Therefore
`A_1,B_1,C_1` are constant, and (3.4) makes `A_0,B_0,C_0` constant as well.

The universal solution has six geometric point labels and five free Gaussian
parameters, and every record has the same six-tuple. The full core therefore
collapses completely.


The verifier checks the generalized-dihedral involutions, all six exact
face-component labels, exact degree two, corner linearity, record recovery,
and the universal collapse profile

\[
 (2(p+q+r),6,5,0,1,0).                           \tag{4.1}
\]

It tests both non-coprime product groups and pairwise-coprime groups through
`(5,7,11)`. In the coprime cases it independently constructs and checks the
ordinary dihedral CRT presentation (1.3).

This is an infinite noncommuting branch theorem, not a proof of full
eight-corner peelability. Its strategic content is that the obvious
nonabelian Cayley replacement does not evade the obstruction: the two
reflection layers and three separable quotient coordinates force an even
stronger record collapse. Any surviving noncommuting core must lack this
global separable-coset structure as well as the abelian translation
structure.
