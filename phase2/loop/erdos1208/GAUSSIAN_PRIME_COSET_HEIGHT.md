# Critical height for Gaussian-prime coset patches

> **Superseded at the stated level.**
> `GAUSSIAN_IDEAL_COSET_HEIGHT.md` removes the prime-norm hypothesis and
> proves the same critical-height conclusion for every nontrivial coset of
> every exact Gaussian ideal.  This file is retained as the simpler first
> proof and for its independent prime-case certificates.

## 1. Result

The exact rank-two models compatible with the quarter-turn `J` are not
arbitrary oblique lattices.  A full lattice `L subset Z^2` satisfying
`JL=L` is an ideal of `Z[i]`, hence is generated by a Gaussian integer `z`
and `iz`.  The missing issue after the unit-lattice theorem is therefore the
coset of this ideal.

The prime-index case already has the scale required by the cube-root problem.

### Theorem 1.1

There are absolute constants `c>0` and `r_0` with the following property.
Let `z in Z[i]` have odd rational-prime norm

\[
 q=|z|^2,
\]

 let `t in Z[i] \ z Z[i]`, and put

\[
 P=\{t+z(a+ib):0\le a,b<r\}.                    \tag{1.1}
\]

If `P subset [-M,M]^2` and no two distinct non-antipodal points of `P`
have the same norm, then

\[
 \boxed{M\ge c r^{3/2}}.                         \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus a balanced Gaussian-prime patch in a radially unique complete
difference set has at most

\[
 \boxed{|P|=r^2\ll M^{4/3}}.                    \tag{1.3}
\]

The exponent `3/2` is the critical one for Erdős 1208: it converts the side
of a two-dimensional patch into the desired `M^(2/3)` scale.  The theorem
does not assert that (1.2) is sharp, and it does not yet control approximate
patches or the aggregate seven-incidence mass.

## 2. A rational shifted-square lemma

The arithmetic input is the following denominator-sensitive extension of
the unit-grid CRT argument.

### Lemma 2.1

Let `q` be an odd prime and let

\[
 U=P/q,\qquad V=Q/q,qquad 0\le U\le V,          \tag{2.1}
\]

where `P` is nonzero modulo `q`.  Suppose

\[
 P^2+Q^2\equiv0\pmod q.                          \tag{2.2}
\]

For a sufficiently large integer `R`, put

\[
 A=2U+R-1,\qquad B=2V+R-1.                      \tag{2.3}
\]

If

\[
 {qB\over R^2}\le 10^{-6},                      \tag{2.4}
\]

then the rational unit patch

\[
 \{(U+a,V+b):0\le a,b<R\}                       \tag{2.5}
\]

contains two distinct points of equal norm.

### Proof

Write `alpha=B/A`.  Choose an odd prime `e`, different from `q`, in

\[
 {R\over64\alpha}\le e\le {R\over16\alpha}.    \tag{2.6}
\]

For large `R`, two applications of Bertrand's postulate supply such a prime.
Condition (2.4) makes the left endpoint in (2.6) much larger than `q`.

Let

\[
 \lambda\equiv QP^{-1}\pmod q.
\]

Choose `d` congruent to `lambda e` modulo `q`, within `5q` of `alpha e`,
such that `d` is odd and not divisible by `e`.  This is possible among a
bounded number of consecutive representatives: adding `q` changes parity,
and two representatives of the required parity differ by `2q`, which the
prime `e>q` cannot divide simultaneously.  In particular,

\[
 \gcd(d,e)=1,qquad {R\over80}<d<{R\over12}.     \tag{2.7}
\]

Multiply squared norms in (2.5) by `q`.  Differences in the first
coordinate at gap `d` form the arithmetic progression

\[
 \mathcal F_d=
 \{d(2P+qd+2qa):0\le a\le R-1-d\},              \tag{2.8}
\]

with centre `d(2P+q(R-1))=qdA` and half-width
`qd(R-1-d)`.  The second-coordinate progression at gap `e` has centre
`qeB` and half-width `qe(R-1-e)`.

Their centres differ by at most

\[
 qA|d-\alpha e|\le5q^2A.                        \tag{2.9}
\]

Using (2.4), (2.6), and (2.7), this is less than one hundredth of the
smaller half-width.  The overlap of the two underlying intervals is
therefore longer than `2qde`.

The two residue conditions are compatible.  Their moduli have gcd `2q`,
and the residue difference is

\[
 2(Pd-Qe)+q(d^2-e^2),                            \tag{2.10}
\]

which is divisible by `2q`: the first term uses
`d congruent lambda e (mod q)`, while the second uses that `d,e` are odd.
The generalized CRT gives a common residue class of period

\[
 \operatorname{lcm}(2qd,2qe)=2qde.              \tag{2.11}
\]

The interval overlap contains a common term.  Hence, for some admissible
`a,b`,

\[
 (U+a+d)^2+(V+b)^2=(U+a)^2+(V+b+e)^2,           \tag{2.12}
\]

proving the lemma.

## 3. Proof of Theorem 1.1

Write `z=x+iy`.  Dividing (1.1) by `z` is a similarity, so the normalized
patch is a unit grid translated by

\[
 {t\over z}=U+iV,
 \qquad U={t\mathbin\cdot z\over q},\quad
 V={t\mathbin\cdot iz\over q}.                 \tag{3.1}
\]

The numerators in (3.1) satisfy (2.2).  Since `t notin zZ[i]`, the first
one is nonzero modulo `q` after interchanging the two coordinates if needed.

First, the diameter of (1.1) gives

\[
 M\gg r\sqrt q.                                  \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus (1.2) follows immediately when `q>=eta r`, for any fixed absolute
`eta>0`.

It remains to consider `q<<r`, with a sufficiently small absolute implied
constant.  In each normalized coordinate, at least half of the `r`
consecutive values lie on one side of zero.  Restricting both coordinates
and then taking a square subpatch leaves side `R>=r/2`.  Reflect and
interchange the normalized coordinate axes so that its translation obeys
`0<=U<=V`.  These operations preserve norms.

Every normalized point has modulus at most `sqrt(2)M/sqrt(q)`.  Consequently

\[
 B\ll {M\over\sqrt q}+R.                         \tag{3.3}
\]

If `M<c r^(3/2)` for a sufficiently small absolute `c`, then (3.3),
`R>=r/2`, and the present small-`q` assumption give

\[
 {qB\over R^2}\le10^{-6}.                       \tag{3.4}
\]

Lemma 2.1 produces two equal-norm points in the one-quadrant subpatch.
They cannot be antipodal there.  This contradicts radial uniqueness and
proves (1.2).

## 4. Structural meaning

The theorem corrects the scope of the phrase “oblique lattice branch.”
Exact quarter-turn stability forces a Gaussian ideal, so arbitrary oblique
bases are not the canonical inverse model.  The relevant dichotomy is:

1. large prime norm `q` already costs `r sqrt(q)` in physical diameter;
2. small `q` leaves a low-denominator shifted-square grid, where the CRT
   collision applies; and
3. balancing the two costs produces `r^(3/2)`.

For an `r`-by-`s` patch with `r>=s`, the same argument on an `s`-by-`s`
subpatch gives `M>>s^(3/2)`, while the patch is covered by `s` parallel
lines.  This is the exact height/line-cover coupling suggested by the
rectangular unit-grid audit.

The remaining steps toward the full problem are substantial: an inverse
theorem must extract such a quarter-turn-stable patch from supercritical
adaptive rich-fibre mass, allow composite index and approximate closure,
and sum the resulting bounds over all fibres.
