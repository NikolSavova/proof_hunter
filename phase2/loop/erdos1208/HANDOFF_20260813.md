## 1. Problem and notation

For an (n)-point set (P\subset\mathbb R^2), let (f(P)) be the maximum
size of a subset (A\subseteq P) for which all unordered pairwise distances
are distinct.  Define

\[
 F_2(n)=\min_{|P|=n}f(P).
\]

Erdős problem 1208 asks for the order of (F_d(n)), in particular (F_2(n)).

## 4. Proof architecture

### 4.1 Local prime-power flags

Let (F) be a totally real field of degree (m), and let a rational prime
(q\equiv1\pmod4) split completely.  For depth (K\ge1), choose a Hensel
lift (x^2\equiv-1\pmod{q^{2K}}).  At every prime ideal
(\mathfrak q\mid q), impose one of the (K+1) conditions

\[
 u-xv\in\mathfrak q^a,qquad
 u+xv\in\mathfrak q^{K-a},qquad 0\le a\le K.
\]

Each pattern has local index (q^K).  If two patterns differ by (h), their
common occurrence forces an additional (mathfrak q^h) divisor of
(u^2+v^2).  For several primes define

\[
 M=\prod_iq_i^{K_i},\qquad
 H=\prod_i(K_i+1),\qquad
 \Lambda=\prod_i\sum_{e=0}^{K_i}q_i^{-e}.
\]

The (H^m) local choices amplify congruent pairs; ideal-divisor switching
accounts exactly for the overlaps, with cost (Lambda^m).

### 4.2 Geometry of numbers and the master bound

Use the Minkowski embedding of (\mathcal O_F), a translated sup-norm box,
Cauchy--Schwarz across the congruence sublattices, and the fact that a
distance-Sidon set realizes any nonzero algebraic squared distance at most
twice.  Ideal-box packing gives the master estimate (equation (3.5) in the
proof):

\[
 |A|\le M^m+
 \sqrt{\frac{2D\Lambda}{H}}
 \left(4+\frac{M^2}{R^2}\right)^{m/2}R^m,
\]

where (D) bounds the tower's root discriminants and (R\ge M).  Choosing
a box with (R=\sqrt D,n^{1/(2m)}) supplies at least (n) ambient points.

### 4.3 Adaptive phase calculation

Put

\[
 w=\frac{\log n}{2m},\quad L=\log M,\quad
 z(w)=\frac{e^{2(L-w)}}D,
\]

and

\[
 C(w)=\log(D\Lambda/H)+\log(4+z(w)).
\]

The two master-bound exponents are exactly

\[
 E_1(w)=\frac L{2w},\qquad
 E_2(w)=\frac12+\frac{C(w)}{4w}.
\]

For a target (alpha), a safe interval ([a,b]) for one fixed depth vector
is certified by

\[
 \frac L{2a}<\alpha,qquad
 C(b)<(4\alpha-2)b.
\]

The first exponent decreases.  For the second, with
(G(w)=C(w)-(4\alpha-2)w),

\[
 G'(w)=2-4\alpha-\frac{2z(w)}{4+z(w)}.
\]

The verifier checks (z(a)/2<2-4\alpha), which makes (G) increasing on the
interval; hence its right endpoint controls the whole row.  The same check
implies (z<1), equivalently (R>M).

Because tower degrees are (m=2^j), every sufficiently large (n) admits a
degree with (w\in[w_0,2w_0)).  A finite set of overlapping safe intervals
covering that phase interval gives a uniform bound, although the chosen
depth vector is allowed to depend on (n).

## 8. Why the present upper framework cannot solve the full problem

At a split DVR place the quadratic form becomes (st).  Every index-(q^K)
lattice on which (st\in q^K R) is exactly

\[
 q^aR\times q^{K-a}R,qquad 0\le a\le K.
\]

Thus the (K+1) flags are all available maximal-index flags; there is no
hidden local family.  Inert and ramified places contribute no comparable
entropy, and composite moduli merely multiply the local counts.

Writing (h=\log H), (L=\log M), the useful primes for (x^2+y^2) satisfy

\[
 \frac hL\le\frac{\log2}{\log5}.
\]

Even deleting all discriminant, packing, and degree-discretization losses,
the two idealized exponents optimize to

\[
 \frac1{2+h/L}\ge
 \frac1{2+\log2/\log5}=0.411408\ldots.
\]

So this entire binary norm-lattice family cannot reach (1/3).  Adaptive
moduli repair most of the dyadic phase loss but not this continuous entropy
barrier.  A full upper solution needs a genuinely cubic/shared-vertex
mechanism rather than more independent pair flags.

Other upper routes tested and killed:

- **A binary norm in which 2 splits.**  Locally better, but forcing 2 to
  split lowers the GS generator rank and the changed discriminant/form costs
  dominate.  Even an unrealistically favourable relaxation is weaker than
  the current certificate.
- **Composite and deeper prime powers.**  Already exhausted by the local
  classification; no supermultiplicative entropy.
- **Tensor products or higher-degree norms.**  Exact norm additivity moves
  to dimension four or higher.  A planar scale-separated encoding creates
  cross terms.
- **Finite-group translation-invariant colourings.**  A non-collinear
  transitive planar finite orbit is cyclic/dihedral, reducing to a regular
  polygon and square-root Sidon extraction.
- **Concentric polar/layer grids.**  Coordinate-swap collisions give a
  (C_4)-free incidence problem and only an (n^{2/3}) upper mechanism.
- **Three-factor swaps.**  They require three orthogonal directions; the
  plane supplies only two.
- **Unions of translated Minkowski grids.**  Generic translates make cross
  distances unique; aligned translates merely form a larger binary grid.
- **Dense (2^a3^b) tower degrees.**  Nonexplicit towers can remove the
  dyadic loss, but explicit common-splitting/base-change constructions incur
  enough discriminant and unit-rank loss to be weaker than (0.49815).

See `FULL_ATTACK.md` for proofs and quantitative versions.

## 9. Lower-bound attack and exact conditional theorem

Let (T(P)) be the unordered isosceles 3-set conflicts and (Q(P)) the
unordered 4-sets that admit a partition into two equal-length pairs.  A
subset is distance-Sidon exactly when it contains no member of
(T(P)\cup Q(P)).

Pach--Tardos give

\[
 |T(P)|\le n^{\tau+o(1)},qquad
 \tau=\frac{11e-3}{5e-1}=2.136464617\ldots.
\]

If for some fixed (delta>0),

\[
 |Q(P)|\le Cn^{3-\delta},
\]

then independent sampling followed by one-vertex-per-conflict deletion gives,
for every (zeta>0),

\[
 |S|\gg_{C,\delta,\zeta}
 n^{\min\{(1+\delta)/3,(3-\tau)/2\}-\zeta}.
\]

The ceiling is

\[
 (3-\tau)/2=0.431767691\ldots,
\]

reached once (delta\ge0.295303074\ldots).  Hence **any fixed power saving
in the all-distinct four-point distance energy would improve the universal
cube-root exponent**.  The missing case is precisely high distance energy.

High energy alone yields only a distance of multiplicity
(n^{1-\delta-o(1)}).  It does not imply few distances, high additive energy,
a rich line/circle, or an approximate group.  Regular polygons plus generic
points and square grids are explicit obstructions to those shortcuts.

## 10. The square-grid barrier

For (P=[m]^2), (N=m^2), let (r_s) count pairs at squared distance (s).
The sum-of-two-squares divisor bound gives

\[
 r_s\le N^{1+o(1)},\qquad
 \sum_s r_s^2=N^{3+o(1)}.
\]

For the equal-distance 4-conflict hypergraph,

\[
 |\mathcal H_4|=N^{3+o(1)},\quad
 \Delta_1\le N^{2+o(1)},\quad
 \Delta_2\le N^{1+o(1)},\quad
 \Delta_3\le N^{o(1)}.
\]

These match, up to subpolynomial factors, a random
(H^{(4)}(N,c/N)), which has independence number only
(O((N\log N)^{1/3})).  Therefore a theorem seeing only edge count and
ordinary codegrees cannot beat cube-root on the grid.

A dyadic inverse statement shows the energetic grid scale consists of
(N^{1+o(1)}) distance colours, each with (N^{1+o(1)}) edges and local
degree (N^{o(1)}): exactly the generic anti-Ramsey one-factorization scale.
The missing information is arithmetic compatibility among the translation
matchings.

Further no-gos:

- DRC on pair-vertices sees only the disjoint union
  (\bigsqcup_sK_{r_s}) and returns one colour.
- The grid has equal-vector additive energy (\Theta(N^3)), so BSG simply
  returns a rank-two progression—the grid itself.
- Generic semialgebraic Ramsey/Zarankiewicz bounds are weaker than the
  special Guth--Katz distance-energy estimate.
- Row, column, primitive-direction, and (q\equiv3\pmod4) modular occupancy
  inequalities give only (O(m)=O(N^{1/2})) upper bounds.

Exact CP-SAT values through side 8 reproduced the known sequence; OEIS
A271490 already records exact values through side 17.  At side 1000, direct
computation gives (E/(N^3\log N)=0.5966), confirming that the grid genuinely
saturates the energy obstruction.  Full derivation: `HIGH_ENERGY_GRID.md`.

## 11. Two credible routes for the next lower-bound attack

1. **Near-extremal Elekes--Sharir/Guth--Katz stability.**  Prove that a point
   set with near-cubic distance energy either has a tractable one-dimensional
   component or has additional arithmetic constraints that force a rainbow
   subset larger than cube-root.  An inverse theorem ending merely at a
   bounded-rank GAP is insufficient because it returns the grid.
2. **Low-rank Euclidean distance matrices.**  The squared distance matrix
   satisfies

   \[
   D_{ij}=u_i+u_j-2x_ix_j-2y_iy_j,qquad
   u_i=x_i^2+y_i^2,qquad \operatorname{rank}D\le4,
   \]

   and is conditionally negative semidefinite.  Seek a rainbow
   principal-submatrix theorem for this special matrix class.  The rank-two
   additive model has square-root Sidon extraction, suggesting that rank plus
   the Euclidean sign condition may contain information absent from generic
   edge-colourings.

These are research targets, not proved lemmas.
