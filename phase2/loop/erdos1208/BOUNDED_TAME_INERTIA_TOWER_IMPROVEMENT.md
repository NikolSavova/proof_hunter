## 2. One relator really does cap one rational ramified prime

Let `T` be a finite set of odd rational primes and let

\[
 G_T=\operatorname{Gal}(L_T/\mathbb Q)
\]

be the Galois group of the maximal totally real pro-2 extension unramified
away from `T`.  Fix `p in T` and a prime of `L_T` above `p`.  Wild inertia is
a pro-`p` group, hence has trivial image in the pro-2 group `G_T`.  The image
of tame inertia is therefore a quotient of the pro-2 Sylow of tame inertia,
which is procyclic.  Choose a topological generator `tau_p` of this image
(with the evident harmless interpretation when the image is already
finite or trivial).

Form the quotient by the closed normal subgroup generated by
`tau_p^2`.  Every inertia subgroup above the rational prime `p` is conjugate
to the chosen one.  Consequently the single **normal** relation

\[
 \tau_p^2=1                                      \tag{2.1}
\]

forces every inertia image above `p` to have order at most two.  It is not
necessary to add one relation for every prime in every finite layer.

The familiar local tame relation causes no hidden extra cost.  With `sigma`
a Frobenius lift it has the form

\[
 \sigma\tau_p\sigma^{-1}=\tau_p^p.               \tag{2.2}
\]

Since `p` is odd, (2.1) makes the right side equal to `tau_p`; the resulting
commutation is already a consequence of the old local relation and the new
square relation.  No second new relator is being suppressed.

## 3. Rank, presentation, and infinitude

Let `N` be any closed normal subgroup of a pro-2 group `G` contained in its
Frattini subgroup

\[
 \Phi(G)=\overline{G^2[G,G]}.
\]

Then

\[
 (G/N)/\Phi(G/N)\cong G/\Phi(G).                  \tag{3.1}
\]

In particular, quotienting by normal closures of squares does not change
the generator rank.  Both an inertia cap `tau_p^2` and a useful-prime cap
`g_q^2` are squares, so (3.1) applies simultaneously to all of them.

Now take `T` to be the first 222 odd primes.  The positive odd quadratic
discriminants supported on `T` form a 221-dimensional `F_2`-space: the
parity of the number of prime factors congruent to 3 modulo 4 is the single
nonzero linear condition.  The standard tame totally-real presentation
therefore has

\[
 d(G_T)=221,\qquad r(G_T)\le 221.                 \tag{3.2}
\]

Add the 222 inertia-square relations, one at each prime in `T`, and the
Frobenius-square relations at the first 11,767 useful unramified primes
under the order-two criterion in `proof_frobenius_order_two.md`.  The
quotient `G` has

\[
 \begin{split}
 d(G)&=221,\\
 r(G)&\le221+222+11767=12210,\\
 4r(G)&\le48840<48841=221^2.                      \tag{3.3}
 \end{split}
\]

Thus the strict Golod--Shafarevich inequality makes `G` infinite.  This
argument already accounts for the possible worst-case base relation rank;
there is no assumed cancellation between a Shafarevich relator and an
inertia cap.

Because every added relation lies in `Phi(G_T)`, the full Frattini quotient
survives.  Hence the useful-prime residue-degree argument is unchanged:
all selected useful primes have residue degree at most two, while a selected
prime congruent to 3 modulo 4 has nonzero Frattini Frobenius and therefore
has residue degree exactly two in the retained finite layers.  As in the
existing Frobenius-square construction, finite quotients with kernel inside
the Frattini subgroup and normal 2-group filtrations supply the required
degrees while retaining this property.

## 4. Exact root-discriminant saving

Let `K/Q` be a finite Galois layer of the quotient tower.  At `p in T`, its
ramification index `e_p` is the order of an image of tame inertia, so

\[
 e_p\in\{1,2\}.                                   \tag{4.1}
\]

All ramification is tame because every `p in T` is odd.  The tame different
formula gives

\[
 v_p(\operatorname{disc}K)
   =[K:\mathbb Q]\left(1-\frac1{e_p}\right)
   \le \frac12[K:\mathbb Q].                     \tag{4.2}
\]

It follows uniformly over all finite layers that

\[
 \operatorname{rd}(K)
 \le D_{\mathrm{in}}:=\prod_{p\in T}p^{1/2}.      \tag{4.3}
\]

There is no contribution at 2, which is outside `T`, and no archimedean
discriminant factor.  Total reality is inherited on passing to a subfield
of `L_T`.

For the 222 primes used here, the verifier obtains

\[
 \log D_{\mathrm{in}}
 =672.4345398746246682227716611595607\ldots.       \tag{4.4}
\]

## 5. The fourth-power refinement

The square cap is not the only valid one.  In a minimal free pro-2
presentation `F -> G_T`, a lift `x_p` of `tau_p` satisfies

\[
 x_p^4\in F_{(4)},                                \tag{5.1}
\]

where `F_(j)` is the Zassenhaus filtration.  Thus imposing `tau_p^4=1`
adds a relation of degree at least four, keeps the Frattini quotient, and
forces `e_p<=4`.  Its local root-discriminant factor is at most `p^(3/4)`.

More generally, if `c_2` inertia generators receive square caps and `c_4`
receive fourth-power caps, while `N` useful Frobenius elements receive
square caps, the safe weighted Golod--Shafarevich polynomial is

\[
 P(t)=1-dt+(r_0+N+c_2)t^2+c_4t^4.                \tag{5.2}
\]

Any `t in (0,1)` for which `P(t)<0` proves infinitude.  This is the precise
mixed-cap optimization problem; it is not correct to charge a fourth-power
cap as a full quadratic relator.

For example, retain the old rank-713 data

\[
 d=713,\quad r_0+N=127092,\quad c_4=714,\quad c_2=0.
\]

At `t=d/(2(r_0+N))`, (5.2) becomes

\[
 -\frac1{d^2-1}
 +\frac{16(d+1)d^4}{(d^2-1)^4}<0,                \tag{5.3}
\]

so all 714 order-four inertia caps fit without deleting any of the 126,379
useful-prime caps.  Numerically, however, the weaker exponent `3/4` in the
root discriminant loses more than this saved relation budget gains.  A scan
of the mixed square/fourth-power choices likewise favored upgrading every
ramified prime to an order-two cap near the best ranks.  This numerical
dominance is not used in the proof of (1.1).

## 7. Adversarial audit summary

The proposed failure modes do not occur:

* **one versus many inertia generators:** tame pro-2 inertia is procyclic,
  and normal closure handles every conjugate prime;
* **rank loss:** every new cap is a square and lies in the Frattini subgroup;
* **hidden local relation:** the tame Frobenius--inertia relation is already
  present and needs no additional cap-dependent relator;
* **torsion:** the quotient is allowed to have finite inertia images; finite
  order in a quotient of an absolute Galois group is not an obstruction;
* **infinite quotient:** (3.3), or the weighted condition (5.2), proves it;
* **discriminant normalization:** (4.2) is per absolute degree, so the factor
  is exactly `p^(1/2)`, not `p` or `p^(1/4)`;
* **useful-prime damage:** the Frattini quotient and all unramified-at-`q`
  conditions survive the quotient.

Accordingly, bounded tame inertia is a genuine improvement to the tower,
not merely a conditional heuristic.
