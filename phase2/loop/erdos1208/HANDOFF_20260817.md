## 4. Falsified full-resolution target: rotated triple energy

For a distance-Sidon set

\[
 A\subset[m]^2,\qquad |A|=k,
\]

let \(J(x,y)=(-y,x)\).  The exact directness relation is

\[
 (A-A)\cap J(A-A)=\{0\},\qquad |A+JA|=k^2.       \tag{2}
\]

Consider

\[
 \Phi(a,b,c)=a+J(b-c).
\]

Its \(k^3+O(k^2)\) inputs lie in a box of \(O(m^2)\) lattice points.  Hence
the representation energy satisfies

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\gg k^6/m^2.                    \tag{3}
\]

The initially desired theorem

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\le k^{3+o(1)}                  \tag{4}
\]

would give \(k\le m^{2/3+o(1)}\), solving the power-law order of #1208.
It is false: the perpendicular-ruler construction has
\(\mathcal E_J(A)=\Omega(k^4)\), matching the universal upper bound from the
fibre-matching property.  See `PERPENDICULAR_RULER_OBSTRUCTION.md`.

The offset in that construction can be chosen so the containing square has
side (m=k^{2+o(1)}).  This follows because a cross-distance collision forces
one Golomb-ruler difference to divide a fixed difference of squares, leaving
only a divisor-function number of bad choices per horizontal pair.  It kills
every nonnegative monomial overlap estimate on the cube-root critical line.

The sharp successor is the non-monomial density estimate

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\ll k^{3+o(1)}+m^{2+o(1)}.       \tag{4a}
\]

It would still imply (k\ll m^{2/3+o(1)}), and the two terms are separately
necessary: generic examples have the diagonal (k^3) scale, while the
perpendicular-ruler family has (k^4=m^{2+o(1)}).  No proof or counterexample
to (4a) is currently known.

### Cross-sum factorization

Put \(B=A+JA\).  Fourier Cauchy--Schwarz gives the exact inequality

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\le\sqrt{E^+(B)E^+(A)},
 \qquad E^+(A)=2k^2-k.                           \tag{5}
\]

Therefore:

* \(E^+(A+JA)\le k^{4+o(1)}\) would prove (4) and solve the problem;
* even \(E^+(A+JA)\le k^{5+o(1)}\) would give
  \(k\le m^{4/5+o(1)}\).

Both proposed energy bounds are false.  Split a dense Golomb ruler into two
sub-rulers with disjoint distance spectra, place them on perpendicular axes,
and use a generic integer offset.  The union is a lattice distance-Sidon set
yet has \(E^+(A+JA)=\Omega(k^6)\).  Thus (5) cannot by itself give any power
saving.  See `PERPENDICULAR_RULER_OBSTRUCTION.md`.

The cleanest current analytic form is

\[
 E^+(A+JA)
 =\frac1{|G|}\sum_\chi
 |\widehat{1_A}(\chi)|^4
 |\widehat{1_A}(J^*\chi)|^4.                    \tag{6}
\]

The missing input is an uncertainty/inverse theorem saying that high additive
structure cannot occur simultaneously in perpendicular Fourier directions for
a radially unique difference set.

### Stronger pointwise route

Put \(D=(A-A)\setminus\{0\}\) and

\[
 M_A(t)=|D\cap(t-JD)|.
\]

Since \(\sum_tM_A(t)=|D|^2\asymp k^4\) and \(D+JD\) lies in only
\(O(m^2)\) lattice positions, the bound

\[
 \max_t M_A(t)\le k^{1+\theta+o(1)}

## 22. Rotation-averaged cubic support

`ROTATION_AVERAGED_CUBIC_SUPPORT.md` proves a positive theorem for the exact
triple map.  If `mathcal R` is any set of `r` distinct rotations and

\[
 \mathcal E_U(A)=\#\{(a,b,c,a',b',c'):
 a+U(b-c)=a'+U(b'-c')\},
\]

then

\[
 \sum_{U\in\mathcal R}\mathcal E_U(A)
 \le r(2k^3-k^2)+2k^4.                         \tag{22.1}
\]

For diagonal collisions, vector-Sidonicity gives exactly `2k^3-k^2` per
rotation.  For a non-diagonal collision, fix `(b,c,b',c')`; its nonzero vector
has the length of at most two directed edges of `A`, and each such vector pair
determines exactly one orientation-preserving rotation.  This proves (22.1).
Consequently some `U` has

\[
 |A+U(A-A)|\ge
 \frac{k^6}{2k^3-k^2+2k^4/r}.                  \tag{22.2}
\]

In particular, `r>=k` gives cubic support `k^3/4`.  If a hostile additive box
contains every support in at most `M` positions, then

\[
 r\le k\Longrightarrow k\le2\sqrt{M/r},
 \qquad
 r\ge k\Longrightarrow k\le(4M)^{1/3}.         \tag{22.3}
\]

This is the exact bridge from many low-expansion unimodular units to the
cube-root upper bound.  It is essentially equal in strength to the earlier
disjoint-rotation lemma but now controls the full triple support directly.
It does not solve the prescribed `J` case: the perpendicular-ruler family
makes one rotation exceptional, and the square lattice has only four integral
rotations.  Rational Pythagorean rotations incur a denominator-square ambient
cost which cancels their multiplicity.  The live arithmetic question remains
the construction of a bounded-root-discriminant non-CM tower with linearly
many sufficiently small independent relative units.

## 23. Shear-averaged cubic support

The rotation theorem now has an affine companion.  If `L` is the maximum
number of collinear points of a distance-Sidon set `A`, `|A|=k`, then every

\[
 S_t=J+tI
\]

\[
 \sum_t\mathcal E_t(A)
 \le r(2k^3-k^2)+k^5L.                            \tag{23.1}
\]

\[
 |A+S_t(A-A)|

`alpha e`, where `alpha=(2V+r-1)/(2U+r-1)`.  Their centres differ by at most
`2U+r-1`, while their half-widths are much larger.  The residue conditions
are compatible because `d,e` are odd, and the overlap is longer than
`lcm(2d,2e)`.  CRT gives equal square differences and hence two
non-antipodal points of equal norm.

Thus a full unit patch in `D subset [-m,m]^2` has `r^2=O(m)` points.  Read
`UNIT_LATTICE_RICH_FIBRE_HEIGHT.md` and run its verifier; it checks 411,502
medium-scale CRT certificates in addition to small exact optima and large

## 40. Rectangular patches: the sharp aspect-ratio dichotomy

The tempting extension of Section 39 from an `r x r` square to an `r x s`
rectangle with height `Omega(rs)` is false.  For `r>=s`,

\[
 \{(s^2+i,j):0\le i<r,\ 0\le j<s\}             \tag{40.1}
\]

has pairwise distinct squared norms.  Indeed, if two norms agree and their
first coordinates differ, the difference of first-coordinate squares has
magnitude at least `2s^2+1`, while the difference of second-coordinate
squares has magnitude at most `(s-1)^2`, a contradiction.

Conversely, every unit `r x s` rectangle contains an `s x s` subpatch, so
Section 39 forces translation height `Omega(s^2)`.  Therefore the optimal
height is

\[
 \Theta(\min(r,s)^2),                            \tag{40.2}
\]

and the containing coordinate scale is `Theta(r+s^2)` when `r>=s`.
The thin escape is structurally visible: it is a union of `s` parallel
lines, so Section 37 gives `|Q|<=s sqrt(S)`.  The next rank-two result must
couple this line-cover bound with height; an area-only theorem cannot work.
Sparse and oblique patches, and the aggregate off-diagonal `Z_K` bound,
remain open.  Read `RECTANGULAR_LATTICE_HEIGHT_DICHOTOMY.md` and run its
exact verifier.

## 41. Prime Gaussian cosets have the critical `r^(3/2)` height

The exact quarter-turn-stable lattice model is narrower than an arbitrary
oblique progression.  If `L subset Z^2` and `JL=L`, then `L` is an ideal of
`Z[i]`, hence `L=zZ[i]` for a Gaussian integer `z`.

The nontrivial coset is now controlled when `q=|z|^2` is an odd rational
prime.  If

\[
 P=t+z\{a+ib:0\le a,b<r\}\subset[-M,M]^2,
 \qquad t\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus z\mathbb Z[i],
\]

is radially unique and `t notin zZ[i]`, then

\[
 M\gg r^{3/2},\qquad |P|=r^2\ll M^{4/3}.         \tag{41.1}
\]

After division by `z`, the translate has coordinates `U=P_0/q,V=Q_0/q`
with `P_0^2+Q_0^2=0 (mod q)`.  If `q` is large, the physical diameter
`r sqrt(q)` proves (41.1).  If `q` is small, choose odd coprime gaps `d,e`
which both approximate the centre ratio and satisfy
`P_0d=Q_0e (mod q)`.  Their shifted-square difference progressions have
compatible residues modulo `2q`, overlap by more than their CRT period
`2qde`, and force two equal radii.  Balancing the two branches gives the
`r^(3/2)` exponent.

For an `r x s` prime-Gaussian rich fibre, `r>=s`, apply (41.1) to the square
subpatch and Section 37 to its `s` parallel lines:

\[
 |Q|=rs\ll M^{2/3}\sqrt{|D+D|}.                  \tag{41.2}
\]

This closes the exact prime-Gaussian patch at the cube-root-critical local
height.  It does not prove that a supercritical adaptive fibre contains such
a patch.  The next section removes the composite-ideal restriction;
approximate modules and aggregation in the off-diagonal `Z_K` estimate remain
the live tasks.  Read `GAUSSIAN_PRIME_COSET_HEIGHT.md`; its 21 exact
certificates pass.

## 42. The prime restriction is removable: every exact Gaussian ideal closes

\[
 t\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus z\mathbb Z[i],\qquad
 t+z\{a+ib:0\le a,b<r\}\subset[-M,M]^2
\]

is radially unique, then

\[
 M\gg r^{3/2}.                                   \tag{42.1}
\]

The new point is to reduce `t/z` to coordinates `p/h,s/h` with

 pd_0\equiv se_0\pmod h,

Set `d=2d_0,e=2e_0`.  The difference-progression moduli then have gcd
`4h`, their residues agree modulo `4h`, and their common period is `hde`.
The same physical-diameter/CRT dichotomy proves (42.1), without a primality,
splitting, or oddness assumption on `N(z)`.

## 43. Arbitrary exact oblique patches: Gaussian core gives `r^(6/5)`

Section 42 closes every exact `J`-stable lattice, but a general rich-fibre
lattice `Lambda` need not be `J`-stable.  The forced copy
`(I+J)Lambda` does not imply `JLambda=Lambda`.

Let `L` be an arbitrary nonsingular integer basis matrix and consider a
complete `r x r` coefficient patch.  The identity

\[
 L\operatorname{adj}(L)=(\det L)I
\]

extracts a Gaussian-ideal subpatch of side `R>>r/B`, where `B` is the largest
basis length.  Physical diameter costs `rB`, while the Gaussian theorem costs
`R^(3/2)`.  Therefore every radially unique exact oblique patch in a box of
radius `M` obeys

\[
 M\gg\max\{rB,(r/B)^{3/2}\}\gg r^{6/5}.          \tag{43.1}
\]

For an `r x s` adaptive fibre patch this gives

\[
 |Q|\ll M^{5/6}\sqrt S.                           \tag{43.2}
\]

The exact collision condition for two coefficient points `x,x+2n` is

\[
 (L^TLn)\cdot x=-n^TL^Tt-n^TL^TLn.               \tag{43.3}
\]

The missing critical oblique theorem is a short inhomogeneous solution of
(43.3), including its gcd divisibility condition, without passing to the
adjugate core.  `OBLIQUE_LATTICE_GAUSSIAN_CORE.md` contains the proof and its
verifier; seven basis families and 700 exact collision identities pass.

The desired exponent `3/2` is best possible.  For every `B>=2`, put
`r=B^2`; then the unimodular shear patch

\[
 \{(B(a+b)+b,a+b):0\le a,b<r\}
\]

has all `r^2` radii distinct and lies at height `O(B^3)=O(r^(3/2))`.
If two points collide, orient them with diagonal-coordinate gap `k>0` and
horizontal drop `h>0`.  Factoring the equality gives `hB<=k`, while the
coefficient range gives `k<=B-1`, a contradiction.  The verifier checks the
family exhaustively through `B=30`.

## 44. Sharp arbitrary-oblique theorem: the exact complete lattice gate closes

The missing `6/5`-to-`3/2` step in Section 43 has a direct modular-midpoint
solution.  Let `L in M_2(Z)` be nonsingular and suppose

\[
 \{t+Lx:x\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}^2\}\subset[-M,M]^2

is radially unique up to antipodes.  Patch diameter gives
`B=max(|v|,|w|)<=6M/r` for the columns `v,w` of `L`.  If
`M<c r^(3/2)`, choose a prime `r/100<q<r/40`.  For small absolute `c`,
`0<|det L|<=B^2<q`, so `G=L^TL` is invertible modulo `q`.

Solve

\[
 Gm\equiv-L^Tt\pmod q
\]

and choose a representative `m` in the central coefficient square with
`p=t+Lm!=0`.  Then `a=L^Tp` is divisible by `q`, and

\[
 n=J(a/q)\in\mathbb Z^2,\qquad a\cdot n=0.
\]

Moreover `||n||_infty<=sqrt(2)BM/q=O(M^2/r^2)<r/10`, so both `m+n` and
`m-n` remain in the coefficient box.  The points `p+Ln` and `p-Ln` are
distinct, non-antipodal, and have equal norm because

\[
 |p+Ln|^2-|p-Ln|^2=4(L^Tp)\cdot n=0.
\]

This contradiction proves the sharp universal bound

\[
 M\gg r^{3/2}.
\]

The shear family in Section 43 shows sharpness.  For an `r x s` adaptive
rich-fibre patch, `r>=s`, the square subpatch gives `s<<M^(2/3)` and the
parallel-line theorem gives `r<=sqrt(S)`, hence

 |Q|\ll M^{2/3}\sqrt S.

## 45. Sparse stability fails down to the `r^(5/4)` height scale

The proposed bridge from supercritical seven-incidence mass to a dense

 P_{B,T}=\{(B(a+b)+b,a+b):0\le a,b<r\}.

\[
 |Q|\ge r^2-T^2r,

## 47. The purported tensor barrier is invalid

 (\lambda_1b+\lambda_2x,\lambda_1c+\lambda_2x),\qquad
 (\lambda_1b+\lambda_2y,\lambda_1c+\lambda_2y)

directed-difference uniqueness determines the endpoints of a nonzero base

There is also an immediate cardinality contradiction.  The six-point base
has `31` difference values.  The draft claimed that a two-fold tensor had
`31^2=961` difference values and was distance-Sidon.  If its digit map is

## 51. Determinant-prime resonance is a codimension-one branch
