## 3. Why the present pair sieve will not reach one third

`LOCAL_PSD_BARRIER.md` proves a sharp local obstruction.  In hyperbolic
coordinates over \(\mathbb F_q\), the norm-zero relation is the union of the
two coordinate axes.  A symmetric translation-invariant kernel supported on
those axes, with weight \(b\) on a nonzero axial difference and \(a\) at zero,
has smallest Fourier eigenvalue \(a-2b\).  Positive semidefiniteness forces

\[
 a\ge2b.
\]

The current sum of the two branch-equivalence kernels attains equality.  Thus
the diagonal double count, and its divisor factor, cannot be removed by a
universal Cauchy kernel, nonnegative branch weights, or a thinned pattern code.
A genuinely higher-order statistic is needed.

## 4. Falsified full-resolution target: rotated triple energy

For a distance-Sidon set

\[
 A\subset[m]^2,\qquad |A|=k,
\]

let \(J(x,y)=(-y,x)\).  The exact directness relation is

\[
 (A-A)\cap J(A-A)=\{0\},\qquad |A+JA|=k^2.       \tag{2}
\]

Consider

\[
 \Phi(a,b,c)=a+J(b-c).
\]

Its \(k^3+O(k^2)\) inputs lie in a box of \(O(m^2)\) lattice points.  Hence
the representation energy satisfies

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\gg k^6/m^2.                    \tag{3}
\]

The initially desired theorem

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\le k^{3+o(1)}                  \tag{4}
\]

would give \(k\le m^{2/3+o(1)}\), solving the power-law order of #1208.
It is false: the perpendicular-ruler construction has
\(\mathcal E_J(A)=\Omega(k^4)\), matching the universal upper bound from the
fibre-matching property.  See `PERPENDICULAR_RULER_OBSTRUCTION.md`.

The offset in that construction can be chosen so the containing square has
side (m=k^{2+o(1)}).  This follows because a cross-distance collision forces
one Golomb-ruler difference to divide a fixed difference of squares, leaving
only a divisor-function number of bad choices per horizontal pair.  It kills
every nonnegative monomial overlap estimate on the cube-root critical line.

The sharp successor is the non-monomial density estimate

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\ll k^{3+o(1)}+m^{2+o(1)}.       \tag{4a}
\]

It would still imply (k\ll m^{2/3+o(1)}), and the two terms are separately
necessary: generic examples have the diagonal (k^3) scale, while the
perpendicular-ruler family has (k^4=m^{2+o(1)}).  No proof or counterexample
to (4a) is currently known.

### Cross-sum factorization

Put \(B=A+JA\).  Fourier Cauchy--Schwarz gives the exact inequality

\[
 \mathcal E_J(A)\le\sqrt{E^+(B)E^+(A)},
 \qquad E^+(A)=2k^2-k.                           \tag{5}
\]

Therefore:

* \(E^+(A+JA)\le k^{4+o(1)}\) would prove (4) and solve the problem;
* even \(E^+(A+JA)\le k^{5+o(1)}\) would give
  \(k\le m^{4/5+o(1)}\).

Both proposed energy bounds are false.  Split a dense Golomb ruler into two
sub-rulers with disjoint distance spectra, place them on perpendicular axes,
and use a generic integer offset.  The union is a lattice distance-Sidon set
yet has \(E^+(A+JA)=\Omega(k^6)\).  Thus (5) cannot by itself give any power
saving.  See `PERPENDICULAR_RULER_OBSTRUCTION.md`.

The cleanest current analytic form is

\[
 E^+(A+JA)
 =\frac1{|G|}\sum_\chi
 |\widehat{1_A}(\chi)|^4
 |\widehat{1_A}(J^*\chi)|^4.                    \tag{6}
\]

The missing input is an uncertainty/inverse theorem saying that high additive
structure cannot occur simultaneously in perpendicular Fourier directions for
a radially unique difference set.

### Stronger pointwise route

Put \(D=(A-A)\setminus\{0\}\) and

\[
 M_A(t)=|D\cap(t-JD)|.
\]

Since \(\sum_tM_A(t)=|D|^2\asymp k^4\) and \(D+JD\) lies in only
\(O(m^2)\) lattice positions, the bound

\[
 \max_t M_A(t)\le k^{1+\theta+o(1)}
\]

would imply

\[
 F_2(n)\le n^{1/(3-\theta)+o(1)}.
\]

These implications are correct, but every size-only power saving is false.
The same perpendicular-ruler construction has a nonzero translation with
\(M_A(t)=\Omega(k^2)\).  The viable successor must use the ambient side length
\(m\): a bound \(M_A(t)\ll m^\alpha k^\beta\) reaches the cube-root grid bound
when \(3\alpha+2\beta\le2\).  See `CROSS_TRANSLATION_OVERLAP.md`.

## 5. Live arithmetic target: exact small rotations

`UNIMODULAR_UNIT_ROTATIONS.md` gives a separate mechanism.  In an imaginary
non-CM field closed under conjugation, relative units are exact rotations at
the chosen planar embedding.  A subset-product family yields a polynomial
saving if there are linearly many independent units with

\[
 \sum_j(\log2-\log M_K(\epsilon_j))\ge\gamma[K:\mathbb Q].
\]

Akhtari--Vaaler imply the necessary regulator condition

\[
 R_{K/F}^{1/r}<\log2.
\]

No bounded-root-discriminant non-CM tower with the required small relative
successive minima is known.  Existing regulator and height lower bounds do not
rule it out.  A Galois-orbit formulation asks for a relative Minkowski unit of
linear orbit rank and full-field log height below \((\log2)/2\).  Primitive
Galois generators are excluded by Amoroso--Masser, but their theorem does not
exclude an element of smaller orbit degree inside a much larger tower.

## 10. New sharp gate: local transverse quarter-turn overlap

The global transverse-collision target has an exact local form.  Put
\(D=A-A\) and, for \(d\in D\), define

\[
 m_{\rm tr}(d)=
 \#\{e\in D\setminus\{0\}: d-Je\in D,\ d\cdot e\ne0\}.
\]

The substitution \(d'=d-Je\) is bijective and
\(\det(d,d')=-d\cdot e\), so

\[
 2E_{\rm tr}(A)=\sum_{d\in D}m_{\rm tr}(d).
\]

Because \(|D|=k^2-k+1\), it would be enough to prove the clean local theorem

\[
 \max_{d\in D}m_{\rm tr}(d)\le k^{1+o(1)}.       \tag{10.1}
\]

For fixed \(d\), the map \(T_d(e)=d-Je\) is a quarter-turn about
\((d-Jd)/2\).  Thus (10.1) says that the realized difference set of a
distance-Sidon configuration has only near-linear overlap with any such
quarter-turned copy after the collinear pairs are removed.  This exclusion is
exactly what makes the formulation survive the perpendicular-ruler family:
all its quadratic local overlaps have \(d\cdot e=0\), and its transverse
local overlap is zero.

## 15. Spectral and unrestricted-energy audit

The row--colour matrix has the sufficient spectral estimate

\[
 \|B\|_{\rm op}\le k^{1+o(1)}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_dr(d)^2\le k^{4+o(1)}.
\]

Its simplest Schur strengthening is not a safe target.  For the fixed heavy
row `d=(0,-1)`, the exact two-step mass
`sum_{e:B(d,e)=1}c(e)` rises from `36,740=10.206...k^2` at `k=60` to
`276,604=19.209...k^2=1.753...k^(5/2)` at `k=120`; the `k^(5/2)`
normalization is much more stable.  Numerical operator norms also rise from
`1.689k` at `k=60` to `2.268k` at `k=120`.  These finite data do not refute a
subpolynomial spectral estimate, but they kill the idea that a uniform local
two-step bound is the easy missing lemma.

There is an exact global identity.  Removing only the transverse indicators,
the restricted row moment is

\[
 \sum_{q,e,f}1_D(e)1_D(e+q)1_D(f)1_D(f-Jq)1_D(f+Je).
\]

Deleting the last factor gives the ordinary mixed energy
`sum_q R_D(q)R_D(Jq)`.  That relaxation is already
`1,344,282,105=55.988...k^4` at `k=70`, with data consistent with a
`k^5` law.  Thus an unrestricted Fourier/BSG relaxation loses the full power
that the proof needs.  The complete derivation, tables, and exact verifier
are in `TRANSVERSE_SPECTRAL_AUDIT.md` and
`verify_transverse_spectral_audit.py`.

## 16. Row--source `C_4` gate and translation slices

Put `B=A+JA`, so directness gives `|B|=k^2`.  There is an exact bipartite
graph with left vertices `d in D`, right vertices the uniquely decorated
sources `p in B`, and an edge when `p,p+d in B` give a transverse relation.
Its left degrees are the row degrees `r(d)`.  If `Q_A` is its number of
unlabelled four-cycles and `W=sum_d binom(r(d),2)`, then

\[
 W^2\le {k^2\choose2}(W+2Q_A).
\]

Consequently `Q_A<=k^(4+o(1))` implies `W<=k^(4+o(1))`, hence the cubic
transverse bound.  Exact heavy-row counts at `k=16,20,30,40,50,60` are
`3349,33067,716180,4284047,12210794,29370111`; all stay on the fourth-power
scale.  At `k=60`, `89.4%` of the cycles use endpoint-disjoint row edges, so
overlap cleanup is not the missing argument.

A separate exact 45-point witness has one right/source degree `250`, killing
any linear maximum-source shortcut.  The fourth moment disintegrates as
`W=sum_{q/{+-1}}W_q`, where

\[
 W_q=\#\{(e,f):e,e+q,f,f-Jq,f+Je\in D\}
\]

up to the transverse indicators.  Dropping the fifth incidence gives the
known `k^5` mixed energy.  At `k=60` there are `187055` active sign-classes,
largest slice `3322`, and average mass only `Theta(k)`: the obstruction is
global rather than one exceptional translation.  See
`TRANSVERSE_ROW_SOURCE_C4_GATE.md` and
`verify_transverse_row_source_c4.py`.  Its optional 122-point extension has
`T=2925748`, row moment `770269576`, maximum row `971`, and
`W=383671914`.

## 17. Longest-edge charging audited and pointwise route killed

Every relation `d=f+Je` has a unique longest underlying edge.  Charging to it
would solve the cubic target if each edge received only `k^(1+o(1))`
relations.  The proposal looked substantially better than the raw row maximum
on all old witnesses, but a targeted strict-global-diameter closure now gives
a decisive finite obstruction.

Fix the diameter `(10000,0)` and keep all other points strictly inside the
circle of centre `(5000,0)` and radius `4990`.  The exact distance-Sidon
closure reaches `k=90`.  Its fixed-diameter row counts are

\[
 61,90,180,266\quad\hbox{at}\quad k=35,45,70,90,
\]

equal to `0.295,0.298,0.307,0.312` times `k^(3/2)`.  At `k=90` the complete
transverse count is `336428=0.46149 k^3`, while the diameter edge receives
`1124=1.31644 k^(3/2)` charges.  Thus the pointwise longest-edge load displays
the same square-root-heavy scale even when the edge is the strict global
diameter.

The proposed two-graphic-forest proof is rigorously false: the diameter row
has `(size,union rank)=(90,83)` at `k=45` and `(266,173)` at `k=90`.
The older 120-point witness also has four longest-column failures, with
`(104,100)` twice and `(60,58)` twice.  The surviving charge theorem is again
the global moment

\[
 \sum_g c(g)^2\le k^{4+o(1)},
\]

or its equivalent rich-tail formulation.  Exact moment normalizations are
`2.540,3.644,6.521` on heavy prefixes `k=30,60,120` and `0.764` on the
diameter witness.  This is a reorganization, not yet a proof, of the same
fourth-moment obstruction.  Read `TRANSVERSE_LONGEST_EDGE_CHARGE_AUDIT.md`;
the exact certificate is `verify_transverse_longest_charge.py` and its
matroid/intersection machinery is in `analyze_transverse_longest_charge.py`.

**Restart priority.**  Do not pursue a pointwise longest-edge or two-forest
bound.  The sharp live target remains the global fourth moment / row--source
`C_4` / decorated-parallelogram estimate.  A useful next structural attack is
an inverse theorem for a large *tail* of longest-edge charges, not for one
heavy diameter.  Even after the wide transverse theorem, the intermediate
line-rich splice remains a separate obligation.

## 18. Fixed-row six-projection `C_4` gate

There is a sharper intermediate theorem between the false pointwise-linear
row bound and the global fourth moment.  For fixed `d`, write a transverse
relation uniquely as

\[
 d=(u-v)+J(x-y).
\]

Every two of the four roles `(u,v,x,y)` determine the other two.  The six
two-role projections are therefore simple bipartite graphs on two copies of
`A`, all with exactly `r(d)` edges.  If any one of these graphs has
`k^(2+o(1))` four-cycles, the usual wedge/codegree Cauchy calculation gives

\[
 r(d)\le k^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

If this holds for every row, then `T<=k^(7/2+o(1))`.  In the wide regime,
where Elekes controls parallel block intersections, random thinning gives
`|A+J(A-A)|>=k^(5/2-o(1))`; for the square grid this is the conditional upper
exponent `2/5`.  It is not the full cube-root theorem, and the fixed-row
four-cycle estimate is unproved.

The gate survives the two targeted square-root-heavy families sharply.  For
the heavy row `(0,-1)` at `k=120`, the relation count is 948 and the six
projection `C_4` counts are

\[
 1869,1922,1923,2008,2063,2071,
\]

with maximum pair-codegree at most eight.  For the strict diameter row at
`k=90`, the relation count is 266 and the six counts are

\[
 473,243,262,312,447,230,
\]

with maximum pair-codegree at most nine.  Almost all projection cycles are
genuinely different: at `k=120` their union has 11,852 members, 11,850 of
which occur in exactly one projection.

The associated row--source classification also kills a tempting cleanup.
For two rows with common source, their difference has a representation
`delta=g+Jh`.  The cases `g=0` and `h=0` are the coordinate-degenerate
classes `delta in JD` and `delta in D`.  They contain the largest individual
codegrees, but at `k=60` the generic class contributes 19,883,439 of the
29,370,111 four-cycles, or 67.7%.  The generic branch is already dominant.

Read `TRANSVERSE_FIXED_ROW_C4_GATE.md`; the exact tables, pair-linearity, cycle
nonredundancy, and row-difference partition are checked by
`verify_transverse_fixed_row_c4.py`.  The live inverse target is to prove that
`k^(2+epsilon)` fixed-row rectangles force efficient reuse of their auxiliary
edge endpoints and hence a forbidden equality between two non-antipodal edge
norms.  Balanced bicliques built with fresh endpoints have only the `k^2`
cycle scale, so bounded forbidden-pattern arguments remain insufficient.

**Updated restart priority.**  The full theorem is still the global
`k^(4+o(1))` row moment.  The fixed-row `C_4` conjecture is a credible easier
intermediate target with a meaningful conditional `2/5` exponent.  Do not
confuse it with a completed upper bound, and do not drop the generic
row-difference class.

## 19. Variable-longest fixed-row rectangle books

`FIXED_ROW_LONGEST_BOOK_GATE.md` refines the preceding gate.  Charge every
projection four-cycle to its unique longest variable actual edge, excluding
the fixed row.  If `c(g)` is the charge load, then

\[
 \sum_g c(g)^2\le k^{2+o(1)}
\]

implies the desired four-cycle estimate by Cauchy.  The maximum exact moment
over the six projections is 15,126 on the 120-point heavy witness and 5,215
on the 90-point strict-diameter witness.  The worst classes are `C_4` books
with 44 and 34 pages centered on a common relation.  The precise next inverse
problem is to count pairs of book pages sharing a variable-longest edge on
the `A^2` scale.

Do not retry the following shortcuts: the heavy witness contains complete
affine quarter-turn orbits; almost every projection cycle occurs in only one
of the six projections; and its fixed-row Gaussian relation system already
has rank 118 on 120 variables.  Rigidity does not force a norm collision.  A
three-factor parabola construction was also negative: the first 1,000
integer-parabola points have all 499,500 distances distinct, and quartic
height defeats the direct sieve scaling.  Run
`verify_fixed_row_longest_book_gate.py` for the exact checkpoint.
